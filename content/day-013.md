## Explanation

2.0121 said an object cannot be thought apart from the possibility of its
connections — no bare thing sits behind the connecting, waiting to be
discovered fitting or not fitting. 2.0122 names what that possibility of
connection actually is, and draws the consequence straight through: the
object's own independence turns out to be built from it.

Read the sentence as one move told in two halves, not a change of mind
partway through. First half: the thing is independent, in that it can
occur in *all* possible circumstances — nothing about which state of
affairs happens to hold decides in advance which combinations the thing
could enter. Carry it into any atomic fact you like; it stays itself.
That is real independence, and nothing that follows takes it back.
Second half: this very form of independence — occurring in every possible
circumstance — just *is* a form of connexion with the atomic fact: a
form of dependence. Not independent *despite* being connectable everywhere,
but independent *by consisting in* being connectable everywhere.
Wittgenstein is not correcting the first half with the second. He is
identifying them.

The parenthetical earns its place in the explanation itself today, rather
than being flagged forward the way yesterday's was — it is the same
thought in miniature, not a second one. A word can sit alone, listed in a
dictionary, or set inside a sentence, but not, Wittgenstein says, in two
different ways. Its meaning is not something complete already in the
isolated word, which a sentence later happens to switch on: the word's
standing on its own already carries every sentence it could occur in,
prejudged — 2.012's word, doing the same work here — the same way an
object's standing on its own already carries every atomic fact it could
occur in. A dictionary entry is not the word with its uses stripped away
down to some residue; it is a compressed statement of all of them.

This is what makes 2.0122 hard rather than merely surprising. It asks the
reader to stop sorting "independent" and "dependent" into two bins a
thing must fall into one of, and see instead one relation described from
two directions: what a thing owes to no single fact (its independence) is
not different from what it owes to the whole space of facts it could
enter (its dependence). 2.0123 takes the next step — what knowing an
object like this, completely, actually amounts to.

## Zen

The Huayan school's image of Indra's net makes the same identification
2.0122 makes, in a different key. At every knot of the cosmic net hangs a
jewel, and each jewel reflects every other jewel in the net — including
all their reflections of it, recursively, without end. Ask what a single
jewel is apart from the reflecting, and there is no answer: its
particular brilliance, the very thing that makes it *this* jewel and not
another, is nothing but the totality of what it reflects and is reflected
in. Independence and interconnection are not held in tension here; they
are one fact under two descriptions, exactly 2.0122's move — the jewel's
standing on its own *is* its connexion to the whole net.

The disanalogy is where the traditions actually part, and it is worth
holding onto rather than smoothing over. Huayan's net has no jewel
underneath the reflecting — take the relations away and nothing is left,
not even a bare particular; it is relation all the way down. 2.0122
denies exactly that reading of itself: the object is not dissolved into
its connexions, it is a fixed, simple thing (the Tractatus will say so
explicitly a few theses on) whose independence merely *takes the form of*
connectability, without ceasing to be a genuine bearer of it. Huayan
has no bearer left once the reflecting is described in full; Wittgenstein
never lets go of one. Same identification, different metaphysics
underneath it.

## Method

Today's parenthetical is not the case day 12's tripwire was written for,
and it is worth saying precisely why rather than letting the two look
alike. Yesterday's parenthetical ("a logical entity cannot be merely
possible") stated a second, distinct claim about logic itself, which is
why it was named and flagged forward rather than folded in. Today's
parenthetical states the *same* claim 2.0122's main clause makes, about a
different kind of thing (words, not objects) — an illustration, not an
addition. Folding it into the explanation rather than flagging it forward
is the right call under the existing rule ("show, don't say," and one
thought per day), and the tripwire itself does not fire: 2.0122's aside
does not turn out to carry a further point the main sentence didn't
already carry. No METHOD.md change needed; the distinction between
*exemplifying* today's thought and *bundling in* a second one was already
available in the existing rules, just not previously named this
explicitly.

On the Zen note: reaching for Buddhist material again is worth flagging
against day 10's tripwire (four of five Zen notes leaning Buddhist,
before days 11–12 deliberately diversified into Stoicism and Daoism).
Checked non-Buddhist candidates first — Confucian relational personhood
(a person constituted by, not prior to, their web of roles) was the most
promising, and was set aside because the domain mismatch is real: it
maps logical objects onto social selves, which strains the resemblance
past what "show, don't say" should tolerate. Huayan's net won on fit, and
it is a different doctrine from every Buddhist note used so far —
dependent origination (days 2, 7), Abhidharma's two-tier realism (day 8),
Madhyamaka's denial of *svabhāva* (day 10), the koan's refusal of the
question's frame (day 4) — not a repeat under a new name. The tension
with day 10's tripwire is real and is being named rather than argued
away: this is a third Buddhist-family return in four days once Stoicism
and Daoism are set beside it, and if the next occasion for a Zen note
also pulls toward Buddhist material without an equally specific,
previously-unused doctrine to justify it, that is the day to treat the
family itself, not just the individual doctrine, as the rut.

No pressure on the interpretive-war rule today, for the same reason days
