## Explanation

2.023 closed a question — the world's shared form consists of the objects
— and opened one it pointedly declined to answer: fixed *in what
respect*? "Die Substanz der Welt kann nur eine Form und keine materiellen
Eigenschaften bestimmen." The substance of the world can only determine a
form, and no material properties. That is the answer, stated as a bare
limit: whatever objects fix by being the world's substance, it stops
short of colour, weight, hardness, or any of the qualities that make a
thing seem to have a *character* rather than merely a place in a
structure.

The second sentence gives the reason, and the reason is what does the
real work. "Denn diese werden erst durch die Sätze dargestellt — erst
durch die Konfiguration der Gegenstände gebildet." For these are first
presented by the propositions — first formed through the configuration
of objects. Material properties are not a second stock of facts sitting
alongside form, waiting to be fixed the same way form is fixed. They do
not belong to objects at all, taken one at a time. They show up only
once objects are combined — configured — into a state of affairs, and
a proposition describes that configuration. Ask what colour a bare
object has, apart from any fact it occurs in, and 2.0231 says the
question has no floor to land on: colour is not a property waiting to
be read off the object, it is something that only exists at the level
where objects have already been arranged, and arrangement is exactly
what a proposition states.

This is also the first place *dargestellt* — presented, depicted,
represented — does real work in this project, one thesis before *Bild*
was named at 2.0212 and several theses before picturing becomes the
book's main business at 2.1. Nothing here yet claims the full picture
theory; that a proposition can *show* a form it cannot *state* is an
argument for chapter 4, flagged once at day 20 as a fight still ahead.
2.0231 only uses "presented" in passing, to say where material
properties come from. But it is worth noticing, on the day the word
first appears in this sense, that it already leans in that later
direction: properties are not read off substance directly, they arrive
by way of what a proposition does.

## Zen

Locke's distinction between primary and secondary qualities (*Essay
Concerning Human Understanding*, II.viii) draws a structurally similar
line inside the object. Primary qualities — solidity, extension,
figure, motion — really inhere in a body and belong to it whether or
not anyone perceives it. Secondary qualities — colour, taste, sound,
smell — are, on Locke's account, nothing in the object itself: only a
power the object's insensible parts have to produce a sensation in a
perceiver. Colour is Locke's standing example of a quality that seems
to belong to the thing and does not. 2.0231 draws its own line in
almost the same place and names almost the same example class
(*materielle Eigenschaften* — colour chief among them, as the very next
thesis says outright) as not belonging to the object.

The disanalogy is where each line is drawn and what it is drawn
against. Locke keeps a residue: primary qualities are still monadic,
still properties the object has in itself, unmediated by anything.
Only the second class gets relocated, and it is relocated to a
*perceiver* — secondary qualities are powers to affect a mind. 2.0231
is more austere on both counts. It grants substance no material
property at all, not even the geometrical ones Locke calls primary;
objects have only form, a purely combinatorial fact about what they can
occur alongside. And what relocates material properties is not a
perceiving subject but a proposition describing a configuration —
nothing in 2.0231's picture needs anyone to look. Locke moves colour
from the object to the eye; Wittgenstein moves it from the object to
the sentence, and asks no eye to be present at all.

## Method

Interpretive-war note included today, and checked as a fresh occasion
rather than a repeat of 2.021's (day 23). That divergence was about
whether the substance-regress is a demonstration or a performed rung;
this one is different in kind, because 2.0231 is the first thesis in
this stretch to use representation-vocabulary (*dargestellt*) to say
where a property comes from, one step short of the showing/saying
argument day 20 flagged at 4.121–4.1212 as the place these two readings
split hardest. Traditional readers can take 2.0231 as a straightforward
positive claim about what propositions do — they present configurations,
including material properties, and that is simply true of them.
Resolute readers have grounds to read it more warily: a thesis that
says what propositions "present," stated in the book's own voice, sits
uncomfortably close to claiming to *say* something the book will later
insist can only be *shown* by a proposition's structure, not asserted
about it from outside. Stated once, briefly, no verdict — this is
flagged as the nearer approach to the day 20 fight, not the fight
itself, which is still chapters away.

Buddhist, 24 none, 25 Kant, 26 Leibniz, 27 Democritus) before choosing
Locke — fresh ground, confirmed by grep that neither Locke nor

No app or presentation change. 2.0231 introduces no new technical term
the existing prose-gloss approach can't carry, and a two-sentence
assertion-plus-reason fits the single-thesis layout without strain. No
destruction argument owed.
