## Explanation

2.023 settled an identity: the world's fixed form just is the objects.
2.0231 draws out what that identity costs. "Die Substanz der Welt *kann*
nur eine Form und keine materiellen Eigenschaften bestimmen." The
substance of the world *can* only determine a form and not any material
properties. The italicized *kann* is not reporting what substance
happens to leave out, the way a guest list happens to omit some names.
It states a limit on what substance is even capable of fixing — form,
and nothing else, not as a matter of fact but as a matter of what
"substance" means here. Ask substance for a colour, a weight, a texture,
and the question is already misdirected; those were never on offer.

The second sentence says why: material properties "werden erst durch
die Sätze dargestellt — erst durch die Konfiguration der Gegenstände
gebildet." They are first presented by the propositions — first formed
by the configuration of the objects. *Erst* twice, doing the same
job twice: not until, not before. A material property isn't a fact
about a bare object, waiting to be discovered once you look closely
enough at it in isolation; it is *made*, and only made, once objects
are actually combined into a state of affairs and stated in a
proposition. This is the exact word Wittgenstein's own text gives, for
the first time, to what 2.023 had only pointed at: *Konfiguration*,
configuration — objects arranged, not objects alone.

This sharpens rather than repeats 2.023. Form is what an object brings
to any combination *before* combination — its fixed range of possible
partners, coined at 2.0141. Material properties are what shows up
*after* — a feature of the arrangement, not of any object taken by
itself. Substance secures the first only. The second is posterior,
built, downstream of fact rather than sitting in wait inside any single
object. What looked, on a first pass, like two different kinds of thing
an object might have — a formal side and a material side — turns out to
be a formal side that belongs to the object and a material side that
belongs to nothing until objects are put together. The next sentence
takes this to its blunt conclusion: objects themselves, roughly
speaking, are colourless.

## Zen

Ontic structural realism, the position E. J. Lowe's critics and later
James Ladyman and Don Ross defend most fully (*Every Thing Must Go*,
2007), makes a structurally close claim about physical objects:
strip away relations and structure, and there is nothing further —
no hidden qualitative residue — left over in the object to describe.
What a particle *is*, on this view, is exhausted by its place in the
relational structure physics describes; intrinsic, structure-independent
qualities are not hidden facts about it, they are not facts at all.
2.0231 draws the same line between what an object contributes on its
own (form, a range of possible combination) and what only exists once
combination happens (material properties) — substance gives structure,
never qualitative filling.

The disanalogy marks exactly where 2.0231 stops short. Radical OSR is
often pushed all the way to eliminativism about objects themselves:
if there is nothing but structure, "object" may name no separate
thing at all, only a node the structure is described relative to.
Wittgenstein's objects do not dissolve this way. They remain
substantial individuals — simple, subsisting, numerically distinct —
even once stripped of every material property; 2.0231 empties the
object of qualitative content without emptying it of thinghood. Ladyman
and Ross ask whether the relata survive the relations; this thesis
never asks the question, because for Wittgenstein the object was never
a candidate for elimination — only for having, on close inspection, far
less packed into it than a first look suggested.
