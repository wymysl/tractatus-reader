## Explanation

"Der Gegenstand ist das Feste, Bestehende; die Konfiguration ist das
Wechselnde, Unbeständige." The object is the fixed, the existent; the
configuration is the changing, the variable.

This sentence has been owed for a long time. Day 2 named the pairs still
to come — "object, state of affairs, configuration" — before any of them
had a technical sense. Day 8 sharpened that into a triad of active
pairs: fact/thing, atomic fact/existence, object/configuration. The
first two were settled early (fact is atomic facts obtaining, by day 8
itself); the third has taken the rest of chapter 2's opening run to
arrive, because "configuration" itself had to wait for 2.023's identity
and 2.0231's first use of *Konfiguration* by name (day 28) before there
was a second term to oppose the first with. 2.0271 is where the
opposition finally gets stated outright, and it does it by reusing
yesterday's sentence rather than adding a new one: "the fixed, the
existent" is 2.027's own identity, verbatim, now given a partner it
never had a partner for before.

The partner is exact, not loose. Object is fixed; configuration is what
changes. But nothing changes *in* an object to produce this — 2.027
already ruled that out, since object, fixed, and existent name one and
the same thing with no room for one of the three to shift while the
others hold still. What varies is which arrangement of objects is
actual: the same fixed stock, combined differently. This is the payoff
2.0141's *Form* was building toward from the start — form is an object's
range of possible partners, fixed in advance; configuration is which
partner, of that fixed range, actually obtains. Objects do not become a
new configuration. They *are* configured, over and over, without any of
them changing in the process — the way the same twenty-six letters don't
change when they spell a different word.

That is also why "das Wechselnde, Unbeständige" needs both words, the
same two-word care 2.027 used for the object's own side. "Changing" says
configurations differ from one another; "variable," closer to
unbeständig's literal *not standing fast*, says no single configuration
is owed permanence the way an object is. A configuration can fail to
obtain, or stop obtaining, without anything fixed being touched — which
is exactly the room chapter 2 needs for the world itself to be
contingent (some atomic facts obtain, others don't, 2.06 still ahead)
while the objects underneath stay put. Fixed and changing are not two
grades of the same thing, one sturdier than the other. They are two
different jobs, and 2.0271 is the sentence that finally hands them out
by name.

## Zen

Plato's theory of Forms draws a structurally similar line. The Forms —
the intelligible, the unchanging, what a thing of a given kind ultimately
*is* — stand opposed to the sensible world of becoming, where nothing
holds still long enough to be known with certainty (*Republic* V,
477a–479e; *Timaeus* 27d–28a: what always is and never becomes, against
what always becomes and never is). Particular things in this world are
many, various, and in flux; they count as beautiful, or just, or equal,
only by resembling or participating in a Form that itself never varies.
Fixed on one side, changing on the other — the same division 2.0271
draws between object and configuration, and Plato's is the older claim
by well over two thousand years.

It is also, on inspection, a different relation entirely, and the
difference is where the note earns its keep. Plato's changing particulars
are not built *out of* Forms the way a configuration is built out of
objects; they stand in a separate relation to them — participation,
imitation — across a real ontological gap, one realm of being and
another of becoming, with the sensible world consequently the lesser,
more shadowy of the two (the Divided Line, *Republic* VI, 509d–511e;
the cave, VII). 2.0271 has no such gap and no such demotion. A
configuration is not a copy or a participant standing at one remove from
the objects it configures; it is nothing but those very objects,
combined. Nothing here is less real for being on the changing side —
2.0272 and 2.03, next, will treat configurations of objects as what
atomic facts themselves are made of, full members of the world's
furniture, not shadows cast by it. Plato needs two levels of being to
get his fixed and his changing to come apart. 2.0271 gets the same
division out of one level, doing double duty.

## Method

The oldest open thread this project has been carrying — day 2's promise
of a coming object/configuration pair, sharpened by day 8 into a named
triad alongside fact/thing and atomic fact/existence — closes today,
twenty-nine days after day 8 stated it and thirty-five after day 2 first
named it. Worth being precise about what "closes" means here: the triad
was a prediction about vocabulary, not a glossary tripwire in its own
right, and the operative glossary check for the *object* side of it was
day 9's separate, more specific one (simple, substance, fixed/existent),
which resolved yesterday. Checked today's explanation against the same
question day 9's tripwire asked — does the prose have to double back and
re-explain a term it already defined — and found no: *configuration* got
its full gloss at 2.0231 (day 28) and *form* at 2.0141, both linked here
by a one-clause reminder rather than a re-derivation. If anything the
case for a glossary is weaker today than it was for 2.027, since both
halves of today's pairing were already load-bearing, defined vocabulary
before this sentence used them side by side. No new apparatus. This is
the day-2/day-8 thread's actual resolution, on the merits, not a
reopening of day 9's already-settled question under a different name.

Checked METHOD's interpretive-war rule against the located divergence at
2.021 (substance as metaphysical fact vs. rung to 6.54) rather than
assuming by default that a newly formalized pairing reopens it. It
doesn't add a distinct disagreement: the traditional reading takes
2.0271 as fixing Wittgenstein's ontology proper, a two-category
furniture list (fixed particulars, variable arrangements) in the lineage
of Russell's own logical atomism; the resolute reading takes the same
sentence as fixing logical grammar — what "object-talk" and
"configuration-talk" are permitted to mean within the symbolism, not a
claim about what is really out there. That is the identical question
2.021 already located, applied to a second pair of terms rather than a
new one. Seventh consecutive skip on this locus (2.0211, 2.0212, 2.024,
2.025, 2.026, 2.027, now 2.0271) — flagged again, as every day in the
streak has, that the count tracks sentences resting on the 2.021
divergence, not the rule going quiet for good.

Zen-source diversity checked against the last five days (33: Aristotle,
34: Kant, 35: Parmenides, 36: Aquinas): clear, and Plato is a first use
for this project despite how central Forms are to the Western half of
this material — no prior day reached for him, including days (23, 28)
that treated fixed/changing or form/matter questions directly. Chosen
over a second look at Aristotelian hylomorphism (used day 33, inside the
window and the more obvious repeat) and over Heraclitus, considered and
set aside because Heraclitean flux has no compensating fixed term of its
own doing the same work object does here — Logos regulates the change
rather than standing beside it as a second, static kind of thing, which
would have blurred the very opposition 2.0271 states, rather than
mirroring it.

No app or presentation change. 2.0271 states its pairing entirely in
already-glossed vocabulary; the single-thesis layout has carried every
term-opposition this project has produced (fact/thing at day 2,
form/material property at day 28) without strain, and today's is no
different in kind, only in being the pairing those two were building
toward. No destruction argument owed — nothing removed or restructured.
