## Explanation

"Die Sachverhalte sind von einander unabhängig." Atomic facts are
independent of one another.

Five sentences (2.04–2.06) just spent themselves building "atomic fact"
into a totality: the world is however many of them exist (2.04), that
same totality fixes which ones don't (2.05), and the pairing of the two
sides has a name, reality (2.06). All five stayed at the level of the
whole — how existence and non-existence, taken together, add up. 2.061
drops back down to the atomic facts themselves and says something none
of the totality-talk needed: they don't depend on each other. Whether
one obtains has, by itself, no bearing on whether any other does.

"Independent" has already done two different jobs in this book, and it
is worth keeping 2.061 apart from both so as not to blur three separate
claims into one word. 2.024 called *substance* — the objects — what
exists independently of what is the case: objects are there whichever
way the facts fall, prior to any particular configuration. 2.0122 used
the word on a single thing's relation to *all possible* atomic facts: a
thing counts as independent because it can occur in any of them, but
that very capacity is, in the same sentence, called "a form of
connexion" — independence and dependence stated as two descriptions of
the same fact about one object. 2.061's independence is neither of
these. Its subject is not an object and not a single thing's relation
to possibility in general; it is one *actual* atomic fact standing next
to another *actual* one, and the claim is that neither one's obtaining
constrains the other's. Three sentences, three different things the
word "independent" is doing — substance from configuration, one thing
from all its possible combinations, one obtaining fact from another
obtaining fact.

Nothing here is a claim about causes. The Tractatus is not asserting
that atomic facts have no physical influence on each other — that
question belongs to natural science and this sentence is not about
natural science. The independence is logical: from the bare fact that
one atomic fact holds, no *inference* to any other atomic fact's holding
or failing to hold is licensed. That is exactly what 2.062 states next,
in so many words — today's sentence supplies the reason, tomorrow's
states the consequence. It also reaches forward past what belongs to
this thesis: the same independence, transposed from atomic facts to the
elementary propositions that will eventually picture them, is what
makes truth-functional logic possible at all — a way of combining
propositions that needs each one to be assessable on its own, not
entailing or excluding its neighbors. That machinery is many chapters
away and does not belong in today's explanation; 2.061 is its first,
narrowest statement, about the world's furniture rather than about
propositions.

Where the traditional and resolute readings part here is not new
ground so much as the sharpest version yet of the fork day 9 opened at
2.01. The traditional reading takes 2.061 as a further, freestanding
fact about how the world's ultimate constituents are arranged: real
atomic facts, genuinely disconnected from one another, and independence
is one more truth logical atomism has uncovered about them. The
resolute reading takes the same sentence as a rule about how "atomic
fact" is to be used from here on — the independence is analytic to the
notion the book has been constructing since thesis 2, not a discovery
about a world encountered ready-made. Both readings render 2.061 the
same six words; nothing this early decides between them.

## Zen

Buddhist dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) holds that nothing
whatsoever exists independently: every phenomenon arises only in
dependence on conditions, and Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*
extends the claim past causal succession to relation as such — even
paired concepts like long and short have no nature of their own apart
from each other. No item, anywhere, stands free of the web. 2.061 is
close to the exact photographic negative of that claim, applied to the
smallest items each system builds its world from: where Madhyamaka
denies unconditioned independence to everything without exception,
Wittgenstein's atomic facts are independent of one another by
definition, as the very first thing said about them once "atomic fact"
is fully assembled.

The disanalogy runs deeper than a shared word pointing opposite ways.
Each system's whole edifice depends on which answer it gives. Madhyamaka's
universal interdependence is not a side note; it is the argument for
emptiness (*śūnyatā*) itself — nothing has *svabhava*, own-nature, precisely
because nothing is independent of the conditions that make it what it is.
2.04–2.06 needed independence at exactly the same load-bearing spot for the
opposite reason: the totality of atomic facts could only "determine what
does not exist" the guest-list way 2.05 described, cleanly and without
remainder, if the guests do not affect each other's attendance. A
dependently-originated Sachverhalt would make the totality's arithmetic
answer to more than which items are checked off; some could tip others
whose absence 2.05 needs to fall out for free. Both traditions notice
that a "totality of items" claim needs an answer to the independence
question before it can do its work. They give opposite answers, and the
work each is trying to do explains why: emptiness needs interdependence
to dissolve the world into relations; the picture theory, still six
chapters off, needs independence to let logic combine elementary
propositions without one silently deciding another.

This closes a debt day 35 flagged explicitly: dependent origination was
recorded there as "the stronger candidate for a day whose thesis argues
*against* a fixed floor" — 2.026, that day's thesis, needed a fixed
floor, and dependent origination could only negate that, not parallel
it. 2.061 needs the opposite floor, independence rather than fixity, and
now the negation is the whole point rather than a mismatch to route
around.

## Method

Checked the glossary tripwire against "independent" specifically,
since the word now carries three distinct senses across 2.024, 2.0122,
and today. Declined a glossary entry: the explanation above does the
disambiguating work in place, each sense is tied to a different
grammatical subject (substance, a single thing, an atomic fact), and a
glossary trying to hold three senses of one word apart would need
nearly the same prose as the explanation already has. Revisit only if a
fourth sense appears.

Flagged, without writing it yet, a self-critique 2.061 owes a much
later day: Wittgenstein's own 1929 paper "Some Remarks on Logical
Form" retracts exactly this independence for elementary propositions
like colour statements — "this is red" and "this is green" ascribed to
the same point turned out not to be logically independent after all,
contrary to what 5.134 will claim by extending 2.061's premise upward.
That is the book's own author finding a load-bearing crack in the
independence thesis, not an external objection, and it deserves its own
honest treatment when the book reaches 4.211 or 5.134, where the claim
actually gets used — not here, where atomic facts, not colour
propositions, are the subject, and no example has yet been supplied for
the crack to bite on. Recorded here so it is not forgotten by then.
