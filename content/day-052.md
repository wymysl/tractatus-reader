## Explanation

"Das Bild ist ein Modell der Wirklichkeit." Four words after the copula,
and the last one is new: *Modell*, model, has not appeared in the book
before now. Everything else on the sentence is already on the table —
*Bild*, picture (2.1), *Wirklichkeit*, reality, defined at 2.06 as "das
Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten," the existence and
non-existence of atomic facts, the same clause 2.11 gave the picture
yesterday as what it presents. What 2.12 adds is the relation between
picture and reality: not resemblance, not description in the ordinary
sense of telling-about. A model.

A model, in the sense the next several sentences make precise — 2.13's
elements correspond to objects, 2.14's elements are combined in a
definite way, 2.15's combination of elements *represents* a combination
of things, 2.1512's picture as "a scale applied to reality" — is
something built the same way as what it models, not something that looks
like it. Wittgenstein's own example, reported by his biographers from
things he told friends years later, is exactly this kind of model: a
Paris courtroom, during the war, using miniature cars and dolls to
reconstruct a traffic accident. The toy cars are not paintings of the
real ones; nothing about their being small and made of tin resembles the
real cars in the relevant sense. What carries the reconstruction is that
the toys stand in the same spatial arrangement the real cars stood in,
so that moving a toy tracks a question about where a real car was. A
newspaper report of the trial is where, on this account, Wittgenstein
found the thought that becomes 2.12.

One thing 2.12 can look like it does, and does not: undo yesterday's
restriction. 2.11 said a picture presents a *Sachlage* — one determinate
configuration, not reality entire. 2.12 says the picture models
*Wirklichkeit*, reality, no qualifier — a bigger claim, on a careless
reading. It is not one. 2.06 defines *Wirklichkeit* as "das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten," full stop; only 2.063, two sentences
later, adds *die gesamte* — "the total reality is the world" — to reach
everything at once. A picture that models one determinate slice of the
existence-and-non-existence structure is already modeling *Wirklichkeit*
in 2.06's sense of the word; it need not model the total to qualify.
2.11 and 2.12 name the same relation from two sides: 2.11 says what a
picture presents (a *Sachlage*), 2.12 says what kind of relation
presenting is (modeling).

Nothing here yet says how a model's elements come to stand for objects,
or what sharing a structure requires. That is 2.13 through 2.17's work,
one clause at a time; 2.12 only names the relation they will spend the
rest of the chapter making precise.

## Elsewhere

The classical four-line formula Chan Buddhism later used to describe its
own transmission — a special transmission outside the scriptures, not
established on words and letters, directly pointing to the human mind,
seeing one's own nature and becoming Buddha (教外別傳，不立文字，直指人心，見性成佛)
— names one thing no model of 2.12's kind can do: hand over seeing
itself. A finger pointing at the moon, a raft used to cross a river, a
picture standing model for a *Sachlage* — all remain instruments,
correlated with what they are of, never identical with it; Chan's claim
is not that words and pictures describe badly and could in principle
describe better, but that direct seeing is not a species of describing
at all, however exact the shared structure.

The disanalogy is where the two projects actually part, and it is worth
stating precisely rather than left as mood. 2.12 does not treat the gap
between model and modeled as a defect awaiting correction — the whole
remaining apparatus of chapter 2 (2.13's correspondence, 2.16's and
2.17's shared form) is built to say exactly how far a model built the
same way as reality can reach, and 2.1511 will say plainly that it does
reach: "the picture is linked with reality; it touches it." Chan's
formula refuses that ambition at the point it starts, for the one matter
it cares about: not "our models are still too crude," but "this, seeing
one's own nature, was never a describing problem." Where 2.12 opens a
research programme in how models reach reality, Chan's line closes one
before it begins — a genuine disagreement about what modeling can be
for, not a shared metaphor wearing two vocabularies.

## Method

2.12 forced a check on 2.11's own claim rather than presenting a new
scope question of its own: does "the picture models *Wirklichkeit*, no
qualifier" contradict "the picture presents a *Sachlage*, not reality as
a whole"? It does not, but only because 2.06 and 2.063 use *Wirklichkeit*
and *die gesamte Wirklichkeit* as two different sizes of the same word,
and nothing forces a reader moving day by day, months apart in this
project's publication schedule, to hold both definitions in view at
once. Re-read 2.06 and 2.063 side by side before writing today's third
paragraph specifically to check this rather than assert it from memory
of the earlier explanation; the reading holds, but it depended on going
back to the primary text rather than trusting yesterday's summary of it,
worth naming since METHOD.md's rule 4 promises the reader precision, and
today the precision needed a source check the explanation itself doesn't
show its work on.

The courtroom anecdote is biographical, not textual — nowhere in the
Tractatus itself, sourced to what Wittgenstein told others later, not to
2.12's own words — and is flagged in the text as exactly that ("reported
by his biographers," "on this account") rather than presented as part of
what 2.12 says. Included because "model" is the word this anecdote is
always attached to, and because it does concrete work the paragraph
needs: showing what "same structure, not resemblance" means for an
ordinary reader before the book itself starts formalizing it at 2.13.
One paragraph, not developed further, per rule 2's "explain less, place
more" — the anecdote earns exactly the space needed to fix the sense of
"model" and no more.

No interpretive-war paragraph today. The traditional and resolute
readings agree on what 2.12 says and on its role in the chapter; nothing
about "model" itself is contested ground between them the way 2.1's
status as the start of a real semantic theory was contested. Silence per
rule 5, same discipline day 51 and day 50's own precedent set: invoke the
war only where it actually forks.

No METHOD.md change. 2.12's demands — checking a scope question against
the primary text, sourcing a biographical anecdote honestly, holding the
Elsewhere note to a stated disanalogy rather than an asserted likeness —
are all already licensed by existing rules; nothing here needed a new
one.
