## Explanation

Since 2.021 the text has been using "substance" without ever quite
defining it — asserting that objects form it, arguing what would fail
without it, tracing its fixed form down to the objects themselves,
distinguishing what it can and cannot fix. 2.024 finally states the
definition all of that was assuming: *substance is what exists
independently of what is the case.*

That last phrase is not a loose echo. "What is the case" (*was der Fall
ist*) is thesis 1's own wording, verbatim: "The world is everything that
is the case." Read side by side, the two sentences carve the world in
two. The world just *is* what is the case — the totality of the
obtaining atomic facts, which could have obtained otherwise (1.21 said
so directly: any one of them can be the case or not, and the rest stay
as they are). Substance is whatever does not move when that totality
does. Change which atomic facts obtain — subtract one, add another —
and the world is a different world. Substance, on this definition,
is untouched: the same stock of objects, available to combine into
whichever facts turn out to obtain instead.

This is why 2.0211 could argue the way it did two days ago: if there
were no such fixed stock, a proposition's very sense would depend on
some other proposition's turning out true, since there would be nothing
holding still underneath the checking. 2.0211 argued from the
consequence backward — here is what collapses without substance. 2.024
gives the thing itself, stated positively, independent of any
particular argument for needing it: not *the floor that keeps sense
from collapsing*, but simply *what does not depend on which facts are
the facts*.

Put the run together and a division of labor comes into view. Chapter
1's business was the world — everything that happens to be the case, all
of it contingent, none of it guaranteed. 2.021 through 2.024 have been
locating what chapter 1 left out: whatever the world's contingency has
to rest on in order to be *this* particular world rather than some other
one. Substance is not a second, sturdier world sitting behind the first.
It is what stays fixed while worlds — this one and every other the
objects could combine into — vary above it.

## Zen

Spinoza's *Ethics* opens with the same word made to do the same kind of
work: "By substance I understand what is in itself and is conceived
through itself" (Part I, Definition 3) — that whose concept requires no
other thing's concept to be formed. Both writers reach for "substance"
to name something that does not lean on anything outside itself for
what it is.

The disanalogy is exact, and it runs on two axes at once. First,
number: Spinoza's substance is necessarily one — Part I, Proposition 14
rules out a second substance sharing an attribute with the first, and by
the end there is exactly one, God or Nature, of which everything else is
a mode. 2.024's substance is irreducibly many: a stock of distinct
objects, never named in the singular anywhere in this run of theses.
Second, and more precisely: what each is independent *of*. Spinoza's
substance needs nothing else, full stop — not for its being, not even
for being conceived. Wittgenstein's objects are independent along
exactly one axis, which fact obtains, and dependent along another: 2.0122
already ruled that an object only occurs at all through its possibility
of combining with others in a fact — it has no standing, and no
occurrence, apart from that possibility. Spinoza's substance is
self-sufficient in the fullest sense the word can carry. 2.024's is
independent of the world's contingency and nothing more.
