## Explanation

2.024 defined substance: what exists independently of what is the case.
2.025 specifies what that independent thing consists of: "Sie ist Form
und Inhalt" — it is form and content.

Read against 2.0231, and the sentence looks like it takes back what
that thesis just gave. Two days ago: "the substance of the world *can
only* determine a form and not any material properties" (*materielle
Eigenschaften*) — those are first formed by the configuration of
objects, not carried by substance itself. If content just meant
material properties — colour, weight, texture — 2.025 would flatly
contradict 2.0231's *kann nur*. It doesn't, because *Inhalt* is not the
word 2.0231 used and does not mean the same thing.

Look instead at what "content" could add to "form" without smuggling
qualities back in. 2.0131 gave the shape already, in a different image:
"a point in space is a place for an argument" (*eine Argumentstelle*) —
a slot, borrowed from Frege's logic of functions, prepared before
anything fills it. Form, since 2.0141 and confirmed by 2.023's identity
with the objects themselves, is exactly this: the shape of the slot,
the range of what could go where. What 2.025 adds is that a slot is not
the same as an empty slot. Substance is not a bare scaffold of
combinatorial possibilities waiting on something else to occupy it — it
is *these* objects, actually there, filling every place their own form
allows. Form is the space of possibility; content is that the space is
never, in fact, empty. Nothing here reaches for colour, weight, or any
of the qualities 2.0231 reserved for configuration alone — content
names only that the possibilities substance opens are possibilities *of
something*, not possibilities as such.

That reading keeps 2.025 continuous with 2.023 rather than in tension
with it. 2.023 already refused to treat form as one thing and the
objects as another — "this fixed form consists of the objects," stated
as an identity, not a relation between two separate items. 2.025 draws
the same identity out along a second axis: the objects are, at once,
what makes the form (their combinatorial possibilities) and what makes
the content (that there is something there to combine). Two
descriptions of one substance, not two ingredients mixed into it — the
same move 2.023 made for form and the objects, now stated for form and
content together.

## Zen

Day 20 checked Aristotelian hylomorphism against 2.0141's bare *Form*
and rejected it: Aristotle's *morphē* is an intrinsic, essence-making
principle, Wittgenstein's *Form* there a purely relational,
combinatorial one, sharing only a translator's word. 2.025 reopens the
question on harder ground, because it now pairs *Form* with something
namable as content — the very words hylomorphism itself pairs, matter
(*hylē*) and form. Worth checking again rather than assuming the
earlier verdict still covers a different sentence.

It still doesn't hold, and for a reason sharper than day 20's. For
Aristotle, matter and form are two really distinct principles standing
in a relation: bronze is indeterminate stuff, a potentiality that could
become a statue, a bowl, a coin, and form is what actualizes one of
those possibilities, making the bronze *this* determinate thing rather
than remaining raw material short of any of them (*Physics* I.7,
*Metaphysics* Z.3). The matter is really there before, and really
independent of, whichever form comes to actualize it. 2.025 has no such
split. Form and content are not two components combining, one
supplying shape and the other supplying stuff — they are the same
objects, described twice: their combinatorial possibilities counted as
form, their sheer being-there counted as content. There is no
substance-as-bronze waiting, indeterminately, for a form to arrive and
make it something. The objects were already fully determinate,
individually distinct, the moment 2.02 called them simple.

So the coincidence of vocabulary runs deeper this time — form paired
explicitly with something namable as content — and the disanalogy runs
correspondingly deeper too: not just "a different kind of form," as day
20 found, but no potentiality/actuality structure at all, where
Aristotle's whole doctrine depends on one. A closer surface match
making a wider real distance visible, rather than closing it.

## Method

Aristotelian hylomorphism now that 2.025 pairs *Form* explicitly with
*Inhalt* — the closer vocabulary match hylomorphism was rejected for
lacking at 2.0141. Re-checked against the doubt test with the sharper
pairing in hand and the rejection still holds, on sharper ground than
before: no potentiality/actuality structure survives the identity 2.023
already committed this project to (form = the objects, not a separate
principle applied to them). Recording this as a second instance of day
20's general standard — a tempting same-word candidate gets checked and
argued against, not silently skipped, whether or not it ends up used.
