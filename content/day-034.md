## Explanation

Since 2.0141, "form" has been a word this project explains rather than
shows: the range of possible combinations an object brings to any state
of affairs, identified outright with the objects themselves at 2.023,
paired with content at 2.025. Not once has a reader been given an
actual instance — a concrete thing that *counts* as a form. "Raum, Zeit
und Farbe (Färbigkeit) sind Formen der Gegenstände." Space, time and
colour (colouredness) are forms of objects. 2.0251 is where the abstract
word finally gets furniture in it.

Read against 2.0131 (day 18), the furniture is not new — only the name
for it is. That day worked out a picture without yet having "form" to
call it by: a sight-thing comes with a colour-slot, a touch-thing with a
hardness-slot, each object's kind fixing in advance *that* some value in
its range will be filled, never *which*. 2.0251 is the text catching up
to its own picture and naming the slot: colour-space, worked out in
practice three propositions ago, now has a formal name. Space joins it
from the same day (a spatial object needing infinite space); time is new
today, first use of *Zeit* as a technical term in this book, added to the
list without commentary — the same possibility-space structure, one more
instance, no argument given for why it belongs.

The parenthesis is the sentence's real precision, and it is easy to read
past. Not *Farbe* alone — *Farbe (Färbigkeit)*, colour glossed
immediately as colouredness. That second word is not decorative. Three
theses ago, 2.0232 said objects are colourless; 2.0231 said substance can
fix only form, never a material property, and offered colour as the
material property's own example. Read carelessly, 2.0251 looks like it
takes that straight back — colour, suddenly, is a form after all. It
doesn't, because *Farbe* and *Färbigkeit* are not one claim twice.
*Färbigkeit* is the general fact that a sight-thing has some colour or
other — the slot, the space of possible values, exactly as fixed and as
empty as the colour-space day 18 already described. Which colour a thing
turns out to have is a *material* property, first formed only once
objects are combined (2.0231) — the filling, not the slot. Nothing in
2.0251 says an object is any particular colour; nothing in 2.0232 says an
object lacks a colour-slot. The two sentences are not in tension because
they answer different questions about the same word.

That leaves the list itself worth a plain remark: three examples, not a
definition, and almost certainly not exhaustive — 2.0131 already named
pitch and hardness in the same breath as colour and space, and neither
returns here. 2.0251 upgrades three members of that earlier list to
technical status and leaves the rest as they were, form by example
rather than form by rule — of a piece with a book that has, since 2.0141,
never once said what a form *is* in general, only shown where one
occurs.

## Zen

Kant's Transcendental Aesthetic makes the closest possible verbal match:
space and time are, in his own technical sense, pure forms of intuition
— not concepts abstracted from experience and not features of things in
themselves, but the a priori structure the mind's own sensibility
imposes on whatever it receives, so that anything can appear to us
spatially or temporally at all (*Critique of Pure Reason*, A22–30/B37–45).
"Form" naming exactly space and time is not a family resemblance here; it
is the same two words in the same grammatical role, in the philosopher
most responsible for making "form" a technical term of art in the first
place.

The match sharpens the disanalogy rather than closing it, on two fronts
at once. First, location: Kant's forms belong to the *subject* — a
contribution sensibility makes, absent from things as they are in
themselves (noumena are neither spatial nor temporal). 2.0251's forms
belong to the *object* — a fixed feature of substance, what a thing
brings to combination whether or not anything perceives it, continuous
with 2.024's "independent of what is the case." No perceiving subject
has entered this book's architecture anywhere to make the contribution
Kant's forms require.

Second, and more exactly targeted to today's sentence: Kant's own system
would refuse the list 2.0251 gives. Space and time are, for him, the
*only* pure forms of intuition — necessary, a priori, the same for every
possible experience. Colour he places on the opposite side of a line he
insists on: a secondary quality, known only a posteriori, through
sensation, varying between perceivers, never a form at all (*Critique*,
B44; *Prolegomena* §13, Remark II — the same cut Locke drew, used here at
day 29). 2.0251 puts space, time and colour in one list, one grammatical
subject, one predicate. Kant's architecture would split that list down
the middle. The surface word is identical; the taxonomy it organizes is
not.

## Method

Second Kant appearance in the project (day 25, day 34), and the honest
check is whether that repeats a source rather than earning a second one.
It doesn't, on the same standard day 31 set for Leibniz's third
appearance: day 25 used the Transcendental Deduction (the unity of
apperception, judgment's condition of possibility); today uses the
Transcendental Aesthetic (space and time as pure forms of intuition) —
a different, independently famous piece of Kant's architecture, not a
restatement of the earlier one. Clear of the five-day diversity window
regardless (29: Locke, 30: Scotus, 31: Leibniz, 32: Spinoza, 33:
Aristotle), and chosen over any alternative because no other source in
the philosophical canon names space and time "forms" with this exact a
technical precision — the fit earns the repetition rather than merely
surviving it.

The real work today was checking 2.0251 against 2.0231–2.0232 rather
than letting the surface reappearance of "colour" pass as harmless
repetition. Read flat, "colour is a form" and "objects are colourless"
look like a straight contradiction three days apart. They resolve only
by taking the parenthesis — *Farbe (Färbigkeit)* — as doing real
disambiguating work, not stylistic doubling, and by reading it against
apparatus this project built three days before "form" needed it: day
18's colour-slot picture for 2.0131, worked out without the word "form"
in hand at all. Today is where that picture and this word turn out to
have been describing the same thing all along, which is a genuine
retrospective payoff, not an invented one — confirmed by rereading day
18's own text rather than assumed from memory of writing it.

Checked day 9's glossary tripwire (*simple*, *substance*, *unalterable*,
held at two of three since day 23, due at 2.027) and it does not move:
"form" has never been one of the three tracked predicates, and 2.0251
elaborates form's own instances rather than adding a fourth predicate to
*Gegenstand*. One thesis remains before it is due — 2.026 next, then
2.027 itself.

Checked the interpretive-war rule fresh, and it stays silent, but not for
the reason the last four days gave. Days 2.0211 through 2.025 tracked one
specific located divergence (substance as metaphysical fact vs. rung to
6.54) and found it unmoved four times running. 2.0251 is not a fifth
instance of that same check — it doesn't touch substance's status at all,
only its internal furniture — so folding it into that streak would
overstate the count. The relevant precedent is instead the older,
separate thread days 9 through 18 ran on the internal-properties
architecture itself (objects, form, combination) and found no divergence
there either. 2.0251 continues that second thread, not the first;
worth keeping the two apart rather than merging them into one number.

No app or presentation change. 2.0251 introduces one first-use technical
term (*Zeit*, time) handled in a sentence, exactly as *Konfiguration* was
at 2.0231 and *Bild* was at 2.0212, and needs no new apparatus beyond
prose; the one-thesis layout carries a three-item list as well as it has
carried everything else in this run. No destruction argument owed.
