## Explanation

"Wir machen uns Bilder der Tatsachen." We make to ourselves pictures of
facts. Ogden's translation keeps the German's reflexive shape rather than
smoothing it into plain English — not "we form pictures" but "we make to
ourselves," *uns*, for ourselves, to ourselves. The picture is something
we produce, not something we merely receive by looking. That reflexive
is the whole content of the sentence, and it is easy to read past.

Everything before this sentence described the world on its own: facts
(1, 2), atomic facts and the objects that combine to make them
(2.01-2.034), and finally reality, the total of what exists and does not
(2.04-2.063). Not one of those sentences mentioned a thinker, a mind, a
sentence, or a representation of any kind. 2.1 is the hinge. For the
first time since the book opened, a second thing enters alongside the
world: us, and something we make. The word for what we make, *Bild*,
picture, is deliberately plain — not a technical coinage, the same word
used for a photograph, a painting, a diagram. What that plain word will
turn out to mean, precisely enough to bear the argument's full weight,
is the work of the next two dozen sentences: 2.11 says a picture presents
a possible state of affairs in logical space; 2.12 calls it a model of
reality; 2.13 through 2.15 explain how a picture's elements stand for
objects and relate to each other the way the objects themselves relate.
None of that is 2.1's job. 2.1 only announces that picturing happens at
all, and that it is something *we* do, to facts, for ourselves.

Two things this sentence does not yet claim are worth marking, since the
plain word invites reading them in early. It does not yet say a picture
must resemble what it pictures — that a photograph and a fact must look
alike. And it does not yet say what pictures are *for*: nothing here
mentions language, thought, or a proposition. Both claims are coming
(language as a picture arrives explicitly at 4.01), but 2.1 is the
opening word for the whole apparatus, not its first application. It
buys only this: pictures exist, we make them, and they are of facts —
of what 2.04 through 2.063 just spent six sentences establishing has a
total, determinate shape.

This is also the sentence where the traditional and resolute readings
of the book diverge most sharply of anywhere so far, more sharply than
at 2.01's simple objects. The traditional reading (Russell's own
introduction, and the mainstream of Tractatus commentary since) takes
2.1 as launching the book's real positive achievement: a worked-out
semantic theory, the picture theory of meaning, explaining how language
manages to say anything about the world at all. On this reading, 2.1
through roughly 4.06 is the argument the rest of the book depends on.
The resolute reading (Diamond, Conant) holds that this is exactly the
apparatus the reader is meant to climb through and then recognize, by
6.54, as nonsense — not a theory to take away and use, but the most
elaborate rung on the ladder, the one that most convincingly *looks*
like a theory while (on this reading) failing to state one. Nothing in
2.1 itself settles which is right; the two readings agree about what the
next several dozen sentences say and disagree entirely about what
standing those sentences have once the book is finished. Worth flagging
here, at the door, rather than waiting.

## Zen

Vasubandhu's *Viṃśatikā* argues for *vijñaptimātratā* — "representation-
only": what a mind ever has before it is its own representation
(*vijñapti*), never an external object directly. On this doctrine, the
work 2.1 assigns to *us* — making pictures of facts, for ourselves — is
not one activity among others available to a mind; it is the only
activity a mind performs, full stop. There is no additional channel by
which facts arrive unrepresented, to be compared against the picture.
Every appearance of anything is already a *made* appearance.

The disanalogy is exactly where the two projects part. Vasubandhu's
argument is built to license a further step: because all we ever have
is representation, an external world behind the representations becomes
dispensable, and much of the *Viṃśatikā* is spent showing that appeals to
external objects to explain regularities in experience can be replaced
without loss. 2.1 draws no such conclusion, and could not without undoing
its own last six sentences. 2.04 through 2.063 spent real argumentative
work establishing that facts and their total, reality, are exactly as
determinate and independent of us as the world itself — and 2.1 says we
picture *that*, facts already secured as standing on their own before any
picture-making began. Wittgenstein's "of," in "pictures of facts," is a
relation between two distinct things; Vasubandhu's representation-only
thesis is built to dissolve that very distinctness. Checked by grep for
reuse: Vasubandhu appeared once before, day 21, for a different and
unrelated argument of his (the *paramāṇu* mereological regress against
partless atoms, used against 2.02's simplicity claim) — a different work,
a different doctrine, twenty-nine days back; noted rather than hidden.

## Method

The site-presentation self-assessment is not hypothetical today: day 9
named this exact day as its re-check point, and the wrinkle it flagged is
now visible. `renderTree()` computed a proposition's indentation depth
by counting digits after the decimal point. That is exact for ordinary
numbers, but Wittgenstein pads several first-level batches with a
leading zero that their un-padded counterparts don't carry — 2.01-2.06
now sits beside its true, equally-first-level sibling 2.1 for the first
time today, and the digit-count rule was giving 2.01 one indent step
deeper than 2.1, as if 2.01 were 2.1's child rather than its sibling.

Checked how far the same padding trick recurs before fixing it, rather
than patching only the case in front of me: the identical device appears
at 3.01-3.05, 4.01-4.06, 5.01-5.02, 6.01-6.03 (padding a whole chapter's
first batch, exactly as at chapter 2), at 3.001, 4.001-4.003, 6.001-6.002
(a second, double-zero layer of the same trick), and one level down
inside the tree at 2.201-2.203 and 3.201-3.203 (a single chapter section
padding its own children the same way chapter openings pad theirs). Day
9 rejected a one-line "strip a leading zero" fix specifically because it
handles the top-level case but silently mis-nests deeper instances like
2.0201, which sits under an unlisted "2.020" rather than directly under
2.02. That rejection was correct, and today's fuller survey shows the
problem is more widespread than day 9 could see from a single instance,
which makes patching it case by case even less appropriate than it
looked then.

The actual fix, in new `site/tree-depth.js`: instead of counting digits,
walk a number's decimal part backward one digit at a time and stop at
the nearest shorter prefix that is an *actually existing* proposition in
the book; that proposition is the real parent, recursively, regardless
of how many digits of padding sit in between. This is precisely
Wittgenstein's own footnote rule (*n.m1, n.m2 are comments on n.m*)
applied literally, using only propositions that exist rather than
assuming every intermediate decimal position is itself a numbered
sentence. It requires no chapter-specific rule and no hand-built parent
table — day 9 called an explicit parent map "real new apparatus,
disproportionate" for a single cosmetic indent; a general algorithm
covering every instance at once, proportionate to a wrinkle that recurs
in five chapters, is a different thing than a map hand-built for one.
Extracted as a pure function, alongside `site/progress.js`'s existing
pattern, so it is tested directly (seven cases in
`tests/tree-depth.test.mjs`) rather than only exercised indirectly through
the DOM. This changes how *already-published* days render — 2.01 through
2.063 (days 9 through 49) all move one indent step shallower in the tree
view, now correctly level with 2.1-2.9 — which is a revision of past
presentation, not of any content file; flagged here per METHOD.md's
revision-confession rule, since nothing in `content/` or `journal/`
changes, only how the existing tree draws.

No METHOD.md change otherwise: today's explanation and interpretive-war
paragraph both ran under wording already in place, and the section
transition from the world's own structure (1-2.063) to the pictures we
make of it (2.1 onward) doesn't strain "one day, one thought" or any of
the other explanation rules — it changes the book's subject, not the
shape of a day's work on it.
