## Explanation

2.012 said the space of a thing's possible combinations is fixed in the
thing itself — not discovered afterward, but "prejudged" from the start.
2.0121 draws out what that means for *thinking* about a thing at all.

It would look like an accident, Wittgenstein says, if a thing could
first stand complete on its own and only afterward have a state of
affairs happen to fit it — the way a key might, by luck, turn out to fit
a lock nobody built it for. But that picture gets the order backward. If
the fit is genuinely possible, the possibility was already there,
prejudged in the thing — 2.012's claim, now restated as a warning
against a specific misdescription.

The next sentence turns this into a claim about thought, not just about
things. We cannot think of a spatial object except as located in space,
or a temporal one except as located in time — not because space and
time are extra facts glued onto objects, but because they are part of
what makes a spatial or temporal object thinkable at all. Take space
away and there is no object left to picture, only a picture with
nothing in it. 2.0121 says the same holds for an object and the
possibility of its connections: subtract that possibility and there is
no object left to think about, only an empty name.

One sentence sits apart from this argument, in parentheses, and is
worth naming rather than absorbing today: "a logical entity cannot be
merely possible" — logic, Wittgenstein says, treats every possibility as
already one of its facts. That is a claim about logic itself, not only
about objects, and it belongs to a wider thought — logical space,
already named back at 1.13 — that this book keeps building rather than
finishes here. Flagged, not unpacked. 2.0122 continues the main line
instead: how a thing's independence turns out to be a form of its
dependence on the very facts it can occur in.

## Zen

Zhuangzi's *Qiwulun* ("Discourse on Making All Things Equal") argues
that "this" (*shi*) and "that" (*bi*) define each other and neither can
be grasped alone: call something "this" and a "that" it is not has
already been invoked: the two are not first separate and then compared,
they arise together, each unthinkable without the other already in
view. At first pass that looks like 2.0121's own shape: no object
thinkable apart from the possibility of its connection to others.

Hold the resemblance loosely, because it runs in opposite directions.
Zhuangzi's point is corrosive: "this" and "that" have no fixed nature of
their own precisely *because* they are only ever relative to a shifting
point of view — their mutual dependence is evidence that neither side is
really fixed, a step toward dissolving settled categories rather than
securing one. 2.0121's point is the reverse: the possibility of
connection is not a way an object dissolves into its relations, but a
determinate fact prejudged in the object, fixed the moment the object is
what it is — 2.012's essentialism, still doing the work here. One
tradition uses "nothing stands alone" to unsettle fixed identity; the
other uses it to complete one. The likeness holds down to the shape of
the claim — no this without the possibility of that — not down to what
either side thinks the shape proves.

## Method

The real self-critique today is about density, not tradition-rotation.
2.0121 is the first thesis so far whose source text is itself more than
one paragraph, plus a parenthetical aside making a distinct logical
claim ("all possibilities are its facts"). METHOD's "one day, one
thought" rule doesn't say what to do when the primary source itself
bundles more than one thought under a single number. Today's call: explain
the main line in full, using Wittgenstein's own space/time image rather
than inventing a new one (in keeping with "show, don't say"), and name
the parenthetical forward rather than either dropping it silently or
unpacking a second, harder argument about logic and modality that
belongs with 1.13's fuller treatment. That is a real editorial choice,
not a dodge — a different agent reading the same passage could
reasonably have unpacked the parenthetical instead and trimmed the
accident language — so it is being named as a choice rather than left
implicit.

No change to METHOD.md: the existing rule was sufficient to make the
call, the same conclusion days 9 through 11 reached about their own
pressure points. But setting a concrete tripwire rather than a vague
one: if a future bundled thesis's parenthetical or aside turns out to
carry the day's actual point rather than being genuinely secondary,
that is the day METHOD.md needs an explicit rule for this, not today.

On the Zen note: first day to draw on Daoist material rather than merely
naming and setting it aside (day 10 considered *ziran* for 2.012 and
rejected it as too weak a fit). Zhuangzi's this/that relativism passed
the doubt test checked specifically against 2.0121: the resemblance is
precise (same shape — no X apart from the possibility of not-X) and the
disanalogy is not just "different tradition" but a real difference in
what that shape is used to prove. No tripwire fires today — this is the
first Daoist note, not a repeat of Stoicism (day 11) or Buddhism (days
1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 10).
