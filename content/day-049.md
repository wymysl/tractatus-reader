## Explanation

"Die gesamte Wirklichkeit ist die Welt." The total reality is the world.

Five sentences of buildup end here. 2.04 built the world from the
existent atomic facts alone. 2.05 added that the same totality also
settles which atomic facts do not exist. 2.06 named both sides together
— existence and non-existence, positive fact and negative fact — and
gave the pairing a new word: *Wirklichkeit*, reality. 2.061 and 2.062
spent two sentences establishing that the atomic facts making up that
pairing are independent, one from another, so that none of them can be
inferred from any other. 2.063 closes the run by asserting, flatly, that
this whole apparatus — reality, built from both halves — is nothing
other than the world, which 2.04 had already built from one half alone.

That identity needs earning, not just stating, because 2.06 itself left
a gap open. Reality, as 2.06 defined it, is built from two ingredients;
world, as 2.04 defined it, from one. A totality assembled from two
things sounds broader than a totality assembled from one. If reality
really were the larger of the two, "the total reality is the world"
would be false, or at best a downgrade — reality minus its negative half
equals world. It is neither. The gap closes because of a sentence
already on the books: 2.05 said the totality of existent atomic facts
*also determines* which atomic facts do not exist. Determines, not
merely accompanies. The negative half of reality is not a second,
independent stock of items alongside the positive half, contributed
from elsewhere; it is fixed in full by the positive half, once logical
space — the fixed field of every possible atomic fact, named back at
1.13 — is taken as given. Name what exists, against that fixed
background, and what does not exist follows for free; nothing further
needs settling. So reality, which sounds like existence-plus-non-
existence, two lists, turns out on inspection to be existence, full
stop, described with its shadow made explicit. The shadow was always
there in 2.04; 2.06 just gave it a name, and 2.063 says the name added
no territory.

This also closes a longer loop than the local one. Chapter 1 opened
with "the world is the totality of facts" (1.1) and, by 1.13, "the
facts in logical space are the world." Everything from 2.04 to 2.062 —
atomic facts, their existence and non-existence, their independence,
the limits on inference between them — has been an excavation of what
that opening claim already contained, not an amendment to it. 2.063
is the moment the excavation reports back: reality, patiently built up
with new vocabulary across six sentences, is handed back to the word
the book started with. Nothing in chapter 2 so far has made "world" a
different or bigger thing than chapter 1 already named; it has only
said, in much more detail, what "world" was always going to turn out
to mean. That is also why 2.06 was not wasted effort even though its
extra half collapses away here: without first defining reality as its
own two-sided totality, "the total reality is the world" would say only
"the world is the world," which asserts nothing. 2.06 had to give
reality independent content before 2.063 could non-trivially fold it
back down.

With 2.063, the sentences purely about the world's own makeup — what it
is built from, how much of it is fixed by how much, what can and cannot
be read off one part from another — are finished. The next sentence,
2.1, turns the book's attention for the first time to us: to the
pictures we make of what 2.01 through 2.063 have just finished
describing.

## Zen

The Chandogya Upanishad's ninth lesson (6.1-6.8) has the sage Uddalaka
Aruni teach his son Svetaketu with the same move nine times running: all
things made of clay, he says, are known through knowing one lump of
clay, because whatever their name and shape as this pot or that dish,
"in reality it is just clay" (*vācārambhaṇaṃ vikāro nāmadheyam,
mṛttiketyeva satyam*). After clay, gold and its ornaments, iron and its
tools, each time the same reduction: the many named things collapse,
without remainder, into the one reality underneath their names. The
teaching closes on the person: *tat tvam asi*, that (single reality) you
are. Structurally, 2.063 makes the identical shape of move — an
apparently two-sided, richly named thing (reality, with its positive and
negative facts) is declared, on inspection, to be nothing over and above
a plainer thing already introduced (world) — an identity that must be
earned by looking underneath the names, not read off them.

The disanalogy is where the direction of the reduction points. Uddalaka's
"just clay" is a reduction to a hidden substance, *sat*, being itself,
sitting beneath appearances and unifying them; the pot was never
really separate from the clay, and the teaching's whole force is that
this same hidden unity extends all the way up to the self, which
*tvam*, "you," turns out to have been secretly identical to the
absolute all along. Nothing is added by looking underneath — a single
depth-reality was there from the start, obscured by name and form
(*nāmarūpa*). 2.063's identity has no depth to it and posits no hidden
stuff: reality did not turn out to be secretly made of world the way a
pot turns out to be secretly made of clay. It turned out to add nothing
because 2.05 had already, explicitly, logically, entailed its second
half from its first — an argued consequence sitting in plain sight two
sentences back, not a hidden sameness waiting to be seen through
appearances. Uddalaka asks Svetaketu to see past what a thing is called
to what it really, deeply is. 2.063 asks for no such seeing-past; it
only asks the reader to have believed 2.05 when it said what it said.
