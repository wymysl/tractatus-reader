## Explanation

"Im Sachverhalt hängen die Gegenstände ineinander, wie die Glieder einer
Kette." In the atomic fact objects hang one in another, like the members
of a chain.

Yesterday's explanation named this sentence by number before it arrived:
2.0272 said configuration forms the atomic fact, but left open what makes
an arrangement a genuine configuration rather than a mere heap — day 9's
old flag on 2.01, still unpaid. 2.03 starts paying it, not with an
argument but with a picture. "Ineinander" is doing the actual work, and
it says more than either natural English rendering ("hang one in
another," "the members of a chain") quite catches on its own: not things
resting near each other, but each one's connexion running through the
space the next occupies, the way a chain link does not merely touch its
neighbor but passes through it. That difference has a visible
consequence. Pull a stone from a heap and the heap is smaller, still a
heap. Pull a link from a chain and there is no smaller chain — there are
two pieces, because each link's contribution was never separable from
its neighbors closing around it. Wittgenstein needs exactly that
resistance to painless removal for what a Sachverhalt has and a heap of
objects lacks.

One more thing is worth not losing in translation, since it is easy to
read past. *Glied* is not a specialized word for a mechanical link —
it is the ordinary German for a limb, a member of a body (*Gliedmaßen*),
used for a chain's segments the way English uses "member" as well as
"link." Ogden's "members" keeps that double sense; a modern translator
reaching for "links" would quietly lose it. The ambiguity is not
incidental to the image: a chain of *Glieder* reads first as jointed,
organic, a single articulated thing bending at its couplings, before it
reads as a machine part. The atomic fact 2.03 pictures is closer to a
finger's knuckles than to a mechanic's coupling.

The image also cashes out vocabulary chapter 2 has been carrying since
2.0141: an object's *Form* is the fixed range of atomic facts it can
occur in — which shapes could ever fit it, not which ones happen to. A
chain link, considered by itself before anything is threaded onto it,
is exactly that kind of thing: a particular shape already deciding, in
advance, what other shapes could pass through it. 2.03 adds no new claim
to *Form*; it gives the existing claim something a reader can picture
closing around another link's curve.

What 2.03 withholds is deliberate. It says the objects interlock, not
which interlocking is the right one for this fact rather than some
other — that is 2.031's job, "in a definite way" (*bestimmter Art und
Weise*), a day off. Nor does it name the fitting, considered as a whole;
that is 2.032, where how objects hang together is called, for the first
time, *Struktur*. Today buys only the picture: distinct, shaped things,
joined without dissolving into a mass and without merely sitting side by
side.

## Zen

The Great Chain of Being reaches for the same picture. Rooted in
Aristotle's ladder of natural kinds (*Historia Animalium* VIII.1), given
its metaphysical spine by Plotinus's doctrine of emanation from the One
(*Enneads* V.2), and named as a governing idea of Western thought by
Arthur Lovejoy (*The Great Chain of Being*, 1936), it too needs an image
of connectedness that is neither fusion nor a heap — links, each keeping
its own place and shape, joined only to its immediate neighbors rather
than merged into one undifferentiated thing. That much the two chains
genuinely share, and it is worth taking as more than a coincidence of
word choice: both need the same object to picture things that touch
without losing their distinctness.

Everything past that point pulls apart. The Great Chain is one chain,
run the whole length of being from bare matter to pure intelligence,
admitting no gaps — Lovejoy's "principle of plenitude" requires every
possible degree of perfection to be occupied — and it is ranked: a
link's place in the chain is its value, higher meaning more real, more
perfect, nearer the source. 2.03's chain has neither feature. It is one
chain per atomic fact, not one chain for the whole of being; the world,
next (2.04), is the totality of these chains, indefinitely many, not
rungs on a single ladder. And nothing in it is graded — no object
outranks its neighbor by hanging with it, the relation is pure fit, not
worth. The medieval chain tells you how much reality a thing has by
where in it that thing sits. Wittgenstein's chain does not grade its
members at all; it only asks whether they hold.

## Method

Day 38 named 2.03 as the sentence that would say what holds a genuine
configuration together, and today only partly collects on that. The
explanation gives the picture — interlocking rather than adjacency, a
chain rather than a heap — but the actual content of a correct fit
(2.031, "in a definite way") and its name (2.032, *Struktur*) are held
back on purpose, matching how the German itself withholds them across
three separate sentences. Recording this explicitly so a future run
does not read today's journal as claiming the day-9 debt fully closed:
it is being paid in three installments, of which this is the first.

Checked METHOD's interpretive-war rule with an actual question rather
than the reflex re-confirmation the last several days have given it.
2.03 is not more of 2.021's substance angle — it is much closer to
2.01's own divergence, restated one more time: is "objects hang into one
another" a real relation obtaining out in the world (traditional
reading, internal relations as a further metaphysical fact machinery
must accommodate) or a picture doing grammatical work on the way to
being thrown away with the rest of the apparatus at 6.54 (resolute
reading, "hanging together" as no more assertable, in the end, than
"combination" was)? That is the same disagreement day 9 located on 2.01
and day 38 relocated the running streak to yesterday, not a new one —
2.03 elaborates *how* objects combine, which is 2.01's question
answered in more detail, not a fresh question about what objects *are*.
Declined for the same reason yesterday declined, continuing the streak
at 2.01's locus rather than resetting it a second time in two days.

Zen-source diversity checked against the last five days (34: Kant, 35:
Parmenides, 36: Aquinas, 37: Plato, 38: Composition as Identity): the
Great Chain of Being is clear of the window as its own entry, though it
shares territory with two names already used this project — Aristotle
(days 15, 33) and Aquinas (day 36, inside the window if credited here).
Checked this directly: the note's primary attribution is to Plotinus
(never used) and to Lovejoy's naming (never used, and not itself a

arranged) is the one Composition as Identity already made yesterday,
and using it today would answer a question 2.03 is not asking — 2.03
is about how parts connect, not about whether the connected whole is a
further entity. Left the chariot simile on the table for a day that
actually needs the parts-versus-whole point, not the connection-as-such
point.

No app or presentation change. 2.03's imagery needs nothing beyond
