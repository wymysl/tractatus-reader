## Explanation

2.01 left something unfinished on purpose: it said an atomic fact is
objects, combined, but not what makes a combination possible in the first
place. 2.011 takes the first step toward answering that, and it is a
small step with a large claim packed into it: "it is essential to a
thing that it can be a constituent part of an atomic fact."

"Essential" here is not the loose, everyday sense — important, central.
It is the older, stricter sense: a property something cannot lack and
still be what it is. Compare an accidental property, like a coin's being
in my pocket right now, with an essential one, like a triangle's having
three sides — take the second away and there is no longer a triangle to
talk about. 2.011's claim is that a thing's *combinability* — its
standing possibility of entering into atomic facts — belongs in the
second column, not the first. It is not an extra fact about a thing,
discovered afterward and tacked on; it is part of what makes the thing
the thing it is.

This is a sharper claim than it first sounds, because it is only about
*possibility*, not about any actual combination. A plug's pin pattern
fits some sockets and not others whether or not it is ever plugged into
anything; the fitting-or-not is settled by the shape of the pins alone.
2.011 says something similar about objects: which atomic facts an object
*could* occur in is fixed by what the object essentially is, prior to
and independent of whether any of them ever actually obtain. Nothing yet
has been said about how large or small that space of possibilities is,
or what fixes it — that is 2.012's job, immediately next: "in logic
nothing is accidental." Today's thesis only opens the door those later
sentences walk through.

## Zen

2.011 makes objects essentially what Buddhist philosophy calls
*svabhāva* — "own-being": each thing carries, built into what it is
and prior to any relation, a fixed nature that determines what it can
and cannot combine with. Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka is built almost entirely
around denying that anything has this. Its central claim, śūnyatā —
emptiness — is that no phenomenon has a nature of its own apart from its
dependence on other things; the very idea of a fixed, relation-prior
essence is the specific error the whole tradition targets.

Worth noting where this sits relative to day 8's Zen note, since it is
close kin: day 8 compared atomic facts to Abhidharma dharmas, the
tradition's own candidate for what is ultimately real, and flagged the
disanalogy (dharmas are momentary; atomic facts are not). Classical
Abhidharma itself holds that dharmas *do* have svabhāva — which is
exactly what later Madhyamaka turns around and rejects in that same
tradition. So the tension named here is not a new import; it is the next
link in the same chain day 8 started, now landing on objects rather
than atomic facts. And it is only the seed of it: 2.011 commits to
objects having *some* essential nature, but the full svabhāva picture —
simple, unchanging, substance itself — doesn't arrive until 2.02 and
2.021. The sharper version of this contrast belongs to those days, not
this one.

## Method

This is the fifth day out of ten to reach for a Buddhist concept in the
Zen section (days 2, 5, 7 used dependent origination; day 8, Abhidharma;
today, Madhyamaka's critique of svabhāva). That is a real pattern, not
an accident of which comparisons happen to be true, and it is worth
naming rather than letting it pass silently a fifth time. The honest
defense: each of the five names a *different* concept doing a
*different* piece of work — no-fixed-things, no-container, no-exemption,
dharma-as-real-simple, and now the specific critique of intrinsic
essence — and today's is the first to engage the same Abhidharma
material day 8 opened rather than starting a fresh thread, which is
progress of a kind, not repetition. But the tradition pool this project
draws from for contrast is narrower than it should be for a book that
claims, in its own preface, to speak to a general reader. If an eleventh
day reaches for Buddhist metaphysics again without a comparably strong
reason, that is the point to treat it as a rut rather than a resonance,
and to look outside Buddhist philosophy for the next Zen note — Daoist
or Stoic material on essence and relation would be worth checking first.
Flagging this concretely now so it cannot be waved off as vague self-
awareness later.

On the explanation itself: chose not to invoke METHOD's interpretive-war
rule today, even though it applies in the background to every sentence
in this stretch of the book. 2.011 doesn't newly divide the traditional
and resolute readings the way 2.01 did — both readings agree this
sentence is doing the same architectural work (building toward internal
properties and logical space), and disagree only about the book's
status as a whole, which day 9 already stated. Repeating that divergence
on a thesis it doesn't newly bear on would be filler, not honesty; METHOD's
rule is to say it where they diverge on *today's* thesis, not on every
thesis touched by the book's larger interpretive question.

No site changes today. 2.011's numbering (three digits after the
decimal) nests cleanly under 2.01 by the tree's existing depth-by-digit-
count rule, without the zero-padding wrinkle flagged in day 9's journal
— that wrinkle is specific to the padded first-level comments on 2
(2.01–2.06 versus 2.1–2.9), not to deeper extensions like this one.
