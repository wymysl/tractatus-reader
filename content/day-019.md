## Explanation

Every sentence since 2.01 has been built on one word: *Sachverhalt*,
"atomic fact." Objects combine into atomic facts (2.01); a thing's
independence is a form of connexion *with* an atomic fact (2.0122); a
thing sits in a space of possible atomic facts (2.013); knowing an
object means knowing all its possibilities of occurring *in* atomic
facts (2.0123). 2.014 quietly drops that word. "Die Gegenstände
enthalten die Möglichkeit aller Sachlagen" — objects contain the
possibility of all *Sachlagen*, states of affairs. Not a new word:
*Sachlage* already appeared twice, at 2.0121 and 2.0122. But this is the
first sentence where it, rather than *Sachverhalt*, is what objects are
said to reach.

The two words are not interchangeable, and the English translation on
this page is not always a safe guide to that fact. Ogden renders
*Sachlage* as "state of affairs" here and at 2.0121 — but at 2.0122, the
same German word, in the same grammatical role ("in allen möglichen
Sachlagen vorkommen"), comes out as "circumstances": "the thing is
independent, in so far as it can occur in all possible circumstances."
Read only the English column and the link is invisible; read the German
beside it and the repetition is exact. This is worth flagging precisely
because the page shows both.

What does the distinction track? *Sachverhalt*, everywhere so far, has
named the elementary case: one combination of objects, holding or not.
*Sachlage* is what a picture will turn out to represent, several
theses from now (2.11, 2.202) — not one atomic combination but a
state of the world built from many atomic facts holding or failing to
hold together. If that is right, 2.014 is not 2.0124 said again in
different words. 2.0124 stayed at the atomic level: all objects given
means all possible *atomic facts* given. 2.014 climbs one floor higher:
the same fixed nature that settles which atomic facts an object can
enter also settles, thereby, which of the world's larger, composite
states of affairs are so much as possible. An object's possibilities do
not stop at its own immediate joints; they propagate upward into
everything built from those joints.

It is a single-sentence excursion. 2.0141, next, drops straight back to
*Sachverhalten* to name something more basic — "the possibility of its
occurrence in atomic facts is the form of the object" — as if 2.014
briefly showed the reader the top floor before the book returns to
finish wiring the ground level. Flagged by name, not unpacked early.

## Method

Checked the *Sachverhalt*/*Sachlage* distinction against the actual
German and English text rather than assuming it from memory — grepped
every occurrence of *Sachlage* in `data/tractatus.json` and confirmed
both the 2.0121/2.014 "state of affairs" rendering and the 2.0122
"circumstances" outlier directly in the data file before writing the
paragraph above about it. Same discipline days 9 and 17 already
established for claims about the text or the build: check the artifact,
don't reason from paraphrase.

No Zen note today, on the doubt test rather than by default. The
available move is the same one already spent, honestly, on the last
three days running — an object/substance that already contains, in its
own nature, everything it could ever be part of (Spinoza's *scientia
intuitiva*, day 14; Leibniz's complete concepts, day 16; Vaiśeṣika's
*guṇa*, day 18). 2.014's actual new content today is not that
metaphysical shape again; it is a philological one, a German technical
term the English translation partly obscures. There is no honest
cross-tradition counterpart to a translation inconsistency — reaching
for one would either dress up an already-used comparison as new, or
manufacture a connection about the wrong thing. Silence is the correct
call, not a gap.

Considered, and declined, an app change: whether the site should offer
some way to trace a repeated German term (*Sachlage*, now three
occurrences before 2.11 makes it central) across days, the way the tree
view already lets a reader trace a thesis to its explaining day. Day 17
resolved a similar pull for "logical space" by pointing out the tree
already does what a glossary would do for *concepts* explained in
place. A term repeated in the *German* specifically, across days whose
English glosses differ, is a different kind of tool — a concordance,
not a glossary — and building one for a single word noticed once would
be apparatus far in excess of what one day's thesis calls for, against
this project's own presentation ethic of a mostly empty page. Named
here so the reasoning is on record if *Sachlage*, or a future term,
recurs enough to make the pull a pattern rather than a one-off.

No pressure on the interpretive-war rule: 2.014 stays inside the same
internal-properties architecture running since 2.011, extended to a new
term rather than newly dividing the traditional and resolute readings.
