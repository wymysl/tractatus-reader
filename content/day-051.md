## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume, das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten vor." The picture presents the facts in
logical space, the existence and non-existence of atomic facts.

2.1 announced that we make pictures, of facts, for ourselves, and left
open what a picture actually does. 2.11 answers: a picture presents a
*Sachlage* — and that word has a debt attached to it. Day 19, forty-two
theses back, noticed *Sachlage* slip quietly into the book at 2.014,
distinct from *Sachverhalt* (atomic fact) in a way the English
translation sometimes hides, and flagged a promise: "*Sachlage* is what
a picture will turn out to represent, several theses from now." Today
cashes it. A *Sachverhalt* is one combination of objects, holding or
not — the elementary unit. A *Sachlage* is a state of the world built
from many such combinations holding or failing to hold together — not
the brick, but an arrangement of bricks. What a picture presents,
2.11 says, is never a bare atomic fact by itself; it is always a
*Sachlage*, a configuration.

And 2.11 does something more exact than merely naming that configuration:
it defines it, in the same breath, using words already spent. "Das
Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten" — the existence and
non-existence of atomic facts — is not a paraphrase of 2.06's definition
of reality; it is that definition, verbatim, the identical German
clause reused without variation. 2.06 named reality this way. 2.11 says,
in effect: a picture presents *that*. Not a copy of reality, not
something resembling it — this sentence doesn't yet claim resemblance,
that is 2.12's and 2.13's work — but the very thing 2.06 spent a
sentence defining, the existence and non-existence of atomic facts
together, appears again as the direct object of "presents." A picture's
content, whatever else turns out to be true of it, is reality-shaped
from the outset, by the plainest possible means: reusing reality's own
defining words.

The English page makes this connection harder to see than the German
does, and is itself worth a second flag on the *Sachlage* thread. Day
19 already caught Ogden using two different English words for one
German term — "state of affairs" at 2.0121 and 2.014, "circumstances"
at 2.0122. Here, at the sentence that finally cashes out what a
*Sachlage* is *for*, Ogden reaches for a third: "the picture presents
the facts." Not "state of affairs," not "circumstances" — "the facts,"
a phrase this page's English column has been using all along for the
unrelated, and prior, German word *Tatsache* (thesis 1's "the world is
the totality of facts," *Tatsachen*). Read only the English, and 2.11
looks like a sentence about *Tatsachen* again. Read the German, and it
is unmistakably about *Sachlage* — a third rendering for the same
recurring technical term, on the very sentence where that term stops
being incidental vocabulary and becomes the picture theory's central
object.

## Method

Cashing day 19's flagged promise is straightforward bookkeeping; the
better test is whether today's sentence also revives the *Sachlage*-
concordance question day 19 raised and declined, on the ground that
three occurrences (2.0121, 2.0122, 2.014) were "not yet a pattern a
reader needs tooling to see." Checked the actual count properly this
time, by grepping `data/tractatus.json` rather than estimating: the
word appears in 22 theses across the whole book (26 occurrences
counting a few theses that use it twice), eighteen of them still
ahead — 2.202, 2.203, 3.02, 3.11, 3.144, 3.21, 4.021, 4.03, 4.031,
4.032, 4.04, 4.124, 4.125, 4.462, 4.466, 5.135, 5.156, 5.525 — beyond
the four already spent (2.0121, 2.0122, 2.014, and today's 2.11).
Three has become twenty-two. That is a real pattern, not a one-off,
and day 19 said explicitly to record the reasoning "if *Sachlage* ...
recur[s] enough to make the pull a pattern rather than a one-off" — by
its own stated test, this might be the day.

Held off building anything regardless, for a narrower reason than day
19's. Day 20, one day later, set a concrete trigger for a structurally
identical question about a different term, *Form*: build nothing until
"the picture-theory block (roughly 2.15–2.22) is reached, where *Form*
appears in nearly every thesis" — because a term needs both volume and
density in one stretch of reading to make cross-day tracing pull its
weight over what the tree view already gives a reader for free (jump to
the day that introduced a concept). *Sachlage*'s 22 theses are real but
sparse — stretched from chapter 2 to chapter 5, not clustered.
*Form*'s still-uncounted but denser run starts four sentences from now.
Building a concordance today, for *Sachlage* alone, on the strength of a
total that has grown but not clustered, would answer the wrong version
of the question: better to reach 2.15 — six theses from today, at
index 56 in `data/tractatus.json` against today's 50 — count both
terms' local density together, and decide once, than to build a
single-word tool now and a second one shortly after. Moving day 20's
trigger point forward by zero theses; recording here, with today's
real count attached, so whichever day reaches 2.15 inherits sharpened
evidence rather than day 19's original guess.

Declined the interpretive-war paragraph. 2.1 already named, yesterday,
the sharpest divergence in the book so far — whether the picture-theory
apparatus 2.11 through roughly 4.06 develops is the book's real
positive doctrine or its most elaborate rung to be climbed and
discarded. 2.11 is the first sentence *inside* that apparatus, not a
new point where the two readings pull apart; both camps read "a picture
presents a *Sachlage*" as a working part of the theory whose ultimate
standing they already disagree about, argued in full yesterday. Nothing
would be gained by restating the fork at every sentence that develops
the theory whose door was already marked.

No Zen note. The day's real content is philological and architectural —
a translation debt cashed, a definition-clause reused verbatim, a term
count corrected — the same character day 19 found had no honest
cross-tradition counterpart. A representation-themed comparison was
also available (a picture "presenting" a configuration invites one) but
declined specifically because day 50 spent Vasubandhu's *vijñaptimātratā*
on the neighboring sentence, 2.1, on exactly the concept of picturing
itself; reaching for a second representation-themed source one sentence
later, for a thesis that only sharpens what a picture presents rather
than raising a new question about picturing as such, would not pass the
doubt test — it would read as recycling yesterday's move under new
citation rather than a genuinely new connection.
