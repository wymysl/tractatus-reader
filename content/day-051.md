## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume, das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten vor." The picture presents the facts in
logical space, the existence and non-existence of atomic facts.

2.1 only announced that picturing happens, and that it is something we
do. It said nothing about what a picture delivers. 2.11 is the first
sentence to say: a picture presents a *Sachlage* — constituted by which
*Sachverhalte* hold and which do not — and it presents it *im logischen
Raume*, in logical space, the very space 1.13 used to define the world
itself ("Die Tatsachen im logischen Raum sind die Welt," the facts in
logical space are the world). A picture, in other words, is not staged
in some separate representational space of its own; it presents
something inside the one space the world already occupies.

That sentence just used three different German technical terms doing
three different jobs, and 2.11 is the first place all three appear
together. *Tatsache*, fact, has been doing work since proposition 1 (the
world is the totality of facts). *Sachverhalt*, atomic fact, arrived at
2.01 (a combination of objects). *Sachlage* arrived more quietly, at
2.0121 (day 24) and 2.0122 and 2.014 — a *state of affairs*: not a fact
that holds, but a candidate configuration, the kind of thing that could
hold or fail to hold. 2.11 puts this third term to its first real work:
a picture doesn't present a Sachverhalt directly, and it doesn't present
the Tatsache-totality that is the world; it presents a Sachlage — one
determinate configuration, picked out by fixing, for some stock of
atomic facts, which exist and which don't.

Ogden's translation, the only English on this site, gets this wrong
exactly once, and the once is here. *Sachlage* occurs twenty-two times
in the book. Twenty times Ogden renders it "state of affairs" or "states
of affairs" — including nine sentences earlier at 2.0121, and three
sentences ahead at 2.202, which restates this very sentence ("Das Bild
stellt eine mögliche Sachlage im logischen Raume dar," the picture
represents a possible state of affairs in logical space) correctly. Once
he renders it "circumstances" (2.0122). And once — 2.11, the sentence
that first puts the word to use in the picture theory itself — he
renders it simply "the facts," the same English word his own
translation already spends on *Tatsache* since proposition 1. A reader
working only from the English default sees "the picture presents the
facts... of atomic facts" and has no way to know a third, freshly
important term sits where "facts" appears twice. Toggling to German (the
button is built for exactly this) shows it directly: *Sachlage* once,
*Sachverhalten* once, two different words where the English repeats one.
Worth noting in Ogden's favor: the sentence's verb is not among the
casualties. 2.11 uses *vorstellen* ("stellt... vor," presents); 2.202
uses *darstellen* ("stellt... dar," represents) for its close restatement.
Ogden's "presents" and "represents" track that distinction correctly —
the slip is local to the one noun, not a sign of carelessness running
through the sentence.

2.11 is also, itself, a compressed sentence that the text will shortly
pull apart. Its two clauses — the Sachlage-in-logical-space claim, and
the existence-and-non-existence-of-atomic-facts claim — get restated
separately at 2.202 and 2.201, each with a word 2.11 doesn't yet use:
*possible* / *possibility of*. 2.11 says only that a picture presents a
Sachlage; it does not yet say that what a picture presents is one
*possibility* among others, a claim with real weight (it is what makes a
picture capable of being wrong). That claim belongs to 2.201 and 2.202,
not to today.

## Method

Preparing to quote 2.11's own English text surfaced a real bug in how
this site renders it: "the existence and non-ex\-is\-tence of atomic
facts" — a literal backslash sitting inside the word, twice. Checked
`data/tractatus.json` directly rather than assuming a one-off typo: the
pattern (`\-` at a syllable break) matches TeX's discretionary-hyphen
command exactly, and it recurs in 37 of 526 statements, wherever the
source text is a long word Wittgenstein's English typesetting could
break across a line — "atom\-ic," "truth-pos\-si\-bil\-i\-ties," "for\-mal—
log\-i\-cal—prop\-er\-ties." Two of the 37 are already published (2.0233,
day 30; 2.05, day 45), so this has been live and visibly broken — a
stray backslash in running prose, on a site whose whole presentation
ethic is clean typography and high contrast — since day 45, unflagged
until it happened to sit inside today's own thesis.

`data/tractatus.json` is immutable, so the fix belongs in `build.mjs`,
not the source: added `deTeXHyphens()`, converting `\-` to the Unicode
soft hyphen (U+00AD), the exact HTML equivalent of a TeX discretionary
hyphen — invisible unless a browser actually breaks the line there,
which is what the original typesetting command meant in the first
place. Applied once, where statements are loaded, so every downstream
consumer (`units/day-NNN.json`, `tree.json`) gets the corrected text
without a per-call-site fix. Two new tests: a unit test on the function
itself, and a build-fixture test confirming the substitution reaches
both emitted files. `npm test` (32 passing, up from 30) and
`node build.mjs` both green before committing. This silently corrects
the rendering of two already-published days (30 and 45) — no
`content/` or `journal/` text changes, only how existing statement text
displays — flagged here per METHOD.md's revision-confession rule, same
as day 50's tree-depth fix.

No interpretive-war paragraph today. The translation point above is a
philological finding, checkable by anyone who toggles to German and
counts; it is not a place where the traditional and resolute readings
would disagree about anything — both would read Ogden's "the facts" here
as a plain translation slip, not a substantive interpretive choice.
Forcing a traditional/resolute fork onto a copy-editing observation
would manufacture a divergence that isn't there, which is exactly what
day 49 declined to do for 2.063.

No Zen note today. Considered the *I Ching*'s hexagram lines — each of
six positions unambiguously yin or yang, a determinate figure built from
six simultaneous existence/non-existence judgments — as a parallel to
"das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten" jointly constituting
a Sachlage. The shape is real, but it belongs to a later day: 2.11
presents one Sachlage, not yet the exhaustive combinatorial space (that
arrives explicitly at 4.27's *K<sub>n</sub>* count of existence/non-
existence groupings and the truth-table apparatus after it). Using the
hexagram parallel now, before the text itself claims exhaustiveness,
would import an idea 2.11 hasn't earned yet — against METHOD's own
"reader is intelligent but untrained" discipline, which cuts against
smuggling in later structure early just as much as against unglossed
notation. Noted here so the parallel isn't lost: it is reserved for
4.27, not spent today.

No METHOD.md change beyond the site fix already logged above: rule 4
(gloss new technical vocabulary in plain speech before leaning on it)
covered introducing Sachlage's first working use without strain, and
the explanation and method-note disciplines both applied as already
written.
