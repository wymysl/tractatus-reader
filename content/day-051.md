## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume ... vor." The picture
presents the situation in logical space. *Sachlage* is not a new
coinage — it has already appeared three times, at 2.0121, 2.0122, and
2.014 — but this is the first sentence to give it a job: something a
picture does. And Ogden's translation, which has rendered *Sachlage*
three different ways already ("a state of affairs," "circumstances,"
"states of affairs"), renders it a fourth way here: "facts." That
collides with a word already spoken for. *Tatsache* — fact — has meant
one thing since 1.1: what is the case, actual, existing. Ogden's "facts"
here makes 2.11 read as if the picture presents facts outright, the same
item 1.1 named, when the German is careful to keep three separate words
apart: *Tatsache* (fact), *Sachverhalt* (atomic fact), *Sachlage*
(situation). This is worth flagging plainly rather than working around
silently, since a reader working only from Ogden's English has no way to
notice that the translation has just erased a distinction the original
draws.

What the sentence itself says, once the terms are kept apart: a picture
presents a *Sachlage*, a situation — and the appositive that follows
names what a situation is made of: "das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von
*Sachverhalten*," the obtaining and non-obtaining of atomic facts,
plural. A situation, then, is not one atomic fact but a configuration of
several — some obtaining, some not, together. That is new. Nothing
before 2.11 named a unit between a single atomic fact and the world
entire (2.04: the totality of *existent* atomic facts). A situation is
partial and can be merely possible: a picture need not present the
world, or even present something actual, to present a genuine situation.

The other half of the sentence, "im logischen Raume," in logical space,
reaches back to 1.13: "Die Tatsachen im logischen Raum sind die Welt" —
the facts in logical space are the world. That sentence put the world's
own facts in logical space. 2.11 puts a picture's situation in the very
same space, not some separate picture-realm alongside it. This is
stated, not yet earned — 2.11 does not say *how* a picture manages to
reach into the world's own space, only that whatever it presents is
locatable there, comparable in principle to what the world itself
contains. The how is reserved: 2.12 calls the picture a model; 2.13
through 2.15 explain the correspondence, element to object, that makes
the reaching-in possible. One day, one thought — today's is only what a
picture presents, not the mechanics of presenting it.

## Zen

The *Yijing*'s sixty-four hexagrams were considered once before, at day
16, for 2.0124, and set aside there: the disanalogy on offer was thin,
a numerological convention (three lines to a trigram, six to a
hexagram) rather than a difference that mattered. Reconsidered here
because 2.11 asks a different question than 2.0124 did. 2.0124 was about
the space of possibility being fixed in advance, once the elements are
given — a claim about closure. 2.11 is about presenting one situation
within an already-fixed space — a claim about a single act of picturing.
A cast hexagram is exactly that: six lines, each settled one way or the
other, broken or unbroken, drawn from the fixed space of all sixty-four
such settlings, presented as an answer to a particular question about a
particular situation. Existence and non-existence of six small
components, standing for the state of something larger — structurally,
line for line, what 2.11 describes.

The disanalogy this time is not the one day 16 gave, and needs to be
found fresh rather than inherited: it lies in how the presenting is
supposed to work. A hexagram answers by correlation — an inherited
scheme of assigned meanings and a cosmology of change (the *Yi*'s own
commentarial tradition) connects a given line-pattern to a given
real-world situation, and nothing about the hexagram's own shape need
resemble what it is read to mean. The very next sentences of the
Tractatus rule this out for a Tractarian picture: 2.12 calls it a model,
and 2.13 through 2.15 require the picture's own elements to correspond
to objects and to relate to each other as those objects relate — the
presenting must run through a shared structure, not an assigned
convention. A hexagram could, in principle, be reassigned to mean
anything; a Tractarian picture, once its elements are fixed, could not
be repointed at an unrelated situation without ceasing to be a picture
of it. Recording both halves of this openly: the same candidate day 16
turned away, returned now on different grounds, because a different
thesis asks a different question of it.

## Method

No interpretive-war paragraph today. The traditional/resolute fork day
50 opened at 2.1 is a fork about the whole apparatus 2.1 begins — what
standing the picture theory has once the ladder is climbed. 2.11 does
not reopen that fork; it is the apparatus's own first move, read the
same way by both camps (they disagree about what the apparatus amounts
to in the end, not about what a "situation in logical space" is while
building it). Naming the fork again here would be repeating yesterday's
flag rather than finding a new one, which METHOD.md's "where they don't
diverge, stay silent" rules out.

The Zen note is this project's first return to a previously-declined
candidate. Worth being explicit about what makes that legitimate rather
than a quiet do-over: day 16's decline was a judgment about 2.0124
specifically — that the *Yijing*'s disanalogy, on the question 2.0124
asks, was thin. 2.11 asks a different question, and the disanalogy that
answers it (correlation versus shared structure) is a different
disanalogy, not a second attempt at the first one. If a third occasion
reached for the *Yijing* on essentially day 16's original question and
found a thicker disanalogy only by trying harder, that would be worth
distrusting; today isn't that, since the thesis itself changed what
there was to compare.
