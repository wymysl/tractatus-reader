## Explanation

2.1 only announced the activity: we make pictures, of facts, for
ourselves. It said nothing about what a picture contains. 2.11 is the
first sentence to say that: "the picture presents the situation in
logical space, the existence and non-existence of atomic facts."

Two different German nouns sit inside that one sentence, and Ogden's
English quietly loses the seam between them. *Sachverhalte* — the second
noun, "atomic facts" — is old vocabulary by now; every sentence since
2.01 has run on it, and Ogden has rendered it "atomic fact" without
exception since the book opened. *Sachlage* — the first noun, what the
picture presents — is not old vocabulary in the same way. It surfaced at
2.0121 ("a state of affairs"), at 2.0122 ("circumstances," the one place
Ogden breaks his own pattern), and at 2.014 ("states of affairs," day
19's thesis, which flagged that the word "makes it central" once 2.11
arrived). Here, in the sentence that finally puts *Sachlage* to its main
work, Ogden reaches for a third English rendering: "facts." Read only in
English, this sentence looks like it says a picture presents *the facts*
— *the existence and non-existence of atomic facts* — as if the same
plural noun were doing the job twice, once loosely and once precisely.
Read against the German beside it, the two clauses are not the same
word restated; they are two different technical terms, one for the
determinate situation as a whole (*Sachlage*, singular) and one for what
that situation is built from (*Sachverhalte*, plural), collapsing onto
one English word only because Ogden's third gloss happens to coincide
with his consistent gloss for the other term. Not an error exactly — a
picture's *Sachlage* really is nothing over and above a determinate
pattern of existing and non-existing *Sachverhalte*, so "facts" is not
false of either — but it is the one place so far where the translation
hides a seam the German keeps visible.

The substance under the philology is what should not get lost: a
picture's content is not restricted to what is actually the case.
Existence *and* non-existence, named together, symmetrically, the way
2.06 named positive and negative facts together five days ago. A
picture that could only present what exists would be a record, not yet
a picture in the sense this book is building toward — one that can also
misrepresent, one that could be checked against the world and found
wrong. 2.11 does not yet say a picture can be false; it only makes room
for the possibility, by making non-existence something a picture
presents exactly as squarely as existence. The next several sentences
spend that room: 2.12 calls the picture a model of reality, 2.13
through 2.15 explain how its elements stand in for objects. 2.11's own
job is smaller and comes first — fixing what a picture is *of*, before
any sentence says how it manages to be of it.

"Logical space" is the sentence's third piece of continuity, not a new
term: 1.13 named it forty-six days ago as where the facts that make up
the world sit, and 2.013 (day 17) cashed the name out for a single
object's possibilities. 2.11 is the occurrence day 5's method note
pointed to when it held off on a glossary entry for the term; nothing
new is owed here beyond what those two days already said, and the tree
view already links a reader back to both.

## Method

Day 19's method note left a specific, checkable trigger on record: it
declined to build any tracking tool for *Sachlage*'s scattered English
glosses, on the ground that three occurrences (2.0121, 2.0122, 2.014)
was not yet a pattern, but noted the word would "make it central" once
2.11 arrived and asked to revisit the question then. That day is today.
The honest answer, now that the trigger has actually fired, is still no
new tool. What the tree view already gives a reader — every thesis
number in the tree links straight to the day that explained it, the
mechanism day 17 relied on for "logical space" itself — reaches
*Sachlage*'s prior occurrences exactly as well as it reaches any other
term: today's explanation names 2.0121, 2.0122, and 2.014 by number, and
a reader can click through to each. A concordance keyed to one German
word would be real, standing apparatus for a job the site's existing,
general navigation already does in this specific case. Watched for two
years of book-time, the watch resolves to: still no.

The real site-presentation fix today is smaller and was forced by this
thesis's own English text, not by the philology above. Two literal
backslash-hyphen pairs sit inside today's own word for "non-existence"
in `data/tractatus.json` — a scanned book's line-break marks, preserved
character-for-character from wherever the original word happened to
wrap on Ogden's page. Rendered verbatim through `site/app.js`'s
`innerHTML`, they show up on the page exactly as typed, the word broken
in two places it no longer breaks at. Checked how far this recurs before treating today's
instance as the only one: 37 statement entries carry it, 141 occurrences
in total, thanks to `\-` being a stable enough marker to grep for
directly against `data/tractatus.json` rather than trust from memory.
Four of those entries have already been published — 2.0231 (day 28),
2.0233 (day 30), 2.05 (day 45), and 2.11 today — meaning three
already-live days have quietly carried this artifact since day 28
without it being caught, because none of the three needed the broken
word inside their own explanation's quoted English the way today's does.

The fix belongs in `build.mjs`, not `data/tractatus.json` — the source
file is immutable by ROUTINE.md's guardrail, and the artifact is a
presentation defect in how the raw text reaches the page, not an error
in the raw text's own record of what Ogden printed. New
`stripLineBreakHyphens()` strips the literal `\-` pairs from every
statement's `de` and `en` field (and from `preface.json`'s, on the same
reasoning, though it carries none today) before they are written into
`dist/units/*.json`, `dist/tree.json`, and `dist/preface.json` — one
function, applied uniformly, rather than a patch aimed at 2.11's own
text alone. Covered directly in `tests/build.test.mjs`: a unit test
against the helper (three cases, including that a real hyphen inside a
hyphenated compound like "subject-predicate" survives while the
scanned line-break marks around it do not), and a build-level test
confirming the stripped text actually reaches `dist/units/`,
`dist/tree.json`, and `dist/preface.json`. `npm test` and `node
build.mjs` both green before committing.

This changes how the three already-published entries above render —
confessed here per METHOD.md's revision rule, the same shape of
confession day 50 made for the tree-depth fix: no `content/` or
`journal/` file changes, only the raw statement text those three days
already display, now shown as Ogden actually printed it rather than
with a scanned book's line-wrap marks still attached.

No Zen note today. The nearest live candidate, the *I Ching*'s
generation of a hexagram from six yin-or-yang lines, was already raised
and set aside once, at day 16, for a nearby thesis (2.0124) — rejected
there for a thin disanalogy, a numerological convention rather than a
difference in the underlying picture. The disanalogy available today is
a better one — the *Sachverhalte* a picture presents are independent of
one another (2.061, 2.062), where a hexagram's lines carry positional
and relational meaning against each other that a bare independent
pattern does not — but it is also, in shape, the same move day 46's
Zen note already spent five days ago against Spinoza and Hegel: fused
versus separate, one act versus two independent items. Reaching for it
a second time this week, against a source already declined once before
that, would be filling the section rather than finding something
genuinely new in it. Checked a second candidate, Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika's
formal category of absence (*abhāva*, distinct from Vaiśeṣika's *guṇa*
already used at day 18): its natural disanalogy — absence as parasitic
on a specific counterpositive presence, against 2.06's already-argued
peerhood of positive and negative facts — is the identical shape again.
Today's genuine new content is the philological seam above, not a fresh
metaphysical one; per METHOD.md, silence rather than a third dressing
of an argument already made this week.

No METHOD.md change: the explanation rules handled a sentence that
