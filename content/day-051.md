## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume, das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten vor." The picture presents the
*Sachlage* — the situation — in logical space: the existence and
non-existence of atomic facts. The sentence has a shape easy to miss on
a first pass: it names what a picture presents, then immediately says
what that thing consists in. A situation just *is* some atomic facts
existing and others not. Nothing new is coined here; the whole sentence
is built from parts this page has already laid down.

Both parts were promised in advance. *Sachlage* first appeared at
2.0121 and 2.0122, and 2.014 used it to say that objects fix the
possibility of every situation, not only every atomic fact. Day 19's
entry on 2.014 flagged, without yet arguing it, that "*Sachlage* is
what a picture will turn out to represent, several theses from now
(2.11, 2.202)" — a bet placed thirty-one days ago, cashed today.
*Logical space* is older still: 1.13 named it, and day 5's method note
predicted by name where it would resurface — "2.013, 2.11, 3.4 onward."
2.11 is the second of those three promised landings, arriving exactly
on schedule.

What the appositive adds is sharper than either promise alone predicted.
"Das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten" is not a fresh
description improvised for pictures — it is 2.06's own definition of
*Wirklichkeit*, reality, word for word: "Das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen
von Sachverhalten ist die Wirklichkeit." 2.11 is not merely saying a
picture presents *something*; it is saying a picture presents something
built exactly the way reality is built. A situation is a candidate
reality — a specific pattern of which atomic facts hold and which do
not — without yet being asserted as *the* reality, the one 2.063 called
the world. That gap between candidate and actual is the whole point:
it is what lets a picture be false. A picture that could only ever
present the facts, full stop, could never misfire. A picture that
presents a *situation* — one determinate way among the others logical
space already holds open, per 1.13 and 2.0121 — can present a pattern
that reality does not, in fact, instantiate.

Ogden's English obscures exactly the connection that makes this work.
"The picture presents the facts in logical space, the existence and
non-existence of atomic facts" renders *Sachlage* as "the facts" — a
fourth distinct English rendering after "state of affairs" (2.0121,
2.014) and "circumstances" (2.0122), the inconsistency day 19 already
flagged in this same word. This one is the most costly of the four: an
English reader meets "the picture presents the facts" and hears an
echo of "fact" from chapter 1, not of *Sachlage*'s own careful,
three-thesis buildup as something merely *possible*. The German column
beside it still carries the load the translation drops.

## Zen

Meinong's theory of objects (*Über Gegenstandstheorie*, 1904) separates
*Sein* — being, existence — from *Sosein* — so-being, having
determinate properties. His golden mountain does not exist, but it is
still, determinately, golden and a mountain; its Sosein holds
regardless of its Sein. That separation is structurally close to what
2.11 does with *Sachlage*: a situation has fully determinate content —
this atomic fact holding, that one not — independently of whether it is
the situation reality actually instantiates. Content and obtaining come
apart in both.

The disanalogy is where each system draws its outer boundary. Meinong's
pure objects subsist "beyond being and non-being" (*Aussersein*) without
restriction, and famously without regard for logical possibility at
all — his round square has determinate Sosein too, round and square
both, and Russell's 1905 attack on exactly this feature was written by
the man who would go on to introduce this book. Wittgenstein leaves no
room for that move. 2.0121 already ruled it out in so many words:
"Etwas Logisches kann nicht nur-möglich sein" — nothing logical can be
merely possible, since logic treats of every possibility and all
possibilities are already its facts. A *Sachlage*, however unrealized,
is not a free object bearing whatever properties get assigned to it
from outside; it is one arrangement already licensed by objects' own
fixed combinatorial form (2.0123-2.014), a position already staked out
inside the one logical space 1.13 opened. There is no Meinongian third
realm here, no *Aussersein* for impossible situations to occupy —
logical space, complete since 1.13, already contains every genuine
possibility and structurally excludes the rest.
