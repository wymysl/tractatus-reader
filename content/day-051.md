## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume, das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten vor." The picture presents the Sachlage
in logical space — the existence and non-existence of atomic facts.
Yesterday's sentence, 2.1, only announced that picturing happens, and
that it is something we do. 2.11 is the first sentence to say what a
picture actually presents, and it says it by way of an appositive: "die
Sachlage im logischen Raume" is not one thing and "das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten" a second, added alongside it. The comma
glosses the first phrase with the second. And the second phrase is not
new vocabulary. It is 2.06's own sentence, word for word: "Das Bestehen
und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten ist die Wirklichkeit" — the
existence and non-existence of atomic facts is reality. Without using
the word *Wirklichkeit* once, 2.11 has just said that a picture presents
reality. That identification is not yet asserted outright — 2.12 will
do that explicitly, "Das Bild ist ein Modell der Wirklichkeit," the
picture is a model of reality — but 2.11 has already smuggled in
everything 2.12 needs, one sentence early, inside an appositive clause
easy to read past.

"Im logischen Raume" is doing similarly quiet work. Logical space was
named exactly once before now, all the way back at 1.13: "Die Tatsachen
im logischen Raum sind die Welt" — the facts in logical space are the
world. Forty-nine sentences separate that first use from this second
one, and in between, logical space was built out, tested, and leaned on
repeatedly without ever being renamed (days 5 through 50 all use the
term without requiring a new one). 2.11 places the picture in exactly
that space — the same space the world itself was said to occupy at
1.13 — and has it present exactly what reality was said to consist of
at 2.06. One clause, two forty-and-more-sentence threads pulled
together: the picture is located where the world is, and it presents
what reality is. That is the whole of what a picture is *of*, stated
before the word "model" or "represents a possible state of affairs"
(2.202, not yet reached) ever appears.

One correction owed to yesterday: day 50's forward glance called this
sentence "a picture presents a possible state of affairs in logical
space." That description belongs to 2.202, not to 2.11. 2.11 has no
"possible" and no indefinite article — it is *die* Sachlage, not *eine
mögliche* Sachlage, and it is glossed immediately as the unqualified
existence-and-non-existence formula of 2.06, not softened into one
possibility among others. Worth being exact about now that the sentence
is actually in front of us, since 2.202 will make the qualified,
"possible" version of this claim in its own right and the two should
not be blurred together three sentences before 2.202 exists.

Ogden's translation earns one flag here too, checked by grepping every
other occurrence of *Sachlage* across the whole book. Everywhere else it
is rendered "state of affairs" (2.0121, 2.014, 2.202, 2.203, 3.02, 3.11,
3.144, 3.21, 4.021, 4.03, 4.031, 4.032, 4.04, 4.124, 4.125, 4.462,
5.135, 5.525), once "circumstances" (2.0122). Only here, at 2.11, does
Ogden render it "facts" — the one place in the entire translation where
*Sachlage* and *Tatsache* ("fact") collapse into the same English word,
three words before *Sachverhalte* ("atomic facts") appears in the same
sentence. The German keeps three distinct technical nouns in view at
once — *Sachlage*, *Sachverhalte*, and the *Tatsachen* of 1.13 sitting
one clause behind them in memory — exactly where the English gives the
reader one word, "facts," doing service for two of the three. The
appositive still does its job either way, since 2.11 immediately glosses
Sachlage in terms of Sachverhalte regardless of which English word
covers it, but a reader working from Ogden alone loses, right here, the
visual cue that this is the same careful three-way distinction the
German has been keeping since chapter 1.

No interpretive-war paragraph today. 2.1 already stated, in full, where
the traditional and resolute readings part ways over the picture-theory
apparatus this sentence belongs to; 2.11 adds no new fork of its own; it
consolidates vocabulary already on the table (logical space, the
existence/non-existence formula, reality) rather than taking a new
position either camp would read differently. Restating yesterday's fork
against a sentence that does not itself turn on it would manufacture
disagreement where the text gives none.
