## Explanation

Yesterday's thesis, 2.1, said pictures exist and that we make them — but
it stopped short of saying what a picture actually presents. 2.11
answers, in one clause with two parts: a picture presents "die Sachlage
im logischen Raume" (a situation in logical space), which the sentence
then restates as "das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten" —
the existence and non-existence of atomic facts.

That restating clause is not new vocabulary. It is a direct quotation of
2.06's own defining formula. 2.06, five sentences and five days ago,
said: "Das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten ist die
Wirklichkeit" — the existence and non-existence of atomic facts is the
reality. Word for word, the same German clause that named reality
itself now names what a picture presents. Checked directly against both
entries in `data/tractatus.json` side by side, not from memory: the
match is exact, not a paraphrase that happens to sound similar.

That repetition is not incidental. A picture's whole purpose, a few
sentences ahead, will be to agree or disagree with reality — to be
capable of being true or false (2.21 announces this; 2.222 will make it
official). For a picture to be capable of matching or failing to match
reality at all, what it presents has to be built from the same material
reality is built from: a pattern of atomic facts holding and failing to
hold. 2.11 does not yet say whether a given picture's pattern is the
real one. It only establishes that a picture's content has the identical
shape as 2.06's reality — the same currency, so that agreement or
disagreement between them is even something that could later be asked.

This is also why the word has to be *Sachlage* rather than *Sachverhalt*
or *Tatsache*. *Sachverhalt*, the atomic fact, is too small: 2.11 needs
many of them, held together in one presented pattern, not a single unit.
*Tatsache* is too committed: thesis 1 named it "was der Fall ist" — what
is the case — an actual holding, built into the word from the start. A
picture must be able to present a pattern that does not hold — a false
picture is still a picture — so the word for what it presents cannot
already mean "actual." *Sachlage* carries neither restriction: a
situation, a candidate pattern, entertainable in logical space whether
or not it happens to be the one pattern that is real.

None of this is a new word today. *Sachlage* surfaced twice early
(2.0121, 2.0122) and once more at 2.014, where day 19's method note
flagged, without developing it, that "*Sachlage* is what a picture will
turn out to represent, several theses from now (2.11, 2.202)." 2.11 is
that debt, paid on schedule.

Ogden's translation on this page obscures exactly the precision above.
It renders *Sachlage* as "facts" here — a third English word for a term
day 19 already caught rendered two different ways ("state of affairs" at
2.0121/2.014, "circumstances" at 2.0122) — and this time the collision
is worse: "facts" is also Ogden's own word for *Tatsache*, the different,
already-established, already-actual-committed term from thesis 1 and
2.04–2.063. A reader following only the English column, arriving here
straight from six days spent on *Tatsache* and *Wirklichkeit*, will read
"the picture presents the facts" and reasonably assume the same word
means the same thing it just meant. The German says otherwise: a
different noun, chosen precisely because it does not carry the
commitment "facts" carries in English no less than *Tatsache* does in
German.

"In logical space" closes the sentence by returning to 1.13, which first
placed facts inside a fixed space of possibility rather than let them
float free (day 5), and whose own method note named this sentence by
number as one of the places the phrase would recur. A picture's
presented situation is not a free-floating pattern; it takes its place
inside the very space 1.13 already asserted the world's own facts sit
inside — one more thread from chapter 1 that chapter 2's picture
apparatus is now visibly built from, not a fresh start.

## Method

Two forward debts closed today, both checked against the source text
rather than trusted at a distance. Day 19's method note named this exact
sentence, by number, as where *Sachlage* would surface as what a picture
presents; day 5's method note named this exact sentence, also by number,
as one of the places "logical space" would recur after 1.13. Grepped
`data/tractatus.json` for both before writing, rather than assuming a
32- and 46-day-old forecast still held — both did.

New finding, not previously on record: 2.11's defining clause, "das
Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten," is not a fresh
formulation but a verbatim repetition of 2.06's own definition of
reality. Confirmed by pulling both entries from `data/tractatus.json`
side by side rather than trusting memory of the German — the clauses
match to the word. This is the strongest available evidence that a
picture's presented content is built to be commensurable with reality
specifically, and it grounds the explanation's central claim in the text
rather than leaving it asserted.

Revisited day 19's declined concordance tool, since today is
*Sachlage*'s fourth appearance and the one day 19 forecast would confirm
the term as central rather than a one-off. Declined again, on a sharper
ground than day 19's own: the problem a concordance would solve —
noticing when Ogden's English hides a recurring German term — is solved
today, as it was on day 19, by one paragraph of prose placed exactly
where it matters. A standing UI feature would run on every day, the
overwhelming majority of which touch no such translation seam, against
this project's presentation ethic of a mostly empty page. Four
occurrences in fifty-one days does not yet clear that bar; the tripwire
stays open, not resolved.

Separately, and not something either forecast flagged: `data/tractatus.json`
carries 2.11's English as "...the existence and non-ex\-is\-tence of
atomic facts" — a literal `\-`, TeX's discretionary-hyphen mark from the
Gutenberg source, left over from justified typesetting this site never
does. `site/app.js` inserts statement text straight into the DOM, so the
mark was rendering as a visible backslash rather than vanishing as
intended. Grepped the full data file: 37 of 526 entries carry the same
artifact, including two already-published, already-deployed days (13,
for 2.0233; 45, for 2.05) — a real, live rendering bug, not a
hypothetical one, and one today's own thesis text happens to trigger
directly. Fixed at the presentation layer, not the source: added
`stripDiscretionaryHyphens` to `build.mjs`, a pure function stripping the
literal `\-` sequence, applied to every statement's `en`/`de` text as it
is written into each day's unit JSON and into `tree.json`.
`data/tractatus.json` itself is untouched, per the immutability
guardrail — the fix runs at build time, on the generated output only.
Covered by a new unit test on the function directly and a new
build-level test confirming the artifact is gone from both emitted
files. `npm test` (32 passing, up from 30) and `node build.mjs` both
green before committing. This changes the rendered text of days 13 and
45 without touching their `content/` files — flagged here per METHOD.md's
confession rule for revising the past, since it is a change to how two
prior days display, not a change to what they say.
