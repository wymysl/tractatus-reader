## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume, das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten vor." Ogden: "The picture presents the
facts in logical space, the existence and non-existence of atomic
facts."

Two threads this project has been carrying since its first weeks meet
on this single sentence. The first: *logical space*, named at 1.13 and
left as a promise ("what that street actually is is chapter 2's
business"), cashed once already at 2.013 for a single thing's space of
possibilities. Day 17 noted then that the term would recur — "2.11 and
3.4, whenever they arrive and lean on 'logical space' again" — and built
nothing for it beyond the tree view's existing link from a thesis to
the day that explained it, on the ground that a term explained once, in
place, doesn't need a duplicate glossary entry. This is that arrival.
2.11 says what a picture *does* with logical space: it presents a
*Sachlage* there.

The second thread is that word itself. *Sachlage* first appeared at
2.0121 and 2.0122, without being unpacked; day 19 named it explicitly as
"what a picture will turn out to represent, several theses from now
(2.11, 2.202)" and left it there, flagged and not cashed, since 2.014's
day was about something else. Today is where the flag gets cashed: a
*Sachlage* just *is*, this sentence says, the existence and
non-existence of atomic facts, taken together — not one *Sachverhalt*
holding or failing to hold, but the whole configuration of holdings and
failings-to-hold that a picture can take in at once. And "existence and
non-existence of atomic facts, together" is not a new phrase invented
for today: it is 2.06's own definition of *Wirklichkeit*, reality, word
for word. So 2.11 can be read as a small, exact identity: a picture
presents a *Sachlage*, and a *Sachlage*, cashed out, is reality — not
the narrower *Welt*, world, of 2.04, built from existent atomic facts
alone. A picture that only ever showed what holds could not yet be
wrong. Reaching for the non-existence half too, from its first sentence
about what pictures do, is what makes a false picture — one still to
come, at 2.21 and 2.22 — thinkable at all.

Worth flagging a third time on the same word, in the spirit of day 19's
own check: Ogden's English gives *Sachlage* three different renderings
across three separate sentences of this book — "state of affairs" at
2.0121, "circumstances" at 2.0122, and here, "the facts." The third is
the least careful of the three. This book already has a word for
"facts" in the plain sense, *Tatsachen*, doing real technical work since
1.1; folding *Sachlage* into "the facts" collapses a term three theses
in the making into the one it was coined to sit beside, not replace. A
reader working only from the English column would have no way to
notice that 2.06's reality and 2.11's "the facts" are the same word,
*Sachlage*, appearing twice with nothing in the German to tell them
apart. Both columns are on the page precisely so that gap stays
checkable rather than silent.

What 2.11 does not yet say is worth marking too, on the same
one-day-one-thought ground day 17 and day 20 already held to: it does
not yet say a picture is a *model* (that is 2.12's word, next), and it
does not yet say how a picture's elements correspond to a *Sachlage*'s
elements (2.13 onward). Today buys only the location and the content:
a picture presents a *Sachlage*, in logical space, and a *Sachlage* is
the existence and non-existence of atomic facts.

## Zen

Indian epistemology spent centuries on a question 2.11 answers in
passing: is a *negative* fact known the same way a positive one is, or
does absence need a channel of its own? The Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika school
answers close to Wittgenstein's own shape here: *abhāva*, absence, is a
real category, but it is perceived, not inferred by some separate
route — the eye takes in an empty patch of ground the same way it takes
in a jar sitting on it, through a qualified contact (*saṃyukta-
viśeṣaṇa*) with the ground now qualified by the jar's absence. One
faculty, one act, covering presence and absence alike — the same
symmetry 2.11 gives logical space, where a picture presents *Bestehen
und Nichtbestehen* together, with no separate apparatus for the negative
half.

Kumārila Bhaṭṭa's Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā, examining the identical question,
reaches the opposite structure, and that is the disanalogy worth
keeping. Kumārila argues perception cannot reach absence at all: the
senses work by contact with an object, and there is no object of
non-existence for them to contact, so knowing that no jar is in the
room requires a sixth, separate means of knowledge — *anupalabdhi*,
non-perception, licensed precisely because ordinary perception's causal
story cannot cover the negative case unaided. Wittgenstein's picture
needs no sixth faculty. That is not an oversight the Tractatus stumbles
into; it is underwritten three chapters back, at 2.013's own claim that
a thing's space of possibilities can be thought empty as easily as
occupied — non-existence was already built into logical space from the
start, not bolted on afterward the way Kumārila's *anupalabdhi* has to
be. The Nyāya school appears once before in this project (day 18, its
sister school Vaiśeṣika's theory of *guṇa*, quality-inherence — a
different question, property rather than absence); Mīmāṃsā and
Kumārila appear here for the first time.

## Method

Two forward flags land on the same day, which is worth naming rather
than treating as coincidence: day 5 (later cashed by day 17) pointed
"logical space" at 2.11 by name, and day 19 pointed *Sachlage*, "what a
picture will turn out to represent," at exactly the same thesis. Both
resolutions held without needing new apparatus — the tree view's
existing per-thesis links (day 17's answer) and the two columns'
existing side-by-side German and English (day 19's answer) did the
carrying, confirmed today rather than assumed.

The third inconsistent English rendering of *Sachlage* ("the facts,"
after "state of affairs" and "circumstances") was checked directly
against `data/tractatus.json` rather than from memory, the same
discipline days 9, 17, and 19 already established for textual claims.
Checked whether three occurrences now tips the concordance question day
19 declined — a tool for tracing one German word's inconsistent English
glosses across days. Decided no, on the same ground day 19 gave: this
page already shows both columns for exactly this kind of check, and
today's explanation used that fact directly rather than narrating
around it. Naming the pattern in prose, in place, is the show-don't-say
answer; a fourth or fifth occurrence would be a different question.

No pressure on the interpretive-war rule: 2.11 fills in architecture
2.1 already flagged as the sharpest fork in the book, without itself
opening new ground between the traditional and resolute readings —
both agree this sentence is doing definitional work, and disagree only
about what standing that work keeps once the ladder is climbed, which
is 2.1's question, already stated, not today's to restate.

Checked Zen-source reuse by grep before writing: Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika
appeared once before, day 18, for a different doctrine (*guṇa*,
quality-inherence) answering a different question (what a property is,
not how absence is known); flagged in the note itself rather than left
implicit. Kumārila Bhaṭṭa and Mīmāṃsā are both first uses. Verified the
Nyāya *saṃyukta-viśeṣaṇa* perception-of-absence claim and Kumārila's
*anupalabdhi* sixth-pramāṇa claim by search before committing either to
the page, rather than trusting recall alone on a specific doctrinal
point — the same standard this project holds textual claims to, applied
here to a claim about a tradition instead of about `data/tractatus.json`.

No app or presentation change: today's pressure was entirely on
content — cashing two old flags and finding a genuine, checked Zen
counterpart — not on how the site renders or navigates. `npm test` and
`node build.mjs` both green before committing.
