## Explanation

"Das Bild stellt die Sachlage im logischen Raume, das Bestehen und
Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten vor." Read the German closely and it names
four things this project has already met, one at a time, and now bolts
together: *Bild*, picture, from yesterday (2.1); *Sachlage*, a word already
used three times (2.0121, 2.0122, 2.014) but never yet explained;
*logischer Raum*, logical space, from 1.13, where the facts that are the
world were first located; and, at the very end, a clause copied word for
word from 2.06 — "das Bestehen und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten," the
existence and non-existence of atomic facts, which 2.06 used to *define*
reality itself. 2.11 is not a new idea. It is an assembly instruction,
built entirely from parts already on the table.

What it says, put together: a picture presents a *Sachlage* — a state of
affairs, a specific way things might stand — and it does this by locating,
in logical space, the existence and non-existence of atomic facts. Not
reality in general (2.06's *Wirklichkeit*, everything that exists and
doesn't), but one determinate configuration of it. A picture is local. It
does not picture the world; it pictures a way the world's atomic facts
could be arranged, true or false, checkable against reality by comparing
this one presented arrangement to the actual one.

The word choice deserves a flag here, because Ogden's translation quietly
erases it. This project's data uses "state of affairs" for *Sachlage* at
2.0121 and 2.014 (and "circumstances" at 2.0122 — Ogden is not even
consistent with himself there). But at 2.11 the same word, *Sachlage*,
becomes "the facts" — the identical English word already spent on
*Tatsache* at 1.13 ("The facts in logical space are the world"). Three
distinct German technical terms are now in play — *Tatsache* (fact),
*Sachverhalt* (atomic fact), *Sachlage* (state of affairs) — and Ogden's
"the facts... of atomic facts" collapses two of them into one English
word, in the very sentence that is supposed to introduce what a picture,
as opposed to reality, actually presents. A reader working only from the
English would have no way to notice that *Sachlage* is a third term at
all, let alone that the sentence closes by reusing 2.06's exact defining
clause for reality. The German repetition is the whole point: a picture
presents the same kind of thing reality is made of (Sachverhalte,
existing or not), just not all of it, and not necessarily matching. That
argument survives in German by literal repetition and is invisible in
English by literal collision.

Nothing here yet says a picture must resemble what it presents, or how its
elements manage to stand for anything — that is 2.12 through 2.15's job.
2.11 only fixes the target: not facts as such, but a *Sachlage*, and not
loose facts but specifically the existence and non-existence of atomic
facts, in logical space, exactly where 1.13 said the world's own facts
already are.

## Method

Three already-published days — 28 (2.0231), 30 (2.0233), 45 (2.05) — carry
a rendering bug that today's thesis makes impossible to ignore, because it
hits the main statement text itself rather than a quotation deep in an
explanation. The source data marks optional print-hyphenation points with
a literal `\-` (`atom\-ic`, `non-ex\-is\-tence`): TeX's discretionary
hyphen, meaning "break the word here if the typeset line needs it,
otherwise run it together." `site/app.js` writes `st.en` and `st.de`
straight into `innerHTML` with no such line-breaking ever happening, so
the backslash-hyphen sequences have been rendering as literal `atom\-ic`,
`prop\-er\-ties` on the live site since whichever day first hit them.
2.11's own English text carries three occurrences in one clause,
`non-ex\-is\-tence`, which would otherwise land as a visibly broken
version of the sentence this explanation spends its opening paragraph
treating as a precise, deliberate piece of text — an argument about close
reading undermined by a typo the source never actually contains.

Fixed in `build.mjs`: a `stripSoftHyphens` function removes `\-`
sequences from every statement's `de` and `en` text at load time, before
they reach `tree.json` or any `units/day-NNN.json` — one point of
correction feeding every consumer, rather than patching `app.js` in two
places (the statement block and the tree view both read the same fields).
`data/tractatus.json` itself is untouched, per the immutability guardrail;
only how this project *displays* its text changes. Covered by two new
tests in `tests/build.test.mjs`: a direct unit test of the function, and
an end-to-end build test confirming a `\-` in fixture source data comes
out clean in both `dist/units/day-001.json` and `dist/tree.json`.
`npm test` and `node build.mjs` both green before committing.

This is a revision of past presentation, confessed per METHOD.md: days 28,
30, and 45 will now render their English (and, for day 28 and 30, German)
statement text correctly for the first time since publication. No
`content/` or `journal/` file changes — the fix touches only the build
pipeline between the immutable source data and the generated site.

No further METHOD.md change: 2.11's own explanation needed no new rule,
only close attention already licensed by "show, don't say" and the
existing practice (day 50) of checking Ogden's word choices against the
German rather than assuming the English is transparent. No Zen note today
— nothing found that would survive being doubted rather than merely
sounding apt: the obvious reach, a picture as a finger pointing at the
moon, fails on the same asymmetry 2.1's Vasubandhu note already used up
yesterday for a related idea, and repeating it one day later against a
sentence that is really about *Sachlage* versus *Sachverhalt*, not about
representation as such, would be forcing a connection rather than finding
one.
