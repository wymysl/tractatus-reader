## Explanation

"Die Gesamtheit der bestehenden Sachverhalte bestimmt auch, welche
Sachverhalte nicht bestehen." The totality of existent atomic facts also
determines which atomic facts do not exist.

This is the same move 2.04 made yesterday, run one sentence further into
chapter 1's opening totality run. 2.04 took 1.1 — "the world is the
totality of facts" — and substituted thesis 2's identification of a fact
with the existence of atomic facts, getting "the world is the totality
of existent atomic facts." 1.12 said something 1.1 alone didn't: "the
totality of facts determines both what is the case, and also all that
is not the case." Make the same substitution in 1.12 that 2.04 already
made in 1.1, and 1.12 becomes 2.05 — word for word, register shifted
from *fact* to *atomic fact*, from *the case* to *exist*.

Nothing here is new content dressed up in new vocabulary. It is 1.12
itself, and 1.12's content was already explained in full on day 4: a
genuinely complete positive list settles absence for free, the way a
guest list marked "everyone who has arrived" tells you who is missing
without a separate word being said about them. That explanation does not
need repeating; only the level has changed. 1.12 worked with facts in
general, before chapter 2 had said what a fact was. 2.05 works with
atomic facts specifically, now that thesis 2 has cashed out what "fact"
meant, and 2.04 has cashed out what "totality of facts" meant. The
guest-list logic needs completeness to run — that is 1.11's contribution,
"and by these being *all* the facts" — but the sentence that actually
states the determines-absence result, the one 2.05 is transposing, is
1.12, not 1.11.

What 2.05 buys, restated at this more basic level, is a foothold 2.06
needs and does not yet have: a name for the two sides of this
determination. 1.12 spoke of what is and is not "the case," staying at
the level of the proposition. 2.05 speaks of atomic facts that exist and
atomic facts that do not — and existence and non-existence, plural and
paired like this, are exactly the two things 2.06 is about to fold
together into a single word chapter 1 never needed: *Wirklichkeit*,
reality. Today secures the pairing at the level where 2.06 will name it.
Nothing about *how* totality fixes absence has changed since day 4;
what has changed is that the fixing now happens to the very entities
2.06 is one sentence away from re-baptizing as positive and negative
facts.

## Zen

"有無相生" — being and non-being produce each other — Tao Te Ching,
chapter 2. Laozi puts existence and non-existence in the same breath
2.05 does, and treats their relation as the sentence's whole content
rather than a side remark, which is 2.05's shape as well: not "there is
existence, and, separately, non-existence," but one determining the
other directly.

The direction is where they part. *Xiang sheng* is mutual: being and
non-being arise together, neither one first, and Laozi elsewhere (chapter
11) makes non-being the more useful half — the hollow of the hub, not
the spokes, is what lets the wheel turn. 2.05 has no such reciprocity.
The totality of what *exists* does all the determining; what does not
exist is settled afterward, without residue, as whatever the complete
positive list left out — day 4's guest list again, one level down.
Non-existence contributes nothing back to fixing existence here; it is
pure output, never input. A different question from day 4's, which asked
whether 1.12 tolerates a third value between yes and no (Joshu's *mu*
said no). Today's question is whether the two values that remain stand
as equals or as ground and consequence. Wittgenstein's totality is a
strict hierarchy dressed as a pair.
