## Explanation

"Das Feste, das Bestehende und der Gegenstand sind Eins." The fixed, the
existent and the object are one. Three nouns, one sentence, no argument
attached — 2.027 does not defend an identity, it simply states one.

Both of the other two words already have a job in this book. "The
fixed" is 2.026's word, from yesterday: only if there are objects can
there be *a fixed form of the world*. "The existent" is 2.024's word:
substance is *what exists* independently of what is the case — besteht,
the same verb. Read on their own, both sentences could suggest that
fixity and existence are properties objects merely carry, features
substance happens to have, sitting one level above the objects
themselves. 2.027 closes that gap. It is not that objects *have* a fixed
form, or *have* independent existence, as a thing has a property
distinct from itself. The fixed, the existent, and the object are the
same thing named three ways. Take "the object" out of that identity and
there is nothing left for "the fixed" or "the existent" to refer to;
they name it, not something behind it.

That is also the third predicate this project has been tracking since
day 9 flagged all three in advance: object is simple (2.02), object is
substance (2.021), and now — in the strongest form the run has taken,
an outright identity rather than a further description — object is the
fixed, the existent (2.027). Nothing about the object has changed
between those three sentences. Each is the same claim seen from a
different angle: nothing to disassemble further (simple), nothing it
rests on outside itself (substance), no gap between what it is and that
it is (fixed, existent). Three words for one thing, arriving fifteen
days apart, is exactly why 2.024 could define substance days before
2.027 ever named it directly — the definition and the identity were
always going to meet here.

One grammatical note the German risks losing in translation, worth
being exact about, especially the day after a Zen note about a
philosopher who argued for exactly one undivided thing. "Sind Eins" —
*are one* — takes a plural subject and stays plural; German has no
trouble saying that several things are, each of them, one with something
else. 2.027 does not collapse every object into a single Parmenidean
whole. It says, of each object, that it and its fixity and its existence
are one — as many "ones" as there are objects. 2.0271, next, needs
exactly this plural reading to work: it will call the object "the
fixed, the existent" and set the *configuration* of objects against it
as "the changing, the variable." A single collapsed One would leave
nothing over to configure.

## Zen

Aquinas holds that essence and existence are really distinct in every
creature and identical in exactly one case. What a thing is (its
essence) and that it is (its existence) come apart for anything made:
existence must be received into an essence the way an act is received
into a potency, which is why a creature can be fully understood — its
whatness grasped — without yet knowing whether it exists (*De Ente et
Essentia*, ch. 4; *Summa Theologiae* I, q.3, a.4). Only God is *ipsum
esse subsistens*, subsistent existence itself: in God alone, essence and
existence are not two components brought together but one and the same,
with no distinction left to draw. That identity is the whole content of
divine simplicity, and its force depends entirely on its being unique —
the one metaphysical fact that marks God off from everything else,
every one of which is composite by comparison.

2.027 asserts the identical-looking collapse — nothing distinguishes an
object's fixity, its existence, and the object itself — but strips out
exactly the feature that gives Aquinas's version its weight. Wittgenstein's
identity is not a privilege reserved for one being against a world of
composites; it is the baseline condition of every object there is, with
no exception standing outside it to make it remarkable. Where Aquinas's
God is unique because essence and existence coincide in him and nowhere
else, 2.027's objects would be unremarkable if a single one of them
failed to show the identity — the whole point of substance, since 2.021,
is that this holds across the board. Same predicate-collapse, opposite
distribution: one case doing metaphysical work by being singular, versus
every case doing it by being uniform.
