## Explanation

1.2 said the world divides into facts. 1.21 says what that division is
worth: "Any one can either be the case or not be the case, and everything
else remain the same." Take away 1.2 and this sentence has nothing to
stand on — you can only flip one piece without disturbing the rest if the
pieces were separable to begin with. 1.21 is the payoff 1.2 was building
toward.

It is worth sitting with how strong a claim this actually is. Not "some
facts are independent of others" — *any one*, *all the rest*, exactly as
they were. Say the cup is on the table. Say, separately, that it is
raining in Vienna. On 1.21's picture, the cup could slide off the table
and the rain in Vienna would not so much as flicker in response — not
because Vienna is far away, but because no fact, however placed, can
by itself force another fact to hold or fail. Nothing underwrites
anything else. This is not a claim about physical causation (a cup
falling has plenty of causes) — it is a claim about *logical* dependence,
about whether one fact's holding could, all on its own, entail or rule
out another's. Wittgenstein's answer is: never, for atomic facts. Each
one stands or falls purely on its own account.

This is also the moment the book quietly picks a side in an old dispute.
The British idealists Wittgenstein's teachers had reacted against —
Bradley chief among them — held that no fact is really separable from
the whole: relations run all the way through, so nothing is what it is
except in virtue of everything else. Russell and Moore's revolt insisted
relations are external, add-ons between things that remain what they are
regardless. 1.21 is that revolt stated as flatly as it can be stated: not
just that some relations are external, but that *every* atomic fact is
its own closed island. Nothing so far in the book has been this
committal.

## Zen

Buddhist dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) says the reverse of
1.21, and says it about everything without exception: nothing holds or
fails to hold on its own account, because nothing has an account of its
own to begin with. Every arising is conditioned by, and conditions, what
surrounds it — pull one thread and there is no fixed remainder that
"stays exactly as it was," because staying-the-same was never available
to anything in this picture. 1.21 needs a world where facts can be
independently flipped, each with its own separate switch. Dependent
origination denies that any such switches exist, because it denies that
anything is separable enough to have one.

This is the same Buddhist concept the day 5 note used for logical space,
and the one day 6 considered and set aside as forced on a thesis too
thin to earn it. It belongs here instead. Day 5 asked whether a fixed
container of possibility stands behind the facts; day 6's candidate
asked whether "dividing into pieces" resembles dependent arising, and
found the resemblance backwards on a claim that was still only about
*shape*. 1.21 is the thesis actually built for this contrast: it is
the strong, specific claim — total mutual independence of atomic facts —
that dependent origination was always going to deny most directly.
Kept, because this is where it fits, not because it was due.

## Method

Checked the reuse before writing it down. Dependent origination has now
grounded a Zen note on day 5, been tried and rejected on day 6, and
appears again here. Three appearances in three consecutive days'
worth of drafting would be the reflexive move day 5's own method note
warned against — except only two of the three actually ran (day 6's was
written, tested against the doubt test, and cut). The concept keeps
coming up because this stretch of the book (1.2 through 1.21) keeps
asking the same question — does the world come apart into separable
pieces? — from different angles, and Buddhist metaphysics has one answer
to all of them. That is a fact about the territory, not a sign the map
is being reused lazily. Still, this is very likely the last use of this
concept for a while; if 2.061's independence-of-atomic-facts restates
1.21 without adding anything new, the Zen section that day should be
omitted rather than reach for this same well a fourth time.

No new technical term today — "independent," "external relation," and
similar stay in plain prose, not a glossary pair. No change to `site/`
or `build.mjs`: still a single-thesis day inside the branch 1.2 already
opened, same rendering as every day since day 2.
