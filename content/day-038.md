## Explanation

"Die Konfiguration der Gegenstände bildet den Sachverhalt." The
configuration of the objects forms the atomic fact.

This sentence has been owed since day 9. 2.01 said an atomic fact is "a
combination of objects" — using, deliberately, an ordinary word,
because the book had not yet earned a technical one. It took the rest
of chapter 2's opening run to earn it: *Form* at 2.0141 (an object's
fixed range of possible partners), *Konfiguration* named outright for
the first time at 2.0231 (day 28, objects actually combined, not
merely combinable), and yesterday's 2.0271, which set configuration
against object as the changing against the fixed. 2.0272 is where all
of that vocabulary finally gets spent on the very sentence it was built
to replace. Read side by side, 2.01 and 2.0272 say the same thing:
objects, combined, are what an atomic fact is. 2.0272 says it with the
one word chapter 2 spent twenty-nine days earning the right to use.

The direction is worth noticing, not just the vocabulary. 2.01 named
the atomic fact first and defined it downward, into objects. 2.0272
starts from configuration and builds upward: configuration *forms*
(*bildet*) the atomic fact, the way a verb builds a sentence rather
than a sentence being taken apart into a verb. Nothing new is claimed
by the reversal — it is the same identity read in the other direction —
but the direction matters for what comes next. Yesterday's thesis
already ruled out configuration as a further fact standing behind or
above the objects; it is nothing but the objects, arranged. Today says
what that arrangement, so understood, amounts to: not a diagram beside
the atomic fact, but the atomic fact itself, under a different name.
There is no gap for anything to fall into between "objects configured"
and "atomic fact obtaining" — 2.0272 closes it by identity, not by
argument.

One thing 2.0272 does not say, and 2.01 already ruled out saying: that
any assortment of objects, merely co-present, counts. Day 9 flagged
this on 2.01's own unfinished business — "a heap is not a combination"
— and 2.0272 inherits the restriction along with the vocabulary. Which
arrangements are genuine configurations, and what holds their objects
together when they are, is not this sentence's job either. That is
2.03's, next: objects in an atomic fact "hang one in another, like the
members of a chain" — a structure, not a pile.

## Zen

Composition as Identity, defended in different forms by Donald Baxter
and David Lewis (*Parts of Classes*, 1991, §3.6), makes a structurally
close claim: a whole just *is* its parts, taken together — not a further
thing caused by them or built on top of them, but the same portion of
reality counted once instead of many times. On this view there is
nothing more to a fusion than the things fused; asking what binds them
into one is, on the strongest versions of the thesis, already asking
the wrong question, since the many were never numerically distinct from
the one to begin with. 2.0272 draws an identity of the same shape: the
atomic fact is not built *from* the configured objects as an extra
layer added on top, it *is* them, configured — Composition as Identity
for exactly this case, some seventy years before the position had a
name and a defender, back when Wittgenstein simply had to state it
himself.

The disanalogy is where classical mereology and 2.01 already parted
ways, and it survives into today's sentence unchanged. Composition as
Identity is standardly paired with unrestricted composition: any
objects whatever, however scattered — a shoelace, the number nine, the
reader's left thumb — compose some fusion, and that fusion is
indifferent to how its parts are arranged. A chair in pieces on the
floor and the same chair assembled are, mereologically, the identical
sum; arrangement makes no difference to which whole you get, only to
whether it looks like anything. 2.0272 will not tolerate that
indifference for a moment. Day 9 already ruled out a mere heap of
objects as a Sachverhalt, and 2.03, tomorrow's likely unit, makes the
positive case explicit: the objects have to hang together a particular
way, "like the members of a chain," for a configuration to obtain at
all. Composition as Identity gets its whole from the parts alone,
arrangement optional. 2.0272 gets its atomic fact from the parts
arranged just so, arrangement doing all the work identity alone cannot.

## Method

2.01's own unfinished business — what a combination is, as opposed to a
heap, flagged on day 9 and left for later sentences to take up — closes
today, not by finally explaining what makes a combination a combination
(that is still 2.03's job) but by naming what chapter 2 has, in the
meantime, built as its answer: *configuration*, defined at 2.0231 as
objects actually combined against *Form*'s bare possibility of
combination. Checked that this counts as 2.01's debt actually being
paid rather than merely gestured at: the explanation above states the
identity 2.01 and 2.0272 share explicitly, side by side, rather than
letting the reader notice the echo unassisted — which is the same
prose-over-apparatus move day 35's 30-day payoff and day 36's
three-predicate convergence both used for debts of this shape. No new
apparatus needed; a single paragraph carries a two-term identity better
than any glossary entry would.

Checked METHOD's interpretive-war rule with more care than a reflex
skip, because today's content sits nearer 2.01's original divergence
than 2.021's. The streak running since 2.0211 (seven days, through
yesterday's 2.0271) tracks a specific angle located at 2.021: object as
*substance*, a metaphysical posit real for the traditional reading, a
rung for the resolute one. 2.0272 is not that content — it restates
2.01's claim (objects combine into atomic facts) in vocabulary earned
since, and 2.01's own divergence, stated on day 9, is about whether
that combination-claim is a real discovery about the world or a piece
of grammar performed and due to be discarded by 6.54. Checked whether
new vocabulary reopens it as a fresh angle, the way day 23 asked the
same question of 2.021 relative to 2.01, and got the same answer day
21 got for 2.02: no. Restating an already-stated claim in a new word
does not change what kind of claim the traditional and resolute
readings disagree about; it changes only the word. Declined, per the
"no new divergence beyond 2.01's statement of it" precedent day 23
named directly — this is that precedent's second use, and it resets
the streak's locus to 2.01 rather than extending the 2.021 count, since
today's decline rests on a different located divergence than yesterday's
did.

Zen-source diversity checked against the last five days (34: Kant, 35:
Parmenides, 36: Aquinas, 37: Plato) — Composition as Identity is clear
of the window and a first use for this project. Considered a second
look at Ontic Structural Realism, used for 2.0231's closely related
configuration claim (day 28, well outside the five-day window but
close in subject matter): declined it on the merits rather than the
window, since OSR's point there was about objects losing qualitative
content to relations, not about parts-and-whole identity, which is
2.0272's actual shape. Considered Gestalt psychology's "the whole is
other than the sum of its parts" as the more obvious first candidate
for a composition question and rejected it for stating the opposite of
today's thesis — 2.0272 asserts the atomic fact is nothing *but* the
configured objects, no gestalt surplus — which would have made a
contrast note stronger than a resonance note and mislabeled the section.

No app or presentation change. 2.0272 states its identity in vocabulary
already glossed on introduction (*configuration* at day 28, *Form* at
2.0141, *Sachverhalt* at day 8); the single-thesis layout has now
carried three separate multi-week vocabulary payoffs (day 35, day 36,
today) without needing a glossary page for any of them. No destruction
argument owed.
