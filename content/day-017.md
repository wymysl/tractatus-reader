## Explanation

1.13 named logical space and did nothing more: "the facts in logical
space are the world," a lit window needs a whole dark street of unlit
ones around it, and what that street actually is was left as chapter
2's business, starting here. 2.013 is where the promise gets paid, and
it starts small — not the world's space, but a single thing's: "every
thing is, as it were, in a space of possible atomic facts."

The hedge is worth pausing on, because it is not filler. "As it were"
(*gleichsam*) is the same word 2.0121 already used ("it would, *so to
speak*, appear as an accident") and the word 2.0131 will use again for
the coloured speck. Three sentences running mark themselves as figures
of speech rather than literal claims. "Space" here is not a further
place things sit in, the way objects sit in a room; it is a picture for
something 2.012 through 2.0124 already said without the word. An
object's nature already prejudges every atomic fact it could occur in
(2.012); once every object is given, thereby is the whole space of
possible atomic facts given too (2.0124). 2.013 takes that same fact
about a single thing and gives it a shape a reader can hold in mind: the
thing sits in the middle of its own possibilities, the way a point sits
in the middle of a region.

The second sentence is the sentence actually doing new work, and it is
an asymmetry, not a description: "I can think of this space as empty,
but not of the thing without the space." Emptying the space costs
nothing to imagine — picture none of a thing's possible atomic facts
obtaining, nothing yet the case for it, pure unrealized possibility.
That is just 2.0124's actual/possible distinction restated: which
possibilities are actual is the world's business, not logic's, and
logic is perfectly content with none of them landing. But run the
subtraction the other way and thought stops: a thing with no space of
possibilities around it at all is not a thing minus a feature, it is
nothing describable. 2.0121 already said this in plainer words — a
thing that could exist alone, in itself, with a state of affairs fitted
onto it only afterward, is not what a Tractarian object is. 2.013 gives
that same unthinkability a picture instead of an argument: subtract the
space, and there is no longer anything left to be in it.

2.0131, next, pushes the picture further — into an actual spatial object
needing infinite space, and a coloured speck needing some colour or
other — and earns its own day rather than borrowing this one.

## Zen

Jain metaphysics gives this asymmetry an unexpectedly exact structural
match. Umāsvāti's *Tattvārtha Sūtra* counts space (*ākāśa*) among the
universe's basic substances (*dravya*), with one function: giving room
(*avagāha*) for the other substances to exist in at all. The tradition
then draws the same empty/occupied line 2.013 draws: *loka-ākāśa* is the
finite region that actually holds the world's substances, while
*aloka-ākāśa*, the infinite space beyond it, holds nothing — genuinely,
totally empty, and perfectly thinkable as such. What is not available to
think, on this account, is a substance with no space to occupy; every
soul and every particle of matter has its place-points (*pradeśa*) as
part of what it is to exist at all. Space empty, easily conceived;
substance spaceless, not conceivable — 2.013's own asymmetry, in a
different vocabulary and a different millennium.

The disanalogy is about what kind of thing "space" is once each system
has made its move. Jain *ākāśa* is a real substance in its own right,
one of six, existing independently and prior to whatever comes to
occupy the occupied region of it — the universe's things are placed
into a container that would exist, empty, even if nothing else did.
Wittgenstein's space is not a further entity alongside objects for them
to be placed into; 2.0124 already ruled that out by making the space of
possible atomic facts nothing over and above what all the objects
already are, once they are given. Jain space could, in principle, stand
alone as *aloka-ākāśa* stands alone, empty of everything else forever.
Tractarian space has no such standing of its own to fall back on — take
away the objects and there is no space left over that was never theirs
to begin with.

A Buddhist-sounding move tempted itself here too, on the strength of one
word: "empty" inviting *śūnyatā*, emptiness as the absence of any fixed,
independent nature in things. Held against the doubt test, it does not
survive first contact — *śūnyatā* denies things a fixed nature at all;
2.013's empty space is the opposite claim, an object's space of
possibilities staying fixed and available (2.012, 2.0124) whether or not
anything in it happens to be actual. Noted and set aside rather than
forced.

## Method

Directly answers a five-day-old flag. Day 5's method note held off on a
persistent glossary for "logical space," on the ground that the term
would recur for the rest of the book (2.013, 2.11, 3.4 onward) and was
therefore heavier than fact/thing — watching, not acting. 2.013 is the
thesis day 5 itself pointed to as where the term gets properly cashed
out, and having now written that explanation, the honest resolution is
that no glossary was ever the right tool: `renderTree()` in
`site/app.js` already links every thesis in the tree view straight to
the day that explained it (`#day/${d}`), so 2.11 and 3.4, whenever they
arrive and lean on "logical space" again, can point a reader back to
this day directly rather than needing a standing definition duplicated
in a widget. Confirmed this by reading the function rather than
assuming it, the same standard day 9's tree-depth finding was held to.
Explaining a term once, in place, and letting the site's existing
navigation carry a reader back to it, is the show-don't-say version of
what a glossary would otherwise do by narration — no site change made,
because none was needed.

Checked a non-Western candidate seriously before settling on one, given
day 16's soft flag that Spinoza, Aristotle, and Leibniz made three
Western Zen days running without an equally deliberate look elsewhere.
The Jain *ākāśa* doctrine is not a consolation candidate reached for to
break a streak; it is a genuinely close structural match, checked
against the doubt test and kept for a disanalogy that does real work
(substance-in-its-own-right versus nothing-over-and-above-the-objects).
Also checked and rejected, on the same page, the more obvious
word-level temptation — "empty" toward Buddhist *śūnyatā* — which did
not survive the doubt test, the same shape as day 4's *mu* rejection.
Two candidates checked, one kept, one rejected with the reason stated,
rather than the first plausible echo taken at face value.

No pressure on the interpretive-war rule, for the reason days 9 through
16 gave: 2.013 continues the same internal-properties architecture,
recast spatially, without newly dividing the traditional and resolute
readings.

Checked the site for a presentation question raised by 2.013
specifically: its raw HTML in `data/tractatus.json` is a single `<p>`
block, the plainest shape, already rendered correctly by
`site/style.css`'s `.statement .en p` rule. Worth remarking, not
changing: the page's own presentation ethic — one thesis, generous
empty margin, nothing else on the page — already pictures what 2.013
says, a thing surrounded by mostly-empty space. That is a resonance
worth noting in passing, not a design decision this thesis forced; the
layout predates today and was not built to illustrate this sentence.
No destruction argument owed, no app change made.
