## Explanation

2.013 said every thing sits in a space of possible atomic facts, and
hedged the claim twice with "as it were" (*gleichsam*) — a figure, not a
description. 2.0131 is where the figure gets paid out in examples, and
it reaches for the same hedge a third time (*sozusagen*, "so to speak"),
exactly as day 17 flagged it would: "a speck in a visual field need not
be red, but it must have a colour; it has, so to speak, a colour space
round it." A tone must have some pitch. The object of touch must have
some hardness. None of these has to be any particular value — red,
middle C, rough — but none can lack a value in its own range entirely.
That is 2.013's "space of possible atomic facts," made concrete: not one
space but a different one for each kind of thing, sized to what that
kind of thing is.

The opening sentence does the same move for the plainest case first: "a
spatial object must lie in infinite space." Not some room, not the
distance to the next object — infinite space, because a spatial thing's
space of possible positions has no edge built into it by logic. The
parenthesis that follows is easy to read past and worth pausing on
instead: "a point in space is a place for an argument" (*eine
Argumentstelle*). That word belongs to Frege's vocabulary for functions,
not geometry — a function has argument-places waiting to be filled, and
here a bare point in space is being described the same way, a slot
logic has prepared before anything has been put into it. The book will
build its picture theory out of exactly this idea, objects as
argument-places a proposition fills; 2.0131 plants the word early and
moves on, one day's thought at a time.

Read the three sensory cases against each other and a pattern
sharpens: colour, pitch, and hardness are not properties an object
happens to carry, the way a coat happens to be blue. They are the
built-in shape of a kind of object's possibility space — sight-things
come with a colour-slot, sound-things with a pitch-slot, touch-things
with a hardness-slot, and the slot is filled before there is anything
describable to ask about. Nothing here yet says which colour, which
pitch — that is 2.014's business next, and today stops at the shape of
the space rather than what occupies it.

## Zen

The Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika school's theory of *guṇa* (quality) draws close to
2.0131's own structure, close enough to check hard against the doubt
test. Vaiśeṣika sorts the world into substances (*dravya*) and the
qualities that inhere in them (*samavāya*), and ties each substance-kind
to a fixed roster of quality-kinds it cannot lack: earth (*pṛthvī*)
always carries some smell, some taste, some colour, some touch; water
always carries some taste, some colour, some touch, but never smell
natively; sound belongs to ether (*ākāśa*) alone. A colour-bearing
substance is never colourless the way a blue coat could have been red —
color as a quality-kind is simply not optional for the substances that
have it at all, only which colour is. That is 2.0131's speck and its
colour-space, in a different idiom nine centuries earlier: given the
kind, some determinate of the relevant quality is not a further fact
about the thing, it is part of what having that kind of quality-slot
already means.

The disanalogy sits in what each system is doing with the claim.
Vaiśeṣika's roster is metaphysics: a specific, closed inventory of which
qualities the world's substance-kinds in fact carry, discovered and
counted (earth has four, water three, air one), open to being got wrong
the way any empirical taxonomy can be got wrong. 2.0131 commits to no
such inventory — Wittgenstein is not telling a reader which objects the
world contains or what qualities they carry, only that whatever an
object's kind turns out to be, its space of possibilities will have this
shape, some value present, no value forced. The Tractatus explicitly
leaves that inventory for application rather than logic to supply
(2.0233, 2.025 and on); Vaiśeṣika hands the inventory over as the
doctrine's own content. Same structural insistence — no colour-slot ever
sits empty — aimed at two different kinds of question.

A same-tradition candidate was checked and set aside before this one:
W. E. Johnson's determinable/determinate distinction (*Logic*, 1921) is
an even closer verbal match — colour a determinable, red a determinate —
but it is Wittgenstein's own philosophical neighborhood restating his
point in cognate vocabulary, not a different tradition meeting it from
outside. It would not survive being doubted by someone who knows both;
there would be nothing to doubt, only a relabeling.

## Method

Second Indian-sourced note running (Jain *ākāśa* yesterday, Vaiśeṣika
*guṇa* today), and worth naming plainly rather than letting it pass
quietly, per the habit day 16 and day 17 both practiced. The two are not
interchangeable padding for the same slot — Jain metaphysics is about
space as a substance things get placed into, Vaiśeṣika is about which
qualities a substance-kind cannot lack, and each was checked separately
against today's and yesterday's thesis rather than one being reused for
convenience. Still, two Indian schools back to back is the same shape of
pattern day 16 flagged for Western sources: a soft watch, not a rule.
One more Indian-sourced day without a deliberate look elsewhere first
would be the point to treat it the way day 10 eventually treated
Buddhist-family overuse.

17 gave: 2.0131 stays inside the same internal-properties architecture,
now spelled out in sensory examples, without newly dividing the
