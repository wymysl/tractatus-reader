## Explanation

"Die Struktur der Tatsache besteht aus den Strukturen der Sachverhalte."
The structure of the fact consists of the structures of the atomic
facts.

Thesis 2 (day 8) is the sentence this one has been quietly owed since:
"Was der Fall ist, die Tatsache, ist das Bestehen von Sachverhalten" —
what is the case, the fact, is the *Bestehen* (existence, obtaining) of
atomic facts. Thirty-five days and a whole vocabulary later — *Form*
(2.0141), *Konfiguration* (2.0231), *Struktur* (2.032), the connection
between them (2.033) — 2.034 is the first sentence since day 8 to put
*Tatsache* and *Sachverhalt* back in the same sentence. It answers, for
structure, the question thesis 2 answered for existence: what does a
fact, built from more than one atomic fact, actually get from them?

The answer is carried by a pun the English cannot keep. Thesis 2 used
*Bestehen* as a noun: an atomic fact's obtaining, on or off, the
technical sense fixed on day 8. 2.034 uses the same verb root
differently — *besteht aus*, "consists of," "is made up of." One German
word, two grammatical lives: bare *bestehen* names a Sachverhalt's
actuality (this is also 2.06's word, two sentences ahead: "Das Bestehen
und Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten ist die Wirklichkeit," reality is
atomic facts' obtaining and non-obtaining); *bestehen aus* names a
whole's composition from parts. Ogden's "existence" and "consists of"
are unrelated English words for what Wittgenstein reaches for with one
root twice. The German keeps visible what the translation quietly
drops: what thesis 2 says an atomic fact *does* (obtain) is exactly the
material 2.034 says a fact's structure is *made of*, once several such
obtainings are collected into one Tatsache.

The verb choice also marks a real shift from the last new-vocabulary
sentence, 2.0272 (day 38): "Die Konfiguration der Gegenstände bildet
den Sachverhalt" — configuration *bildet*, forms, an atomic fact, an
identity claim, no gap between the configured objects and the atomic
fact they are. 2.034 does not say the fact's structure *is* the
Sachverhalte; it says it *besteht aus* them — consists of, built from,
explicitly partitive language, appropriate exactly because thesis 2
already established that a Tatsache can rest on *one or more* atomic
facts. Identity fits a single Sachverhalt becoming one Struktur; only
composition fits several Strukturen becoming one.

What 2.034 does not say is how several structures become one. It names
the material (the Strukturen of the constituent Sachverhalte) without
saying whether anything besides listing them is needed — whether a
fact's Struktur is their plain sum, or needs some further combining the
sentence leaves silent. That question stays open here on purpose; later
sentences on the composition of facts out of atomic facts have room to
take it up without this one reaching for it early.

## Zen

The compositionality principle in logical semantics — that the meaning
of a complex expression is a function of the meanings of its parts and
the way they are combined — is the standard label for an idea read out
of Frege's semantics of sense (*Über Sinn und Bedeutung*, 1892) and
sharpened into that exact slogan only later, by logicians reconstructing
his method (the formulation is commonly traced through Tarski's and
Montague's formal semantics, not Frege's own words). Given
Wittgenstein's direct debt to Frege's logic — already on this site's
record twice, at *Argumentstelle* (day 18) and the function-slot
borrowed for *Form* (day 33) — the shape is close enough to be worth
naming outright: a whole's relevant property (meaning, there; structure,
here) is not a further fact discoverable on its own, but is built up
from the same property in the parts.

Where the parallel gets genuinely uncertain, rather than cleanly broken,
is the clause compositionality insists on and 2.034's wording leaves
out. Frege's principle needs two ingredients, not one — the parts'
meanings *and* their mode of combination; "dog bites man" and "man
bites dog" share every part-meaning and differ only in how the parts
are combined, which is why the clause cannot be dropped. 2.034 states
only the first half: Struktur besteht aus den Strukturen. Whether a
second, unstated clause about how those structures combine is implicit
— perhaps already absorbed, since 2.032 already made *Struktur* mean
the manner of hanging-together rather than a separate ingredient beside
it — or whether 2.034 is, on this point, closer to a bare sum than
Frege's principle would allow, is not a question this sentence answers.
Flagged as open rather than resolved either way; forcing a verdict now
would claim more than the German text on the page actually gives.

## Method

Today's finding is philological before it is philosophical: *bestehen*
(obtain) and *besteht aus* (consist of) share a root in the German that
Ogden's English severs entirely, and thesis 2 and 2.034 turn out to be
the same word doing two different jobs, thirty-five days apart. This is
now the second time a translation choice has been flagged as losing a
connection the German keeps (day 40 flagged Ogden's "are combined" for
the reflexive *verhalten sich... zueinander*, quietly admitting an
assembler the German never posits). Considered whether two occurrences
make this a pattern worth a formal check, the way interpretive-war
locus and Zen-source diversity get one, and declined: METHOD's existing
"show, don't say" and "as simple as possible" rules already produce
this note whenever the German genuinely offers something Ogden's
English doesn't; a standing per-day translation-fidelity check would
risk manufacturing flags on days with nothing honest to report, the
same failure mode "silence over strain" warns against for Zen notes.
Two organic occurrences are not yet a rule's worth of evidence. No
METHOD.md change.

Zen-source choice: Frege's compositionality principle is a first use in
this section — Frege has appeared twice in Explanation prose, for
notation and vocabulary (days 18, 33), never before as a Zen-note
comparison. Checked diversity against the last five days (38:
Composition as Identity, 39: Great Chain of Being, 40: Zhu Xi, 41:
Saussure, 42: Aristotle, third pass): clear.

Considered reusing Composition as Identity outright, since day 38's
note is the project's most recent composition-shaped comparison and
sits only five days back. Declined on the merits, not the window: CAI's
claim is identity, no gap between whole and parts, which is the shape
2.0272's *bildet* took (day 38 used it there for exactly that reason).
2.034's *besteht aus* is explicitly partitive, and thesis 2's "one or
more" leaves room for a genuine plurality collapsing into one Struktur
— a different logical shape than CAI's, which is why Frege's
two-ingredient principle, with its live question about a missing
combination clause, fit today's open question better than re-running
day 38's identity claim would have.

Checked the interpretive-war rule and declined it on the same grounds
day 42 gave for 2.033: 2.034 is not on 2.01's locus (whether objects
hanging together is discovery or grammar) — it is ground-floor
composition, connecting *Tatsache* and *Struktur* the way 2.033
connected *Form* and *Struktur*. The locus shift day 42 flagged from
2.03–2.032's four-day streak continues to hold; no new divergence to
report.

No app or presentation change. No new technical term — *Tatsache*,
*Sachverhalt*, and *Struktur* are all already glossed on first use, and
today's explanation carries the *bestehen*/*besteht aus* connection in
a single paragraph without needing to double back and re-explain either
term, the same test day 8's tripwire set for when a glossary becomes
necessary. Not yet. No destruction argument owed.
