## Explanation

"Die Struktur der Tatsache besteht aus den Strukturen der Sachverhalte."
The structure of the fact consists of the structures of the atomic
facts.

This sentence closes two threads at once, one four days old and one
thirty-five. The near thread: 2.03–2.033 spent four days building
*Struktur* from the ground up — the chain image (2.03), determinacy
(2.031), the name itself (2.032), and yesterday's link back to *Form*
(2.033) — all of it pitched at the level of a single atomic fact. 2.034
is the first sentence to take that finished vocabulary and move it up a
floor. The far thread: day 8 named the fact/atomic-fact distinction
that chapter 2 opened with — "what is the case, the fact, is the
existence of atomic facts" — and promised nothing about how the two
relate *structurally*, only that a fact is what you get when atomic
facts obtain. 2.034 is where that promise comes due. A Tatsache can be
the existence of several Sachverhalte at once (2's own wording left
that plural open); today's sentence says what unites them into one
structure rather than a mere list: the fact's structure just *is* the
structures of its atomic facts, taken together.

Read the German once more with *besteht aus* in view — "consists of."
Thesis 2 named the same root differently: "ist das Bestehen von
Sachverhalten," the fact *is* the obtaining, the noun form of the same
verb, of atomic facts. 2.034 takes that root and turns it from a noun
of existence into a verb of composition — *besteht aus* rather than
*das Bestehen von* — doing parts-and-whole work where 2 did identity
work. Nothing is added on top of the atomic facts' own structures to
make the fact's structure: no further fact of combination, no separate
glue-structure holding the Sachverhalte
together the way 2.03 needed a chain image to hold objects together
within one Sachverhalt. That question — what makes objects a
combination rather than a heap — was already answered, once, at the
lower level (2.03–2.032). 2.034 does not re-ask it one level up. It
says the upper-level structure is simply built from what the lower
level already supplies, with nothing extra required to cross the
seam.

That is also why 2.034 is the right sentence to end this stretch on.
The next thesis, 2.04, drops the word *Struktur* entirely and reaches
for the widest term chapter 2 has: not a fact's structure, but "the
world" — the totality of existent atomic facts. 2.034 is the last stop
before that totalizing move, and it earns the move by showing structure
itself already composes cleanly across the fact/atomic-fact seam.
Nothing stands in the way of taking the same step once more, from all
facts' structures to the world.

## Zen

C. D. Broad's *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* (1925, ch. 2) names
the shape of 2.034's claim exactly, using Mill's older distinction
between homopathic and heteropathic laws (*A System of Logic*, 1843,
III.vi) as its raw material. A property of a whole is *resultant* when
it is nothing over and above the properties of the parts, taken
together — deducible, in principle, from a full description of the
parts and how they are arranged, with nothing left over to explain.
Broad set this against the *emergent*: water's behavior, on the classic
textbook illustration he and Mill both reached for, was supposed to
outrun anything predictable from hydrogen and oxygen described in
isolation, however completely. 2.034 asserts, for structure
specifically, that the fact/atomic-fact case is always the resultant
kind. The fact's structure is deducible from — is nothing but — the
atomic facts' structures, taken together. No residue, no surplus, no
heteropathic law needed to get from one level to the other.

The disanalogy is where Broad's framework stops fitting. Resultant and
emergent were meant, for Broad, to sort *empirical* cases: some
composite properties turn out resultant on investigation, some turn out
emergent, and which is which is not settled from the armchair — it
takes the science. 2.034 offers no such sorting. It does not survey
facts and report that this one's structure resulted while that one's
emerged; it states, as a matter of what "fact" and "atomic fact"
*mean*, that the resultant relation always holds, with no logical room
for a Tractarian counter-case the way water was Broad's counter-case to
naive mechanism. Broad's distinction earns its keep by admitting both
outcomes. Wittgenstein's sentence rules one of them out before any
investigation could begin.
