## Explanation

The reductio that ran 2.02 through 2.0212 is over, and 2.022 does not
continue it. It opens something else: "Es ist offenbar, dass auch eine
von der wirklichen noch so verschieden gedachte Welt Etwas—eine
Form—mit der wirklichen gemein haben muss." It is clear that however
different from the real one an imagined world may be, it must have
something—a form—in common with the real world. Where 2.0211–2.0212
asked what breaks if substance is denied, 2.022 takes substance as
secured and asks a new question: what constrains an *imagined* world,
one that is not the real one at all?

Notice the shape of the sentence before its content. "Etwas—eine Form"
does not name the answer outright; it reaches for it in two steps,
something first, then the precise word for that something. The
something is not being discovered by argument here — no premises, no
"therefore" — it is being noticed as obvious ("es ist offenbar"), the
kind of claim Wittgenstein expects the reader to see once it is pointed
out rather than to be walked to by inference. That fits the sentence's
place: not a new leg of demonstration but a return to plain assertion,
the register 2.02 itself used ("the object is simple," no argument
attached until 2.021 supplied one after the fact).

"However different... may be" grants imagination everything: replace
every object, rearrange every fact, and the sentence still holds. What
it will not grant is total freedom — some *something* survives every
variation, on pain of the variation no longer counting as a world at
all, imagined or real. That "on pain of" is not stated here; 2.022
only claims the constraint exists, not why crossing it would be fatal.
It does not have to explain why: 2.0212 already showed what a world
without a shared, determinate form to picture would cost — no picture
at all, true or false. 2.022 assumes that result and asks what any
picturable alternative must still carry over.

The word doing the naming, *Form*, is not new to this project. 2.0141
coined it for a single object — the form of an object is the fixed
range of ways it can occur in atomic facts. 2.022 uses the same word
one level up: not the form of an object but a form shared by whole
worlds. Day 20 flagged this scaling-up as coming "in the next few
pages," citing 2.023, 2.026, and 2.033 — worth a small correction now
that it has arrived: the word actually resurfaces here, at 2.022, one
thesis earlier than that forward note named. What this shared form
consists in — objects, the same ones the real world is built from — is
not this sentence's business. 2.023 says it outright, tomorrow.

## Zen

Leibniz's doctrine of possible worlds, worked out across the
*Discourse on Metaphysics* and the *Theodicy*, starts from a structurally
similar move: every possible world, including every one God did not
choose to create, is bound by the same eternal truths — the laws of
logic and mathematics hold across all of them without exception, the
one thing no possible world can vary. 2.022's form is that same kind of
invariant: whatever an imagined world changes, something survives the
change and ties it back to the actual one.

The disanalogy sits in what selects the actual world from the space of
possible ones, and in what the shared element is doing there. For
Leibniz the eternal truths are necessary but not sufficient: they fix
what any possible world *could* look like, and then God's choice,
governed by the principle of sufficient reason, picks the best one out
of infinitely many logically equal candidates — actuality is an
evaluative achievement layered on top of the shared logical space.
Wittgenstein's 2.022 has no chooser and no evaluation. The form is not
a menu constraint that a further criterion narrows down to one world;
it belongs to the objects themselves, the same objects the real world
already has, and there is no second step in which something *decides*
which arrangement of them is real. One tradition needs a best-of
selection after the shared structure is fixed; the other needs nothing
beyond the structure and the objects that carry it.

## Method

Checked METHOD's interpretive-war rule fresh, per the trigger day 25
set rather than the general reminder to check every day: 2.022 changes
the argument's frame (imagined worlds sharing form with the real one)
rather than continuing the reductio 2.021's divergence note already
covered, so this is a genuine new occasion, not a repeat. The
divergence is real. The traditional reading takes 2.022 as a
substantive modal claim: a fact about what any possible world, however
imagined, would have to contain — real metaphysics of possibility, not
decoration. The resolute reading takes the same sentence as further
ladder-construction, apparatus that looks like a claim about all
possible worlds but is being built so the reader can climb past it and
see, by 6.54, that no such claim was ever really assertable. Both
readings parse "however different... may be" the same way; they differ
on whether the sentence is doing metaphysics or performing it. Stated
once, briefly, no verdict, per METHOD's own instruction.

Applied the bundling test to the 2.022/2.023 pair before deciding
against bundling. 2.022 states a complete claim with its own warrant
("es ist offenbar") — some form is shared, full stop — and does not
need 2.023's answer to what the form is in order to mean something on
its own. That is the same test the 2.02–2.0212 arc passed at every
joint (day 25's note); this is a fifth correct application in a row of
the same rule to consecutive, tightly related theses, worth naming
again as it continues to hold up rather than treating it as settled
