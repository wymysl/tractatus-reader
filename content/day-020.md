## Explanation

2.0141 gives a name to something this page has already described four
times over without naming it. 2.0123 and 2.0124 said that knowing an
object means knowing everything about how it can occur in atomic facts;
2.013 pictured that fixed range of occurrence as a space the object
sits in; 2.014 said the same range holds the possibility of every state
of affairs the object could be part of. 2.0141 stops adding new content
and instead performs an act of naming: "Die Möglichkeit seines
Vorkommens in Sachverhalten, ist die Form des Gegenstandes" — the
possibility of its occurrence in atomic facts is the *form* (*Form*) of
the object. Nothing here that was not already true on the last four
theses. What changes is that it now has one word.

That word is worth pausing on, because this is the first time it is
used this way and it will not be the last — *Form* recurs some eighty
times before the book is through, most of it far from here. In the next
few pages it becomes the form of the world's substance (2.023, 2.026,
2.033); a chapter later it becomes the *form of depicting* (*Form der
Abbildung*, from 2.15 on), the thing a picture and the reality it
depicts must share for the picture to depict at all; by 2.18 it is
*logical form*, and by 4.12 the book will argue that a proposition can
show its own form but never state it. None of that is 2.0141's business
today — one day, one thought — but it is worth knowing, on the day the
word is coined for objects, that the same word is about to become load-
bearing for nearly everything the book does with pictures and
propositions. A reader who remembers this coinage will recognize the
later uses as the same idea moved up a floor: form was always a fixed
range of combinatorial possibility, first for a single object among
atomic facts, later for a picture among the realities it could depict.

## Method

No Zen note today, the second running (after day 19). The reason is the
same reason as yesterday, arrived at the same way: checked the actual
content of 2.0141 before reaching for a comparison, and found a naming
act, not a metaphysical claim. There is a name obviously waiting in the
wings — Aristotelian hylomorphism, matter and form — and it was checked
and rejected rather than silently skipped. The rejection matters enough
to record: Aristotle's *form* (*eidos*, *morphē*) is an intrinsic
principle of what a thing essentially is, the reason a lump of bronze
counts as a statue or a body counts as an organism of some kind.
Wittgenstein's *Form* here is nothing intrinsic at all — it is a purely
relational, combinatorial fact about which other objects a given object
can occur alongside in a fact, with no claim about what the object
essentially *is*. The two doctrines share an English (and, translated
back, a German) word and answer entirely different questions. Reaching
for the comparison anyway would build a Zen note on a translator's
shared gloss rather than on the actual thought in either text — thinner
ground than day 18's rejected W.E. Johnson candidate, which at least
shared Wittgenstein's own problem and vocabulary. Silence, per METHOD.md,
rather than a note built on a coincidence of translation.

No pressure on the interpretive-war rule today, but a real one is now
visible ahead rather than behind: the traditional and resolute readings
diverge sharply over *logical form* once 4.121–4.1212 argue that form
can be shown but never said, a claim resolute readers take to undercut
any doctrine of form as a further sayable fact and traditional readers
take at closer to face value. 2.0141 does not yet carry that weight — it
is a plain definition, nothing here claims form is unsayable — but it is
the thesis where the word those later chapters will fight over first
enters the book, and today is the natural place to flag that the fight
is coming rather than pretend it arrives from nowhere at 4.12.

No app or presentation change. Considered, and did not build, a
concordance for *Form* itself — the same question day 19 raised for
*Sachlage* and declined for the same reason: three occurrences (2.0122,
2.014's neighbourhood, now 2.0141) is not yet a pattern a reader needs
tooling to see, and the tree view already lets a reader trace a concept
back to the day that introduced it. But *Form* is a different scale of
case than *Sachlage* was — eighty occurrences ahead, not three, and a
genuinely central technical term rather than an incidental one. Recording
a concrete trigger rather than a vague watch: once the picture-theory
block (roughly 2.15–2.22) is reached, where *Form* appears in nearly
every thesis, that is the point to actually reconsider whether the site
owes the reader some way to trace the word across days — not before,
since there is nothing yet to trace.
