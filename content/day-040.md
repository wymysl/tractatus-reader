## Explanation

"Im Sachverhalt verhalten sich die Gegenstände in bestimmter Art und
Weise zueinander." In the atomic fact the objects are combined in a
definite way.

Yesterday gave the picture and stopped short of it on purpose: objects
in a *Sachverhalt* hang into one another like the links of a chain,
rather than sitting side by side like stones in a heap. But a chain
does not say which links go with which, or how — only that they
interlock rather than merely touch. 2.031 closes that gap by one notch.
It is not enough for the objects to hang together somehow; they hang
together in *this* way and no other. *Bestimmt* — determinate, definite,
fixed — is the word doing the work, and it rules out the one
possibility 2.03's picture on its own left open: a fact whose objects
interlock, but loosely, ambiguously, indeterminately, the way two
puzzle pieces might be jammed together several different ways. There is
no jamming here. For a given atomic fact, there is exactly one way its
objects are combined — that is what makes it *this* fact and not
another built from the same objects differently arranged.

The German is worth pausing on, because Ogden's "are combined" quietly
turns something the objects do into something done to them. *Verhalten
sich... zueinander* is reflexive: the objects hold themselves, comport
themselves, toward one another — the grammatical subjects of their own
relating, not raw material a combiner acts on from outside. Nothing in
the German suggests an assembler; the definite way of hanging together
is simply how the objects are, once they are hanging together at all.
"Combined" is not wrong, but it lets an image slip in — parts fastened
by something else — that the reflexive verb was built to keep out.

The word *bestimmt* is also being spent carefully, and it will not stay
this quiet. Two propositions from now, "the way in which objects hang
together" gets a name — *Struktur*, 2.032, tomorrow's business, not
today's. And much later, in the discussion of sense and the simple sign
(3.23), the same root returns as *Bestimmtheit des Sinnes*, the
determinacy of sense: the requirement that a proposition's sense be
fixed, not vague, on pain of not being a sense at all. 2.031 is where
that later requirement first shows its face, at the level of facts
rather than propositions — a demand that will not go away once we move
from what atomic facts are to what pictures of them can say. Today only
states it; it does not yet ask what would go wrong if combination were
indefinite, or how determinacy of fact underwrites determinacy of sense.
That is owed later, when the picture theory needs it.

## Zen

Neo-Confucian metaphysics has a doctrine built for exactly this kind of
claim. Zhu Xi's *li yi fen shu* (理一分殊, "principle is one, its
divisions many") holds that every particular thing has its own *li* —
not a vague general pattern but a specific, determinate one, this
thing's own manner of holding together, not some other thing's. A given
bamboo stalk's li is not "plant-shaped in general"; it is the definite
pattern that makes this stalk grow as it does and no other way. That
much lines up with 2.031 cleanly: both insist that a thing's internal
coherence, once it obtains, obtains in one determinate manner, not an
approximate or interchangeable one.

The disanalogy is not small, though, and it is worth stating rather
than skating past. Zhu Xi's *li* is one thing wearing many faces — the
single, undivided Supreme Principle (*taiji*) differentiating itself
into each particular li the way moonlight differentiates into its
reflection on ten thousand rivers, still one moon. It is also
normative: a thing's li is what it ought to do or be, correctness and
value built into the pattern itself, most sharply in human nature,
where li is the good the sage recovers. Wittgenstein's "definite way"
carries no such freight. Each atomic fact's determinate combination is
its own affair, answerable to no single principle threading through
every other fact — the world (2.04) is a totality of independent facts,
not divisions of one underlying unity — and there is nothing better or
worse about combining one way rather than another: only that, in fact,
it is this way. Where Zhu Xi's many determinate patterns are local
faces of one moon, Wittgenstein's are just many moons.
