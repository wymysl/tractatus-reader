## Explanation

The "1" branch is finished — seven sentences (1, 1.1, 1.11, 1.12, 1.13,
1.2, 1.21), and they never once said what a fact actually *is*. They used
the word from the first line and leaned on it harder with each step:
facts, not things (1.1); all of them, nothing left over (1.11–1.12);
sitting in a space (1.13); each one switchable on its own (1.21). Thesis
2 is the first sentence to look back and answer the question the whole
branch had been quietly asking: what, exactly, is a fact?

"What is the case, the fact, is the existence of atomic facts." Fact
(*Tatsache*) turns out not to be the ground floor after all. It is built
from something smaller: the atomic fact (*Sachverhalt*, in the Ogden
translation used here) — a fact is not a new kind of thing, it is what
you get when one or more atomic facts *obtain*. "Existence" here is a
technical, narrow use: not the existence of objects (chairs exist, full
stop), but whether a particular possible combination is actually the
case — the same on/off Wittgenstein was already describing in 1.21,
now given its name. To say a fact holds is just to say: this atomic
fact, or these, obtain.

This is why 2 opens a new number rather than continuing 1.21. 1.21 said
atomic facts can be switched independently, without yet saying what an
atomic fact was, or how it relates to "fact" — the word chapter 1 had
been using all along. 2 supplies both answers at once, and in doing so
closes the loop: the world (1) is what is the case; what is the case
is facts (1.1); a fact is the existence of atomic facts (2). Chapter 2
will spend the next stretch — starting at 2.01 — saying what an atomic
fact itself is made of. That word has not been used yet. This sentence
only promises that it is coming.

## Zen

The Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma tradition drew a structurally similar line:
most of what we call things — a chariot, a self, a forest — are only
*prajñapti-sat*, real by designation, real the way a word is real. What
is *dravya-sat*, substantially real, are the dharmas: the irreducible
momentary constituents that composite things are built from. A chariot
is nothing over and above its wheels, axle, and frame arranged just so
— which is close to 2's own move: a fact is nothing over and above
atomic facts obtaining.

The disanalogy is exact enough to be worth stating rather than papering
over. A dharma is a momentary event — it arises and immediately ceases;
dharma theory is an account of flux, of things happening. An atomic
fact does neither. It does not arise or cease; it simply obtains or
fails to, a fixed possibility sitting in the logical space 1.13 already
named, timeless in a way no Abhidharma dharma is. Both traditions
refuse a middle-sized world of chariots and selves as the last word,
and both hand the real work to something smaller. But one smaller thing
is a flicker and the other is a standing fact about a static space of
possibilities. The resemblance is real; so is where it stops.

## Method

The tree page's `renderTree()` in `site/app.js` computed indentation by
counting dots in the thesis number, so `2` (no dot) already rendered at
depth 0, flush left, the same as `1` — no change was strictly needed for
correctness. But nothing marked the seam: chapter "1" is now a closed,
complete run of seven sentences, and the tree page gave no visual sign
of that boundary, only prose could have said it. Per METHOD's "show,
don't say," added a `chapter` class to any depth-0 row that isn't the
first, with a hairline rule and extra space above it in `site/style.css`
— the tree now shows the seven-fold structure the book itself imposes,
not just this session's explanation of it. Small, reversible, no new
dependency; `npm test` and `node build.mjs` both still pass.

Also revisiting day 2's tripwire, which flagged watching for a glossary
once technical pairs piled up "likely around the 2.0x statements." Today
adds one new term, atomic fact/*Sachverhalt*, alongside "existence" used
in its technical sense — still explainable in a sentence, still no
glossary needed. But 2.01, tomorrow's likely unit, introduces *object*
(*Gegenstand*), which would make three technical pairs active at once
(fact/thing, atomic fact/existence, object/configuration) inside a
single short run of sentences. If prose starts doubling back to remind
the reader what each term meant last time it was used, that is the day
to build the glossary day 2 predicted — not before, since inventing
apparatus ahead of the need it serves would violate "as simple as
possible" as surely as leaving it too late would.

Chose Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma over dependent origination for today's Zen
note on purpose. Day 7's method note already flagged three uses of
dependent origination in three days' worth of drafting and called this
theme's connection to it "very likely the last... for a while." 2's
actual content — one thing analyzed as built from smaller things — fits
Abhidharma's real/nominal distinction more precisely than it fits
dependent origination's denial of any fixed constituents at all, so the
switch is a better fit on the merits, not merely a rotation for its own
sake.
