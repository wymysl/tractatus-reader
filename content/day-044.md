## Explanation

"Die Gesamtheit der bestehenden Sachverhalte ist die Welt." The totality
of existent atomic facts is the world.

Chapter 1 said this three times without the vocabulary to say it exactly.
Day 1 opened with "the world is everything that is the case." Day 2
sharpened that into 1.1's totality claim — "the world is the totality of
facts, not of things" — and days 3 and 5 restated the same shape twice
more: "the facts... being *all* the facts" (1.11), "the facts in logical
space are the world" (1.13). Three sentences, one insistence: whatever
else is true, the world is however many facts there turn out to be, all
of them, no remainder. What chapter 1 never supplied is what a fact
*is*. That arrived on day 8, as thesis 2's own opening move: "what is the
case, the fact, is the existence of atomic facts" — *das Bestehen von
Sachverhalten*. Today's sentence is the substitution nobody had to
announce. Take 1.1's totality of facts; replace "fact" with what thesis 2
already said a fact is; the totality of facts becomes the totality of
existent atomic facts. Nothing new is asserted. Everything needed was
already on the page, four sentences and thirty-six days apart.

The vocabulary does the same substitution the logic does. Thesis 2 named
a fact using the noun-phrase *das Bestehen von Sachverhalten*, "the
obtaining of atomic facts." 2.04 uses the participle built from the same
verb, *bestehenden*, directly as an adjective on *Sachverhalte* itself:
not "atomic facts, and separately, their obtaining," but "the
existent-atomic-facts," one compound notion where thesis 2 needed a
noun phrase to say the same thing. The identity is mechanical at the
level of the word, not only the thought.

It is also the reason chapter 2 spent thirty-five sentences (2.01
through yesterday's 2.034) on what atomic facts, objects, form, and
structure are before circling back to say this. 2.04 could have followed
directly on thesis 2's heels, using nothing but 1.1 and 2 themselves, and
it would have been true — and empty. A reader who has not yet been told
what an atomic fact is, what makes a combination a combination rather
than a heap (2.03), what it is for one to be determinate (2.031) or
structured (2.032–2.034), gains nothing from being told the world is a
totality of them. Only now, with that content actually earned, does
"existent Sachverhalt" carry enough weight to redefine "world" with. 2.04
is less a new claim than a receipt: everything chapter 2 built between
thesis 2 and yesterday, cashed in at the top of the ladder it climbed
down from.

One more structural echo is worth flagging on the way out, since it
frames what is coming rather than what just closed. 1.1's totality claim
was not a single sentence; it took three (1.1, 1.11, 1.13) to state fully.
2.04 opens what looks like the same three-sentence pattern one register
down: 2.05 will say the totality of atomic facts also fixes what does
*not* obtain, echoing 1.11's "and by these being all the facts"; 2.06
will fold existence and non-existence together into "reality," a word
chapter 1 never needed. Today is the first beat of that run, not the
whole of it.

*Gesamtheit* — "totality" — deserves one flag before it recedes into
background vocabulary again, because it is not an ordinary predicate.
Nothing in the book yet says so explicitly, but the machinery it will
need is already visible from here: a genuine concept sorts some things
under it and others not, the way "red" sorts red things from the rest.
"Totality" does not obviously do that; it looks closer to what the book
will later call a formal concept (4.1272's "pseudo-concept object" is
the same family), better shown by a variable ranging over everything
than said by a further name standing for a further thing. If that is
right, "the world is the totality of Y" is not, on the book's own
eventual terms, a report about one more item in the world called the
totality — it is a rule for how "world"-talk works. The traditional
reading takes 2.04 as chapter 2's machinery finally cashed out in a
substantive result: a real totality, a real fact about what the world
consists of. The resolute reading takes the identical sentence as
scaffolding aimed at 6.4's "view of the world as a whole" — training the
reader to use "world" so precisely that, once the ladder is climbed, the
question "what does everything add up to" is retired rather than
answered. Day 9 opened a related fork about whether objects combining is
discovery or grammar; this is the same fork, reached for the first time
by the word carrying the most weight in the book's opening sentence, and
no more settled here than it was there.

## Zen

Democritus, fragment DK 68B9 (via Sextus Empiricus, *Adversus
Mathematicos* VII.135): "by convention sweet, by convention bitter, by
convention hot, by convention cold, by convention colour; but in reality
atoms and void." The fragment is a totality claim of exactly 2.04's
shape — everything that seems to be the world's furniture reduces,
without remainder, to the sum of the most basic units reality actually
has. It is not a coincidence that Ogden reached for "atomic fact" to
translate *Sachverhalt* at all; the word choice imports Democritus's own
vocabulary of indivisible ultimates into Wittgenstein's book, inviting
exactly this comparison two thousand years later.

The disanalogy is where thesis 1.1 already answered Democritus before
Democritus's name ever came up. His reduction bottoms out in atoms —
substances, things, bodies differentiated by shape and position (day
27's ground, at 2.023). But "the world is the totality of facts, not of
things" was chapter 1's opening refusal, made before atomic facts had
even been introduced. Sachverhalte are not a subtler kind of thing;
they are obtainings, combinations holding or failing to hold, and
2.04's totality is a totality of which combinations obtain, not an
inventory of what furniture exists. Democritus also needs a second,
equally real category for the reduction to work — the void, genuinely
existing alongside the atoms so they have room to move in. 2.04 needs no
second stuff-category: the space of atomic facts that do not obtain
(2.013, 2.0124) was never a further existent thing standing beside the
world, only unfilled possibility. One reduction ends in two kinds of
being. The other ends in one totalizing fact about which combinations,
among possibilities that need no further place to sit, happen to hold.

## Method

Framed 2.04 as a substitution closing the distance between two threads
that have been open since the book's first eight days: 1.1/1.11/1.13's
totality-of-facts claim, and thesis 2's identification of a fact with
the existence of atomic facts. Checked that this is genuinely what the
sentence does rather than an imposed reading — the German confirms it
at the word level, not just the logic: *bestehenden* (2.04) is the
participle of the same verb thesis 2 used as *das Bestehen von*, so the
substitution shows up in the morphology, not only the inference.
Declined to re-summarize 2.01–2.034's own content, which day 43 already
closed out in full; today only needed to note that the whole run is
what makes "existent Sachverhalt" non-empty by the time 2.04 reuses it.

Checked METHOD's interpretive-war rule and, for the first time since day
9 opened the combination-locus, located a genuinely different locus
rather than declining a repeat of the same one. The tracked thread
(2.01, 2.03, 2.031, and others since) has always asked whether some
specific worldly-sounding claim — objects combining, being determinate,
having structure — is discovery or grammar on the way to 6.54. 2.04
raises a related but distinct question, about the word "totality"
itself rather than about what it is a totality *of*: whether *Gesamtheit*
functions as a genuine concept or a formal one (anticipating 4.1272's
pseudo-concepts), and whether "the world is a totality of Y" survives as
a substantive claim once that distinction lands, or dissolves into
grammar aimed at retiring 6.4's "world as a whole" talk. Flagged both
readings in three sentences each, picked no winner, and logged this as a
second, separate locus alongside day 9's — worth tracking on its own
terms if 4.1272 or the 6.4s make it live again, rather than folding into
the combination-locus's running count.
