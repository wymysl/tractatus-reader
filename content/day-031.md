## Explanation

2.0233 bracketed external properties and held logical form fixed, then
found that two objects meeting that description are still, brutely, two
— distinctness needing no further ground. 2.02331 removes the bracket.
It asks about the sharper case: objects sharing not just form but "the
totality of their properties," everything true of one true of the
other. And it splits into two.

Either a thing has some property no other thing has — then a
description can name that property and, through it, single the thing
out. Nothing new here; this is how picking a particular object out by
words ordinarily works. Or several things share every property, down to
the last one, and then, the sentence says, "it is quite impossible to
point to any one of them."

Read that claim carefully: it is not that the things collapse into one.
2.0233 already settled that shared form (and here, shared everything)
does not erase numerical difference — they remain two. What collapses
is something else: the possibility of picking one out. The closing
sentence gives the reason, and it is close to a bare logical point.
Distinguishing a thing means marking it off by something. If nothing
marks it off — no leftover feature, no unshared property — then no act
of distinguishing, including pointing, can land on it instead of its
twin. And the argument has a further edge: if you did manage to point
successfully, that very success would itself be a mark, which
contradicts the case as stated. The impossibility is not a practical
limit on our powers of description. It is built into what "distinguish"
means once nothing distinguishing is left to reach for.

So 2.0233 and 2.02331 divide labor. The first says bare difference needs
no ground; the second says bare difference, however real, can go
unreachable by description. Two objects can be two without there being
any way, from inside the language of properties, to say which is which.

## Zen

Leibniz's Identity of Indiscernibles (*Discourse on Metaphysics* §9;
the Leibniz–Clarke correspondence) considers the very same setup — two
things sharing the totality of their properties — and treats it as a
case that cannot arise: no two substances differ *solo numero* alone,
so anything indiscernible in every respect just is one thing counted
twice, not two things at all. The Principle of Sufficient Reason backs
this up: God (or reason generally) could have no ground for making two
qualitatively identical but numerically separate substances rather than
one.

The disanalogy is exact and load-bearing. Leibniz uses total
property-sharing to *collapse* the pair: the supposition is incoherent,
so there are not really two. 2.02331 grants the supposition — total
property-sharing among several things — a real hearing, and 2.0233 has
already ruled that sharing everything still leaves them two, not one;
what total sharing costs is not their number but our reach, the
describability that would let anyone single one out. Leibniz treats the
case as impossible so that identity can do the individuating work.
Wittgenstein treats it as thinkable and lets reference simply fail.
Where Leibniz's principle is a metaphysical law ruling something out,
2.02331 is closer to a semantic observation about what description can
and cannot do once nothing is left for it to grip.

## Method

Leibniz was flagged explicitly in yesterday's method note as the right
candidate for exactly this thesis, held back from 2.0233 because that
sentence only bracketed external properties while 2.02331 stages the
full case — total property-sharing — the Identity of Indiscernibles
actually addresses. Spent it here as planned. That makes three Leibniz
appearances in sixteen days (16, 26, 31), which the project's diversity
discipline would normally question on its own; the fit here overrides
it, for a reason stronger than "this is a Leibniz-shaped thesis": no
other historical position states the *identical* setup (things sharing
every property) and then draws a conclusion, making the disanalogy
possible to state precisely rather than approximately. A weaker-fitting
substitute chosen only to avoid a third appearance would have cost more
than the repetition does.

2.02331 turns on reference and description, a question neither the
traditional nor the resolute reading has been staged, in this project,
