- Explanation centers on a vocabulary shift the German text makes and the
  English translation partly hides: every sentence since 2.01 has run on
  *Sachverhalt* ("atomic fact"), but 2.014 says "Die Gegenstände enthalten
  die Möglichkeit aller *Sachlagen*" — objects contain the possibility of
  all *states of affairs*, a different, though not brand-new, word (it
  already appeared at 2.0121 and 2.0122). Flagged a real translation
  inconsistency: Ogden renders the same German word *Sachlage* as "state
  of affairs" at 2.0121 and 2.014, but as "circumstances" at 2.0122 —
  invisible in English alone, exact in the German the page shows beside
  it. Read the distinction as tracking something real rather than
  stylistic variation: *Sachverhalt* is the elementary case (one
  combination of objects), *Sachlage* is what a picture will later
  represent (2.11, 2.202 onward) — a composite state built from many
  atomic facts together. On that reading 2.014 is not 2.0124 restated
  (2.0124 stayed at the atomic-fact level); it is a genuine climb one
  floor up, from an object's fixed possibilities at the joint level to
  its fixed possibilities at the level of whole states of affairs. Noted
  that 2.0141, next, drops straight back to *Sachverhalten* — 2.014 reads
  as a one-sentence excursion to the top floor before the book returns to
  finish the ground level.

pattern rather than a one-off. No destruction argument owed; nothing was
