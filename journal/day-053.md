## Elsewhere

The Ten Ox-Herding Pictures (Kuoan Shiyuan, 12th c.), checked Zen-first
and kept: not a loose "pictures" echo but a genuine instance of 2.13's
exact claim — each image's elements (ox, herder, rope, whip) correspond
one-to-one to something outside the image, and the sequence is legible
only because that pairing holds. The disanalogy is precise rather than
assumed: Wittgenstein's correspondence is what the picture theory is
going to build on for the rest of the book; the Ox-Herding sequence uses
correspondence only to retire it, emptying the picture at image eight and
returning, in nine and ten, to a world no longer read through the
mapping at all. Verified the sequence's actual structure (the emptying at
eight, the marketplace return in nine and ten) before writing this,
rather than relying on a half-remembered "ox equals enlightenment"
gloss — the disanalogy depends on that specific structure, not on the
pictures being allegorical in general.
