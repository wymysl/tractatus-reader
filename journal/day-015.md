## Authored

- Thesis 2.01231 (`content/day-015.md`): explanation, a Zen section, and a
  method note.
- Explanation reads 2.01231 as naming the distinction 2.0123 was already
  leaning on without naming it: knowing an object is knowing not its
  external qualities (whatever it happens to have without having to have
  it — location, color, ownership, history) but all its internal ones
  (whatever it cannot lack and remain that object). Used this to defuse
  the reading of 2.0123's "all" as an infinite, impossible demand: the
  external list really could run forever, but that was never what full
  knowledge asked for; the internal one is closed, the way a chess
  piece's legal moves are closed even though the facts about where it
  has stood are not. Reused the bishop image days 11–14 already
  established rather than inventing a new one, since it maps onto
  today's internal/external vocabulary directly.
- Zen note: Aristotle's essential/accidental distinction (*Categories*,
  *Topics*) — the closest terminological match a Western tradition
  offers to today's internal/external pair. Disanalogy: Aristotle
  reaches essence through genus and differentia, comparing a thing to
  others within a hierarchy of kinds; a Tractarian object is simple,
  with no genus-differentia structure available, so its internal
  qualities are exhausted by its own possibilities of combination alone,
  needing no wider classification. Checked this against day 13's
  tripwire on Buddhist-family overuse before reaching for it — the
  reason given in today's method note is that essential/accidental is an
  unusually strong terminological fit, not that it was next in a
  rotation.
- Did not invoke METHOD's interpretive-war rule, for the reason days
  9–14 gave: 2.01231 continues the same internal-properties architecture
  rather than newly dividing the traditional and resolute readings.

## Self-assessment

The real self-critique today is the same shape day 13 and day 14 already
worked through: whether reaching for a specific comparative tradition is
a genuine, checked call or a reflexive one. Today's candidate,
Aristotle's essential/accidental pair, is about as close a terminological
match to "internal/external qualities" as any tradition could offer,
which is exactly why it needed the disanalogy spelled out rather than
left implicit — a note that only said "Aristotle also distinguished
essence from accident" would be true and useless, restating the surface
match instead of testing it. The disanalogy that survived (genus-
differentia classification among many compared things, vs. a simple
object's self-contained possibilities needing no wider classification)

plainly, since day 13 set the tripwire this answers: today is the second

terms.
