  general śūnyatā argument (already used day 10), used Vasubandhu's
  *Viṃśatikā* argument against the coherence of a literal partless atom
  (*paramāṇu*): an atom surrounded by six neighbours must present six
  distinguishable faces or all seven atoms collapse to one point, but
  six faces means parts, so no partless unit survives its own spatial
  description. Read as Buddhist philosophy's own most direct formal

  specific than day 10's general svabhāva material, and landing exactly
  on 2.02's own claim. Named the disanalogy precisely: Vasubandhu's
  target is a spatial atom needing faces toward its neighbours; 2.0131
  already showed not every Tractarian object is spatial (colour, pitch,
  hardness), so the six-sided regress has no purchase on something never
  claimed to have sides. Flagged, without unpacking, that Wittgenstein's
  own forthcoming regress argument for needing simples (2.021–2.0212)
  has nearly the same shape as Vasubandhu's, run toward the opposite
  conclusion — one tradition stops the regress to save sense, the other
  finds it cannot be stopped and takes that as the discovery.
