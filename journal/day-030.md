# Day 30 — 2026-08-18

## Authored

- Thesis 2.0233 (`content/day-030.md`): explanation, Zen note, method note.
- Explanation reads 2.0233 as a shift in question from 2.0231–2.0232
  (what objects lack) to what makes two form-identical, property-bracketed
  objects two rather than one. Centered on "only" as marking a floor:
  once logical form is shared and external properties set aside, no
  further describable fact distinguishes them — distinctness is bare,
  ungrounded numerical difference, not inferred from any found
  difference. Flagged 2.02331 explicitly as the sharper case still to
  come (full property-sharing, not just shared form) rather than
  importing it early.
- Zen note compares Duns Scotus's *haecceitas*: individuals sharing a
  common nature completely are still genuinely two, requiring a formally
  distinct individuating principle ("thisness") beyond any shared or
  unshared quality — structurally the same move as 2.0233's bare
  difference. Disanalogy is load-bearing: Scotus's haecceity is positive
  furniture, an added formality meant to *ground* individuation; 2.0233
  adds nothing and names no principle — it stops one step earlier and
  refuses the further entity Scotus posits. Confirmed by grep that Scotus
  had not appeared in this project before today.
