## Authored

- Thesis 2.11 (`content/day-051.md`): explanation, Zen note, method
  note.
- Explanation centers on a translation problem rather than a purely
  philosophical one: *Sachlage* (situation) has already appeared three
  times (2.0121, 2.0122, 2.014), each time rendered differently by Ogden
  ("a state of affairs," "circumstances," "states of affairs"); at 2.11
  Ogden renders it a fourth way, "facts" — which collides with
  *Tatsache*, already spoken for since 1.1. Flagged this plainly, since
  a reader working only from the English has no way to see that the
  translation just erased a distinction (fact / atomic fact / situation)
  the German keeps apart in three separate words. Read the sentence
  itself as introducing a new unit: a *Sachlage* is a configuration of
  several atomic facts' obtaining and non-obtaining, partial and
  possibly merely possible — the first unit named between a single
  atomic fact and the world entire (2.04's totality of *existent*
  atomic facts). Tied "im logischen Raume" back to 1.13's "the facts in
  logical space are the world," read as putting a picture's situation in
  the same space the world's own facts occupy, not a separate
  picture-realm — stated, not yet earned; the how (model, correspondence
  of elements to objects) is reserved for 2.12 through 2.15, per
  one-day-one-thought.
- Declined the interpretive-war paragraph. The traditional/resolute fork
  day 50 opened at 2.1 is about the whole apparatus 2.1 begins; 2.11 is
  the apparatus's own first move, read the same way by both camps while
  building it. Reopening the fork here would repeat yesterday's flag,
  not find a new one — METHOD.md's "where they don't diverge, stay
  silent" rules it out.
- Zen note: the *Yijing*'s hexagrams, reconsidered. This exact candidate
  was raised and declined at day 16, for 2.0124, on the grounds that its
  disanalogy (a numerological convention, three lines to a trigram) was
  thin. Raised again today because 2.11 asks a different question:
  2.0124 was about the space of possibility being fixed in advance;
  2.11 is about presenting one situation within an already-fixed space
  — exactly what a cast hexagram does, six settled lines drawn from the
  fixed space of sixty-four. Found a different, thesis-specific
  disanalogy this time: a hexagram answers by an inherited correlative
  scheme, no resemblance required between the hexagram's own shape and
  what it is read to mean, while 2.12-2.15 (the very next sentences)
  require a Tractarian picture's elements to correspond to objects and
  relate as those objects relate — presenting through shared structure,
  not assigned convention. Recorded explicitly as this project's first
  return to a previously-declined Zen candidate, and why that's a
  distinct judgment rather than a quiet do-over: the thesis changed,
  so what there was to compare changed with it.
