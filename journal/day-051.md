## Authored

- Thesis 2.11 (`content/day-051.md`): explanation, Zen note, method

- Explanation cashes two flags this project has carried for a long
  time: day 5 (resolved by day 17) pointed "logical space" forward to
  2.11 by name; day 19 pointed *Sachlage*, naming it "what a picture
  will turn out to represent, several theses from now (2.11, 2.202),"
  at the same thesis. 2.11 says what a picture does with logical space
  — presents a *Sachlage* there — and, read against 2.06, identifies
  *Sachlage* exactly with *Wirklichkeit*, reality: existence and
  non-existence of atomic facts together, not the narrower positive-
  only *Welt* of 2.04. Drew out why that matters: a picture that could
  only show what holds could never be wrong; reaching for the negative
  half from its first sentence is what makes falsity (2.21, 2.22)
  thinkable at all.
- Flagged a third inconsistent Ogden rendering of *Sachlage* — "state
  of affairs" (2.0121), "circumstances" (2.0122), now "the facts"
  (2.11) — checked directly against `data/tractatus.json`. The third
  is the least careful: it collides with *Tatsache*, this book's
  existing, unrelated word for a plain fact, doing real work since 1.1.
- Zen note: the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika/Mīmāṃsā debate over how absence
  (*abhāva*) is known. Nyāya holds absence is directly perceived, the
  same faculty and act that perceives presence (*saṃyukta-viśeṣaṇa*,
  qualified contact) — the same symmetry 2.11 gives logical space, no
  separate apparatus for *Nichtbestehen*. Kumārila Bhaṭṭa's Mīmāṃsā
  disagrees: ordinary perception works by sense-object contact, and
  there is no object of non-existence to contact, so absence needs its
  own, sixth means of knowledge (*anupalabdhi*, non-perception). Kept
  the disanalogy: Wittgenstein's picture needs no sixth faculty because
  2.013 already built non-existence into logical space from the start
  (a thing's space of possibilities is as easily thought empty as
  occupied), not bolted on the way Kumārila's *anupalabdhi* has to be.
  Verified both doctrinal claims by search rather than from recall
  alone before committing either to the page — the same check this
  project already applies to claims about its own text file, applied
  here to a claim about a tradition. Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika reappears from day
  18 (there, *guṇa*, quality-inherence — a different question);
  Kumārila and Mīmāṃsā are first uses. Flagged, not hidden.
