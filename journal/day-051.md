- Explanation cashes the promissory note day 19 left open forty-two
  theses back: *Sachlage* (2.014, 2.0121, 2.0122) is "what a picture
  will turn out to represent" — 2.11 is that turn. Read *Sachlage*
  against *Sachverhalt*: an atomic fact is the elementary combination,
  a *Sachlage* is a configuration built from many atomic facts holding
  or failing to hold together, and a picture always presents the
  latter, never a bare atomic fact alone. Flagged the sharper point
  underneath the vocabulary: 2.11's defining clause, "das Bestehen und
  Nichtbestehen von Sachverhalten," is not a paraphrase of 2.06's
  definition of reality but the identical German clause reused
  verbatim — a picture presents exactly what 2.06 called reality, by
  the plainest textual means available, before the book has argued any
  resemblance between picture and pictured (that is 2.12-2.15's work).
  Also flagged a third English rendering of *Sachlage* itself: Ogden
  gives "the facts" here, after "state of affairs" (2.0121, 2.014) and
  "circumstances" (2.0122) — a third word for the same recurring
  German term, on the sentence where the term stops being incidental
  and becomes the picture theory's central object, and one that
  happens to collide with the English page's existing use of "facts"
  for the unrelated, prior term *Tatsache*.
