# Day 31 — 2026-08-19

## Authored

- Thesis 2.02331 (`content/day-031.md`): explanation, Zen note, method
  note.
- Explanation reads 2.02331 as removing the bracket 2.0233 put around
  external properties: now things share *every* property, not just
  form. The dichotomy splits on whether a thing has a property nothing
  else has (then description can single it out) or several things share
  everything (then, the text says, pointing to any one of them is
  impossible). Read that impossibility as a failure of reference, not a
  collapse into one thing: 2.0233 already settled that shared
  everything still leaves the objects two, so what's lost is only the
  ability to say, from inside the language of properties, which is
  which. Gave the closing sentence's near-circular argument
  ("distinguished by nothing" implies "cannot be distinguished, for
  otherwise it would be distinguished") its own reading: any successful
  act of pointing would itself constitute the very mark the case
  stipulates is absent.
- Zen note spends the Leibniz Identity of Indiscernibles connection
  flagged explicitly in yesterday's method note, comparing it against
  2.02331's identical setup — things sharing the totality of their
  properties. Disanalogy is load-bearing: Leibniz uses the shared-everything
  case to rule out real duality (indiscernibles are one thing, not two,
  by the Principle of Sufficient Reason); 2.02331, building on 2.0233,
  grants the case and lets the two things stay two while only reference
  fails. Leibniz's is a metaphysical law forbidding the case; 2.02331 is
  closer to a semantic finding about what description can grip.
