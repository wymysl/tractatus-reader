## Authored

- Thesis 2.034 (`content/day-043.md`): explanation, Zen note, method

- Explanation is built around a philological finding: thesis 2 (day 8)
  used *Bestehen* (existence, obtaining) for what an atomic fact does;
  2.034 uses *besteht aus* (consists of) for what a fact's structure is
  made of. Same German root, two grammatical jobs, thirty-five days
  apart — Ogden's "existence" and "consists of" are unrelated English
  words that sever the connection entirely. Used that to frame 2.034 as
  the sentence that finally puts *Tatsache* and *Sachverhalt* back
  together (last done at thesis 2 itself) and to explain why it uses
  partitive *besteht aus* rather than 2.0272's identity-shaped *bildet*:
  thesis 2's "one or more" atomic facts leaves room for a genuine
  plurality collapsing into one Struktur, which identity language
  can't carry the way composition language can. Flagged, honestly as
  open rather than resolved, whether 2.034 says anything about *how*
  several Sachverhalt-structures combine into one Tatsache-structure,
  or leaves that for a later sentence.
- Zen note is a first use of Frege's compositionality principle as a
  Zen-section comparison (Frege had appeared twice before in
  Explanation prose only, days 18 and 33, never here). Resonance: both
  claims refuse to let a whole's relevant property be a further fact
  discoverable independently of the parts. Left the disanalogy
  genuinely open rather than forcing a verdict: compositionality needs
  parts' meanings *and* their mode of combination as two separate
  ingredients; 2.034's wording states only the first. Whether the
  second is already absorbed into what *Struktur* means (per 2.032) or
  is missing outright is exactly the question the Explanation section
  also left open — noted as the same uncertainty showing up from two
  directions rather than manufacturing a clean contrast to close it.
