- Zen note: Hume's denial of any necessary connexion between distinct
  existences — a resonance close enough that Wittgenstein's own later
  6.36–6.37 is widely read as a direct answer to Hume, mediated through
  Russell. Disanalogy argued on the ground that actually separates the
  two: Hume's "cannot" is an epistemic finding about the limits of
  reason, agnostic on any hidden real connection; 2.062's "cannot" is a
  logical fact following from 2.061's independence, true a priori,
  making no claim about the mind's actual powers at all. Both leave
  room for natural science to find regularities anyway, for different
  reasons on each side.
