  and probably not exhaustive — 2.0131 also named pitch and hardness,
  neither returning here.
- Zen note uses Kant's Transcendental Aesthetic: space and time as pure
  forms of intuition (*Critique of Pure Reason* A22–30/B37–45) — the
  closest possible verbal match, "form" naming exactly space and time,
  in the philosopher most responsible for the technical sense of the
  word. Two disanalogies, both load-bearing: (1) location — Kant's forms
  are the subject's contribution, absent from things in themselves;
  2.0251's are the object's own fixed feature, no perceiving subject
  anywhere in this book's architecture; (2) taxonomy, targeted
  specifically at today's list — Kant restricts pure forms of intuition
  to space and time alone and places colour on the far side of the
  primary/secondary line as a merely subjective sensation (*Critique*
  B44; *Prolegomena* §13, Remark II — the same Lockean cut used at day
  29), where 2.0251 puts colour in the same list, same predicate, as
  space and time.
